*Earliest first. Each marked **realistic**, **stretch** or **long-shot** so you know where to spend effort. Checked 17 August 2026.*

## Start here — the two things that matter most

### 1. The best scholarship available to you is Tunisian, and you apply for it at home

The **MESRS *bourse universitaire à l'étranger*** (Tunisian Ministry of Higher Education overseas study grant) is the only scheme we found that covers **living costs, tuition, social cover, insurance, study-permit fees and your flight** in one package.

- **€800/month in the EU** — €600 as a grant, €200 as a loan
- **Published minimum: exactly 12/20.** You meet it. Most schemes have no published floor and quietly favour much stronger files; this one states its bar and you clear it
- **Filed through FSEG Sousse** — your own faculty
- The circular normally appears around **October 2026**

**Watch for that circular. Ask the scolarité now to tell you when it drops.** This is the highest-value single action in this deliverable.

There is also a **MESRS *prêt universitaire à l'étranger*** — the loan version, means-tested on parental income with ten-year repayment. That one is **realistic** rather than a stretch, and works as your backstop if the grant doesn't come through.

### 2. Italy's ISEE is not a scholarship and beats most of them

Italian public universities scale fees to family income. At Bologna, an ISEE at or below **€27,000 means a tuition contribution of zero** — total outlay around **€157/year**, verified from the university's own fee decree. No competition, no panel, no deadline race.

If money is the binding constraint, find out what an *ISEE parificato* would come to for your family before anything else. The input most likely to push you over the line is the **conventional valuation of your family home at €500/m²** — model that first.

## The schemes, in detail

### Tunisia — MESRS bourse · **STRETCH** · highest value

Covered above. €800/month in the EU, plus tuition, insurance, permit fees and flight. **12/20 floor, which you meet.** Applied for via FSEG Sousse, circular around October 2026.

### Tunisia — MESRS prêt · **REALISTIC**

The loan counterpart. Means-tested on parental income, repayable over ten years. Less attractive than a grant but genuinely attainable, and it converts an unaffordable option into an affordable one.

### Stipendium Hungaricum (Hungary) · **REALISTIC** · closes ~15 Jan 2027

**Tunisia holds a 250-place quota** — that is a large, country-specific allocation, and it is why this ranks realistic rather than long-shot. Economic Science is an eligible field. Covers tuition entirely, plus dormitory accommodation.

**One correction worth making:** the stipend is **HUF 43,700 ≈ €110/month**, plus about €100/month housing. Figures of HUF 200,000 circulating online are the **PhD** rate, not the master's rate. Budget accordingly — this covers your fees and roof, not a comfortable life.

### NAWA Banach (Poland) · **REALISTIC** · ~May 2027

**This corrects an earlier finding in this project.** A previous researcher concluded there was no Tunisia-specific scheme for Poland. There is: **Tunisia is explicitly listed (Group 3)** in the Banach programme.

Tuition exemption plus a stipend, **no field restriction**, and a preparatory-course route is available. Combined with Warsaw's Digital Business master's — where B2 English is met by the university's own free online test — this is one of the better-matched combinations in the report.

### Eiffel Excellence (France) · **LONG-SHOT** · institution deposits ~8 Jan 2027

€1,200/month for 24 months at M1 level, plus travel, insurance and housing support — **and tuition exemption *de plein droit***, worth another €3,950/year. "Économie Gestion" is an eligible domain. Age limit 29.

**The mechanism matters more than the money here: you do not apply. Your university does.** The campaign opens around 1 October 2026 and institutions must deposit files by about 8 January 2027.

So the action is: when you apply to a French university, **ask the international office in writing whether they will nominate you for Eiffel**, and ask early — before their internal selection closes. A candidate who never asks is never nominated.

It is a long-shot on merit given a 12–13/20 average. But the ask is free.

### MAECI (Italy) · **REALISTIC–STRETCH** · ~26 Mar 2027

€10,800 over nine months. Age limit 28. **A B2 certificate is mandatory.** Stacks on top of the ISEE and ER-GO findings, which is what makes Italy so strong overall.

Tunisia's beneficiary status for the specific cycle is `NOT VERIFIED — check directly`.

### ER-GO (Emilia-Romagna, Italy) · **REALISTIC** · 23 Jun – 24 Aug 2027

**€7,171/year** for students living away from home with an ISEE at or below €16,666, plus fee exemption, a €140 refund, residence and canteen access. **Non-EU students explicitly eligible.** If you go to Bologna, this stacks on the near-zero ISEE fee.

### AECID África-Med Máster (Spain) · **STRETCH** · ~Jul 2027

Tunisia is listed, and it covers any 60-ECTS official *máster*. **But sources conflict on whether it is restricted to public-sector employees** — flagged `NOT VERIFIED`. Check before investing effort.

### IsDB-France · **LONG-SHOT**

€700/month plus French-government-scholar status (which brings the automatic fee exemption). Tunisia is among the 42 eligible countries, historically about 12 places. Fields are development-themed, and continuation into 2027 is unverified.

### Erasmus Mundus

Fully covered in deliverable 4. **SDSI** (no GPA floor, IELTS 5.5, five places reserved for countries including Tunisia) and **CLIDE** (opens Feb 2027, ~half the cohort funded) are your two realistic targets. Note the €1,400/month *includes* travel and installation, and scholarships are capped at roughly 10% per nationality — so you compete against other Tunisians, not the world.

## Two warnings

### Austria — your top-ranked destination has no scholarship for you

This is important, because Austria heads your shortlist.

**Ernst Mach explicitly does not fund full degrees at Fachhochschulen.** The Erhard Busek scholarship, for which Tunisia *is* eligible at €1,300/month, is **suspended with no further call planned**.

**So cost Austria as fully self-funded.** The good news is that this is survivable precisely because FH Kufstein costs **€727 a year** — the cheapest option in the report. Austria doesn't need a scholarship in the way a €15,000 programme would. But do not build a budget assuming one will appear.

### The English test is the gatekeeper — not your GPA

Four separate researchers have now reached this conclusion independently.

The missing IELTS **blocks Stipendium Hungaricum, NAWA Banach and the Italian English-taught route** — three of your most realistic funding options. MAECI requires a B2 certificate outright.

Your GPA is not what stands between you and this money. A test you can sit in Sousse for about 700 TND is.
