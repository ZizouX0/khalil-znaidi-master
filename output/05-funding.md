## The single most important thing on this page

**Your cheapest funding is not a scholarship. It's Italy's ISEE system.**

Italian public universities scale fees to family income, and foreign families can obtain an *ISEE parificato*. At Bologna, an ISEE at or below **€27,000 means a tuition contribution of zero** — total outlay around **€157/year**, verified from the university's own fee decree.

No competition, no selection panel, no deadline race. It is means-testing, and on a Tunisian income you very likely qualify. If money is the binding constraint, this beats every scholarship below.

**Do this:** find out what an *ISEE parificato* would come to for your family before you do anything else. It is free to have calculated at affiliated CAF offices in Italy, and Bologna doesn't even require one — foreign-income documents go straight to the regional body. The one input most likely to push you over the line is the **conventional valuation of your family home at €500/m²**. Model that.

## Erasmus Mundus — the complete picture

All 220 funded programmes in the official EU catalogue were checked. Tunisia counts as a **Partner Country**, which is the most generous scholarship tier: tuition, travel, installation and about **€1,400/month**, up to **€33,600** total.

**One correction:** the €1,400/month *includes* the travel and installation package. There is no separate travel allowance on top.

**A second correction that changes your odds for the better:** scholarships are capped at roughly **10% per nationality** (GOALS says 3, CLIDE says 5). You are competing against other Tunisian applicants, not against the whole world.

### Worth applying to

**1. SDSI — Service Design Strategies and Innovations** · **REALISTIC — top pick**
Lapland / Latvia / Estonia. Accepts "Business/Administration/Law". **No GPA floor.** **IELTS 5.5 — the lowest requirement in the entire catalogue.** 20 scholarships, **5 reserved for NDICI/IPA III countries, which includes Tunisia**. Your CRM platform genuinely *is* service design, so your strongest asset maps straight onto their selection criteria. €8,000/yr unfunded; €0 plus €33,600 funded.

**2. CLIDE** · **REALISTIC**
Toruń / Granada / Linz / Kharkiv / **Casablanca**. New in 2025: roughly 12 scholarships plus 12 tuition-free places against an intake of 20–30 — about half the cohort funded. Five per nationality. B2 English. **Opens February 2027 — the latest window available**, which is what you need if your IELTS is still pending.

**3. GOALS** · **STRETCH**
Lille / Lisbon / Vilnius / Toruń / LUNEX. Entry wording explicitly names "Licence, 180 ECTS", no GPA floor, IELTS 6.0. The problem isn't eligibility — it's that the sector is sport, not music or marketing. Only apply if you can write an honest motivation letter for it.

**4. MAGMa** · **STRETCH**
Glasgow. Best Direction-4 subject fit, and explicitly accepts business/marketing/tourism backgrounds. But it wants a UK 2:1, which 12–13/20 likely misses. **Email Glasgow before spending an application slot.**

**5. MARIHE** · **2028, not 2027**
Any discipline, no GPA floor, tuition-free for everyone. Deadline **21 September 2026** — five weeks away — and it requires **IELTS 6.5 documented at application**. You cannot make that. Put it in the calendar for next year.

### Where Erasmus Mundus cannot help you

- **AI/data programmes (about ten of them): all blocked.** Every one requires a computer science, engineering or maths first degree. Hard blocks, not stretches.
- **Music: zero.** In all 220 programmes, the word "marketing" appears in **no** title, and there is no music-business EMJM at all.
- **EMMIE requires 240 ECTS.** Unrecoverable — strike it off.

**Useful precedent:** one programme (DIGICREA) accepts "150 ECTS + a sworn statement". That's evidence that a credit shortfall can be handled by declaration — exactly the argument your FSEG attestation would make.

## France — where a scholarship is worth double

This is the most actionable finding in this deliverable.

France's May 2026 decree ended blanket fee exemptions, but kept three grounds. Two you can pursue:

1. **French government scholarship holders are exempt from tuition *de plein droit*** — automatically, outside any quota. So a *bourse du gouvernement français*, available through the French Embassy and Institut français in Tunisia, is worth its stipend **plus €3,950/year in waived fees.** That makes it far more valuable than its headline figure suggests.
2. **Individual exemption on grounds of "situation personnelle... notamment au regard de leurs ressources"** — explicitly means-based, applied for at the institution after admission. This suits a self-funding student far better than merit schemes do.

**Realistic.** Both are worth pursuing. Note that having a French *baccalauréat* earns you nothing by itself — the decree says so explicitly.

## Italy — regional grants

**ER-GO (Emilia-Romagna), €7,171/year** · **REALISTIC**
For students living away from home with an ISEE at or below €16,666. Adds fee exemption, a €140 refund, residence and canteen access. **Non-EU students explicitly eligible.** Window **23 June – 24 August**. If you go to Bologna, this stacks on top of the near-zero ISEE fee.

Note: **Tunisia is not on Italy's "particularly poor countries" list** — checked against the actual decree text (DM 176 of 24 February 2026, 46 countries). The €200 flat-fee route does not apply to you.

## Hungary — Stipendium Hungaricum

**STRETCH.** Tunisia is a **confirmed full-degree sending partner**. Covers tuition entirely, plus roughly €110/month stipend and €100/month accommodation, plus insurance. English-taught. The 2027/28 call opens around **November 2026**.

Worth exactly one application. Competitive, and the programme-level matches for your directions have not been researched — funding is confirmed, suitable programmes are not.
