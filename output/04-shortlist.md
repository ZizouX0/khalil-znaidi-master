# 4. Your shortlist — the twelve worth your effort

Ordered by how strongly we'd back them. Each entry says what it costs, why it suits *you* specifically, and what could go wrong.

Before the list, one thing that shapes all of it:

> **Your binding constraint is not your GPA, and it is not the 146 credits. It is that you have no English test.** Three independent researchers reached this conclusion separately. Almost every good option on this list opens the moment you have an IELTS score. Book it now — you can sit it in Sousse.

## The top three

### 1. FH Kufstein Tirol — MA Digital Marketing (Austria)

**≈€1,453/year · English · Deadline 3 March**

The best single find in this project, and it came from an unexpected direction.

Austrian applied-sciences law (*Fachhochschul-Gesetz* §4(4)) defines master's entry as a relevant bachelor's **or an equivalent foreign qualification** — and contains **no 180-credit rule at all.** It explicitly lets a programme director admit someone whose equivalence is partial and settle the gap with exams *during* the master's. The only number published is *"at least 30 ECTS"* in business subjects, which your Licence in Marketing clears several times over.

Then: fees identical to what Austrians pay, teaching entirely in English, a **12-month job-search permit** afterwards, and a **Red-White-Red Card with no points system and no labour-market test** — which matters enormously when your GPA is modest, because nothing scores you.

**Best feature:** English B2 may be satisfiable with a **medium-of-instruction letter rather than IELTS**. Confirm this for your case — your degree was taught in French and Arabic, so it may not apply, but if it does, your single biggest obstacle disappears.

**Risk:** nobody has swept the rest of the Austrian FH sector. There may be better. Also confirm the fee: one source says €726.72 per *semester* (€1,453/year), another says per *year*. Assume the higher figure.

### 2. Università di Bologna — GIOCA (Italy)

**≈€157–1,000/year with ISEE · English · 120 ECTS**

If you want the music direction without wrecking your employability, this is how you do it.

It is a real *laurea magistrale* — a full state degree, not one of the one-year *Master di I livello* that most Italian "music business" offerings turn out to be. It sits in a **Management** department, so your marketing licence is on-topic, and it covers the recording industry as part of the creative industries.

Its entry wording is the most 146-proof sentence we found anywhere: *"a Bachelor's or equivalent degree **in any discipline** ... of minimum three-year duration."* Years, not credits. Any discipline.

**And 25 of its 50 places are reserved for non-EU applicants resident abroad.** No portfolio, no GMAT. Selection is CV (40 points) plus interview (60) — an interview is good for you, because your CRM project is more persuasive in conversation than on a transcript.

With an *ISEE parificato* under €27,000, Bologna's contribution is **zero** and your total outlay is about €157/year.

**Risks:** the ISEE paperwork is genuinely demanding — documents must be issued by Tunisian authorities, legalised at the Italian Embassy in Tunis, and translated. Model your family home's valuation at €500/m² before assuming you qualify. And you need an English certificate.

### 3. Université Lumière Lyon 2 — M1 Management de l'innovation → M2 Écosystème de la Musique (France)

**≈€4,055/year · French · No language test needed for the language itself**

The only music route we found that **admits at M1** from an economics/management licence with **no credit numeral in its entry requirement**, then progresses internally to a music-industry M2.

That matters more than it sounds. France's structural trap is that the attractive music programmes admit only at M2, which assumes you already have an M1 — i.e. 240 credits. This route sidesteps it entirely.

You'd study in your native language, in a country where you can work in that language from day one.

**Risks:** France's fees rose and exemptions tightened in May 2026 — budget €4,055, not the old near-free figure. And Campus France closes around **15 January 2027**, well before the university's own deadline. You are also **not** exempt from a French language test, which we expected you would be.

## The strong middle

### 4. Universidad de Málaga — Dirección y Gestión de Marketing Digital (Spain)

**≈€1,296 for the entire degree · Spanish (Tier B) · 90 ECTS**

The cheapest state-recognised master's found anywhere in Europe. Spanish is reachable for you in roughly 6–9 months, faster than any other new language.

**Two conditions.** File proof of your student authorisation before **1 November** or they may bill you up to the full real cost of the course. And the fee schedule we verified is 2023/24 — treat €1,300 as an order of magnitude.

**Realistically this is a 2028 entry**, not 2027, because of the Spanish requirement.

### 5. NOVA IMS — Data-Driven Marketing, Digital Marketing & Analytics (Portugal)

**€7,500 total · English · 120 ECTS · Public**

The best pure Direction-1 fit at a sane price. 2026/27 is full and **2027/28 opens soon — exactly your cycle**, so watch it.

### 6. Uniwersytet Warszawski — Digital Business (Poland)

**€7,500 total · English**

Digital marketing, e-commerce and analytics at Poland's leading university. Two features suit you unusually well: **B2 English can be satisfied by UW's own free online test**, and entry is decided by a **subject test rather than GPA ranking** — so your 12–13/20 doesn't hold you back.

Note Poland now statutorily requires universities to verify B2 in the language of instruction, so the free test route matters.

### 7. MTU Cork — MSc Artificial Intelligence (Ireland)

**€13,500 list → net €11,000 · English**

The **€2,500 award is automatic**, not competitive — applicants are considered once approved for the programme. Ireland gives **24 months** of post-study stay against the UK's 18 from January 2027.

We checked and disproved a claim that Ireland bars marketing roles from work permits. It doesn't — only estate agents and conference organisers are excluded from that group. There's even a carve-out favouring speakers of non-EEA languages in specialist digital-marketing roles, which describes you.

**Risk:** Ireland wants an NFQ Level 8 honours degree, commonly four years. Your file is genuinely borderline. **NARIC Ireland's comparability check is free — run it early**, before you spend an application.

### 8. ULiège / HEC Liège — International Strategic Marketing (Belgium)

**€5,369/year · French and English**

Analytical, strategic and digital marketing, in French, with a **confirmed exemption from French language testing** for applicants educated in French — one of the few places that exemption is verified. Belgium's search year is **12 months with unrestricted labour access**.

**Risk:** ULiège publishes a **minimum 12/20 average**. You clear it by nothing at all. Have a backup.

### 9. IAE Montpellier — M1 e-Marketing (France)

**≈€4,055/year · French · Public**

Selects on file **plus an oral interview** — and an interview is where your CRM platform actually lands. Also one of the few French pages that explicitly routes non-EU applicants through Études en France.

**Caveat:** only about six of France's ~30 IAEs were checked before the research was cut short. There are likely better IAE matches that nobody has looked at yet.

## The funded long-shots — apply anyway

Erasmus Mundus pays tuition plus about €1,400/month. Even at low odds, two of these are worth an application because the payoff is total.

### 10. SDSI — Service Design Strategies and Innovations

**Fully funded (€33,600) · Lapland/Latvia/Estonia**

**The single most realistic Erasmus Mundus target for you.** It accepts "Business/Administration/Law" backgrounds, has **no GPA floor**, asks only **IELTS 5.5** — the lowest in the entire catalogue — and reserves **5 scholarships specifically for NDICI/IPA III countries, which includes Tunisia.**

And your CRM platform *is* service design. This is the rare case where your strongest asset maps directly onto what they're selecting for.

### 11. CLIDE

**~Half of places funded · Toruń/Granada/Linz/Kharkiv/Casablanca**

Brand new, with roughly 12 scholarships plus 12 tuition-free places against an intake of 20–30. Crucially it has the **latest deadline — opening February 2027** — which is exactly what someone without an English test yet needs.

### 12. Stipendium Hungaricum (Hungary)

**Tuition-free + stipend + accommodation**

Tunisia is a confirmed full-degree partner. The 2027/28 call opens around November 2026. Competitive — apply, don't rely on it.

## Deliberately not on this list

**The UK.** Viable through roughly three institutions and no more. Fees are £16,000–30,000 nearly everywhere, plus £776/year health surcharge and £10,539 to hold for 28 days. Northern Ireland is not the cheap alternative it looks like — Queen's charges internationals **£23,000**. And from January 2027 the post-study route drops to 18 months. If you want the UK, York St John at £11,800 is the sane entry point.

**Germany.** Enforces six semesters **and** 180 credits, applied-sciences institutions included. But the likelier blocker is your GPA — German universities commonly want a 2.5 or "good", which 12–13/20 probably misses. Studienkolleg cannot help; it's for bachelor's entry.

**Norway and the Nordics.** Norway assesses by years of study, which would have suited you perfectly — but it now charges non-EEA fees with no fee-free institutions left, and requires ~€14,600 in proven funds. Finland's applied universities want two years' work experience you don't have.

**Any Italian *Master di I livello*, Spanish *título propio*, or private music academy.** Not state master's degrees. Several would likely fail the degree-level test for the very post-study permit you'd be relying on.

## The honest ranking of your four directions

We have to be direct here, because it affects everything above.

**Direction 1 (AI/data marketing) ≫ Direction 2 (digital marketing) > Direction 4 (music business) > Direction 3 (music marketing).**

Direction 3 barely exists as an affordable, accredited master's in Europe — that's a finding from six independent searches, not a gap in ours.

The music sector is also structurally hard to convert into a work visa, and the reason is worse than "small companies won't sponsor". **31.6% of EU cultural employment is self-employment**, against 13.7% across the economy — a third of the sector is legally invisible to employee work permits. There are 2.1 million cultural enterprises for 8.9 million cultural workers: about four people per firm.

And the pay doesn't clear the bar. In the UK, the **median** musician earns £37,500 and the median arts producer £38,100, against a £41,700 work-permit floor. Those are mid-career figures. A junior is nowhere near.

**This does not mean giving up on music.** It means treating music as a *sector you work in*, not a *degree you buy*. The door that actually opens is a data or CRM role at Spotify, Deezer, Believe or Universal — and you reach that through Direction 1, not Direction 3.

That's why GIOCA sits at number 2 on this list: it's the one way to study the culture industry without giving up a management-track qualification.
