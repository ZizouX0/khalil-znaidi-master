# 3H — Private business schools & specialist institutions: net cost after funding
**Checked 2026-08-17. Target intake: autumn 2027.**

---

## 0. BOTTOM LINE

The single most consequential finding is a **correction to Agent 2A**: a plain
*titre RNCP niveau 7* **does** qualify for the French post-study job-search permit.
The blanket "RNCP-only = post-study-work risk" caveat is wrong as a matter of law
(§1). That reopens a whole category of French private schools — though most of
them are still poor value on price.

On price, only a handful of private/specialist schools genuinely land under
€15,000 net, and only three land under €8,000. The best of them (**ICHEC
Brussels, €5,369/yr, no scholarship needed**) is cheap because it is a
state-funded Belgian Haute École, not because a private school discounted
anything. **The pattern across this whole sweep: where the net price is good, it
is because the institution is publicly funded; where the "scholarship" is large,
the list price was inflated to start with.** Four institutions in scope should be
excluded outright on accreditation (Rome Business School, EU Business School,
BSBI Berlin, IMB Barcelona) — all four sell what is legally a *título propio* or
a partner's degree, none awards a state degree of the country it operates in.

## 1. THE RNCP-7 / APS QUESTION — SETTLED

### 1.1 The four French qualification types, distinguished

| Type | Who awards | State-recognised? | ECTS | Grants APS/RECE? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Diplôme national de master** | Public university (or private school with *grade de master*) | Yes — national diploma | Yes, 120 | **Yes** — explicitly, arrêté 12 May 2011 art. 1, 1° |
| **Grade de master** conferred on a private/consular school diploma | Private school, by ministerial visa | Yes — same *grade* as above | Yes, 120 | **Yes** — art. 1, 1° covers *"diplômes délivrés par les établissements d'enseignement supérieur privés et consulaires... conférant le grade de master"* |
| **Mastère Spécialisé** (CGE label) | CGE-member school | Not a national diploma; a CGE label registered as RNCP-7 | Varies (75+ ECTS) | **Yes** — named on service-public.gouv.fr |
| **Titre RNCP niveau 7** (plain, non-CGE) | Any certifier registered with France Compétences | **Yes — state-registered certification, but not a diploma and not a *grade*** | **Not automatically.** ECTS are a school claim, not a legal attribute of the title | **Yes — see 1.2** |

### 1.2 The legal chain that settles it

1. **CESEDA art. L.422-10** grants the *carte de séjour "recherche d'emploi ou
   création d'entreprise"* (RECE, the successor to the APS) to a graduate holding
   *"un diplôme au moins équivalent au grade de master **ou figurant sur une liste
   fixée par décret**"*, obtained in an establishment *"habilité au plan national"*.
   https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000049052670
2. That list is the **Arrêté du 12 mai 2011**. Its **Article 1, 2°** reads:
   **« Les titres et diplômes inscrits au niveau I au répertoire national des
   certifications professionnelles. »**
   Consolidated version confirmed **still in force as at 17 August 2026**.
   https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000023995338/
3. **Décret n° 2019-14 of 8 January 2019** (cadre national des certifications)
   replaced the 1969 Roman-numeral scale. Certifications classified at **niveau I**
   were reclassified, by 1 January 2020 at the latest, to **niveau 7 or niveau 8**.
   https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000037964754

**Therefore: "niveau I au RNCP" in the arrêté now reads as "niveau 7 (or 8) au
RNCP". A titre RNCP niveau 7 is on the statutory list and grants the RECE/APS.**

Corroboration, citizen-facing: service-public.gouv.fr fiche **F17319** lists as
qualifying *"une licence professionnelle, un Mastère Spécialisé, un Master of
Science (labellisé par la conférence des grandes écoles) **ou un autre diplôme au
moins équivalent au master**"*. Note it even admits a **licence professionnelle**
(a bac+3) — the threshold is clearly not "grade de master".
https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F17319 (checked 2026-08-17)

**Terms of the permit (verified 2026-08-17):** 1 year, **not renewable**;
available within 4 years of obtaining the diploma; requires health cover; to
convert to a salaried permit the job must pay **more than €2,800.53 gross/month**
(figure on F17319). **Tunisia has a bilateral migration agreement giving
*conditions de délivrance plus favorables*** — a real advantage (established by 2A).

### 1.3 Where the residual risk actually sits — and it is not the RNCP level

The remaining exposure is the **other** limb of L.422-10: the establishment must be
**« habilité au plan national »**. Registering a *title* with France Compétences is
a national act, and prefectures in practice accept RNCP-7 titles; but the two
conditions are not identical, and prefecture practice is not uniform. So:

- **Verdict: change ICART, IESA and ESG Nantes from "real post-study-work risk"
  to "low residual risk".** They should no longer be held off a shortlist on APS
  grounds. The one thing worth doing is asking each school, in writing, for the
  RNCP fiche number and confirmation that alumni have obtained the RECE.
- **Passeport Talent does NOT rescue a weaker qualification** — it is stricter,
  not looser. The *Talent – salarié qualifié* route (CESEDA L.421-9) requires a
  **master-equivalent** qualification **and** a salary of **2× the annual SMIC
  (~€44,000 gross/yr in 2026)**; a 2025 reform introduced a lower **€35,000**
  floor for young graduates and innovation roles. `VERIFY the €35,000 figure
  before relying on it — it comes from practitioner commentary, not from
  Légifrance.` For a first marketing job in France this threshold is the binding
  constraint, not the diploma. **The RECE is the realistic route; Passeport Talent
  is the step after it.**

### 1.4 ISEG — the check the previous run flagged, completed

ISEG (Marketing & Communication School, IONIS group; Paris, Lille, Nice,
Strasbourg, Bordeaux, Toulouse, Nantes, Lyon).

- **RNCP titles are live and genuine**: *Manager de la stratégie marketing
  digital* **RNCP 37782** (decision 19/07/2023, so valid to ~2028) and *Manager de
  la communication* **RNCP 41730** (renewed 27/11/2025). So the recognition scare
  is **not** about expired registrations.
- **What ISEG does NOT have:** no *grade de master*, no ministerial *visa*, **not a
  CGE member**, no AACSB/EQUIS/AMBA. It is a commercial (for-profit) school. Its
  own explainer page concedes the point: an RNCP registration "does not
  automatically mean a *diplôme visé* or a *grade*".
- **The real red flag is structural, not legal:** ISEG's bac+5 "MBA Spécialisés"
  (including *MBA Digital Marketing, Communication et Médias Sociaux*) are run
  **in alternance** — the tuition is paid by an employer via its OPCO. **A non-EU
  student outside the EU cannot realistically secure an apprenticeship contract
  before arrival**, and the "no tuition" framing is meaningless to him. Fees
  quoted for the initial-training track run to ~**€10,150/yr in year 5**.
- **Verdict: EXCLUDE.** Not on APS grounds (per §1.2 it would qualify) but on
  **value**: ~€10,000/yr for a plain RNCP-7 title with no international
  accreditation, when Rennes SB gives a triple-accredited CGE MSc for ~€11,950
  net and ICHEC gives a state master for €5,369.

### 5.4 The general warning, stated once

Across every school in this sweep, the "scholarship" is a **discount off the
school's own list price**, decided by the same admissions committee that sets that
price, awarded automatically at admission with no separate application. That is a
pricing mechanism, not funding. Two consequences:

1. **The bottom rung is near-automatic and the top rung is near-unreachable.**
   Early-bird 10% and "partner" 10% cost the school nothing and are given to
   almost everyone; the advertised "up to 50%" goes to a handful. Budget on
   **10–20%**, never on 50%.
2. **None of these schools publishes employment statistics separating non-EU
   graduates who need sponsorship.** BSB's "74% within 4 months" and BBS's ">90%
   at 6 months" are all-cohort figures dominated by EU nationals with automatic
   work rights. **They tell him nothing about his own prospects.**

  music, and has no data component.
- **No Africa- or North-Africa-specific scholarship** at any of: IÉSEG, EM
  Normandie, BSB, Bologna BS, ICHEC, ESSCA. EM Normandie has an **India**-specific
  50% award and nothing equivalent for Africa. The Africa-relevant levers found in
  this scope are indirect: **(a)** ICHEC's exemption from the €4,175 non-EU
  supplement for **Wallonie-Bruxelles International** scholarship holders, and
  **(b)** the French rule (per the orchestrator's supplement) that **BGF holders
  are exonerated de plein droit from the €3,950 differentiated fee** — which
  applies to public institutions such as EM Strasbourg, not to private schools.
- **No published minimum GPA** at any school in §3 — all use holistic file review.
  That is favourable to him, but it also means the *scholarship* ladder is where
  his 12–13/20 will cost him, not the admission decision.
