# 1A — Credential Recognition: Is the 146 ECTS a real shortfall?

**Agent 1A · Research date: 2026-08-17 · All pages checked 2026-08-17**

---

## BOTTOM LINE (read this first)

**The 146 figure is almost certainly a transcript artefact, not a shortfall. Confidence: HIGH (~85–90%).**

Three independent facts drive this:

1. **Tunisian law fixes the licence at 180 credits over 6 semesters.** Both the governing decree of the student's era (Décret n° 2008-3123 of 22 Sept 2008, Art. 5) and its successor (Décret Présidentiel n° 2022-631 of 14 July 2022) state: *"Les études en vue de l'obtention du diplôme national de licence durent trois ans après le baccalauréat et comprennent **180 crédits répartis sur six semestres**."* The volume is a legal property of the award, not something a transcript can shrink. **VERIFIED.**

2. **The Tunisian credit rules produce transcripts that under-count by design.** Under Art. 34 of the 2008 decree, a *unité d'enseignement* is **capitalised** ("capitalisée") only by scoring ≥10/20. Art. 35 lets a student progress via **annual compensation** (weighted average ≥10/20 across all UEs). So a student who graduates legitimately by compensation has UEs that were *validated* but never *capitalised* — and a "relevé de notes" that prints the **crédits capitalisés** column will show **less than 180**. **VERIFIED (decree text).** The inference that this explains 146 specifically is **INFERENCE**, but it fits arithmetically: 180 − 146 = **34 credits**, and UEs are worth **4–7 credits each** (Art. 22), i.e. roughly **5–7 UEs failed individually and carried by compensation** — exactly the profile of a 12–13/20 student.

3. **European ENIC-NARIC bodies evaluate the award, not the transcript arithmetic.** Sweden's UHR states flatly that both the *Diplôme de Licence Fondamentale* and the *Diplôme de Licence Appliquée* are **3 years, 180 national credits, comparable to a Swedish Kandidatexamen (180 hp / 180 ECTS)**. **VERIFIED.**

**Answer to Q4 (the decisive question):** Yes — a completed Tunisian licence is recognised as a 180-ECTS-equivalent first-cycle degree by mainstream European evaluators, **regardless of the number printed on the relevé de notes**. The 146-credit constraint should be **removed from downstream planning** as a general blocker.

### The one genuine exception you must carry forward

**The Netherlands is the outlier, and its problem is a LEVEL problem, not a credit problem.** Nuffic rates the **Licence Fondamentale as "2 years of WO"** (i.e. *below* a Dutch bachelor) and the **Licence Appliquée as "3 years of HBO."** **VERIFIED on Nuffic's Tunisia page.** Fixing the 146 number changes nothing here. If the student holds a *licence fondamentale*, Dutch research-university (WO) master's programmes are structurally hard; Dutch **HBO** master's / universities of applied sciences are the realistic Dutch route. This is the single most important downstream implication.

**Note on the "local credits vs ECTS" hypothesis:** Tunisia's credit is defined identically to ECTS in structure (workload-based, 30/semester, 180/licence, capitalisable and transferable). Sources describe it as ECTS-aligned. However, no source I found states an official 1:1 legal conversion decree. **The practical effect is 1:1** — UHR literally maps "180 (national credit)" → "180 ECTS." Treat 1:1 as safe; treat "there is a decree saying 1 Tunisian credit = 1 ECTS" as **NOT VERIFIED**.

A public-university licence needs no such certificate. **VERIFIED (uni-assist country page).** The site itself returned HTTP 503 on 2026-08-17 — programme-level credit tables for the Marketing licence are **NOT VERIFIED**; the student should pull his own plan d'études.

## 3. Why the transcript reads 146 — hypotheses ranked

### ① MOST LIKELY — "crédits capitalisés" ≠ programme credits (compensation artefact)
**Probability: ~50%.** Art. 34 capitalises a UE only at ≥10/20; Art. 35 permits progression by compensation. A relevé printing capitalised credits therefore omits every compensated-through UE. 180 − 146 = 34 credits ÷ (4–7 credits/UE) = **5 to 8 UEs**. For a student averaging 12–13/20, having 5–8 UEs under 10/20 across three years is entirely ordinary. This hypothesis explains the *specific* number better than any other.

### ② LIKELY — incomplete / interim transcript
**Probability: ~30%.** Options: (a) the relevé covers only 5 semesters (150 credits) plus part of S6; (b) the PFE / stage / mémoire is credited on a separate document and omitted; (c) the document is a mid-year "relevé de notes" rather than a final "attestation de réussite." Note 180 − 146 = 34, which is *not* a clean 30 — so a bare "missing S6" alone doesn't fit unless combined with ①.

### ③ POSSIBLE but not the primary story — local-credit / per-semester reporting quirk
**Probability: ~15%.** Some Tunisian faculties weight UEs so that a semester's printed total departs from exactly 30, or report only credits carrying a coefficient. Real, but it usually causes small deviations, not a 34-credit gap.

### ④ UNLIKELY — genuine shortfall
**Probability: ~5%.** A genuine shortfall would mean the licence was **never awarded**. If the student holds the *diplôme national de licence* (or an *attestation de réussite*), 180 credits are a legal property of that award and a shortfall is definitionally impossible. **The only scenario where 146 is real is if he has not actually graduated.** That must be confirmed, not assumed.

### What the student must check on his own paperwork — a concrete checklist

1. **Which document is it?** A *relevé de notes* (semester marks, provisional) vs an *attestation de réussite* (pass certificate) vs the *diplôme national de licence* itself (final parchment, issued later). Only the last two prove the award.
2. **Does it span 6 semesters (S1–S6)?** Count them. If it stops at S5, it is incomplete by construction.
3. **Is the PFE / stage / projet de fin d'études listed and credited?** For a *licence appliquée*, internships alone must total ≥30 credits (Art. 8).
4. **What is the column header?** "Crédits" vs "**Crédits capitalisés**" vs "Crédits acquis" vs "Crédits obtenus." If it says *capitalisés/acquis*, hypothesis ① is confirmed on the face of the document.
5. **Are there UEs with a mark below 10/20 but the semester/year still marked "validé" or "admis par compensation"?** If yes — hypothesis ① confirmed.
6. **Sum the theoretical credits, not the awarded ones.** Add the credit weight *assigned to each UE in the plan d'études*, ignoring pass/fail. If that sums to 180, the programme is 180 and the 146 is bookkeeping.
7. **Which licence is it — fondamentale or appliquée?** This is the single most consequential fact for the Netherlands (see verdict) and matters for framing everywhere. It is printed on the diploma.

## 4. The decisive conclusion (restated)

**A completed Tunisian licence from a public university is a 180-credit, three-year, EQF-level-6 first-cycle degree, and mainstream European ENIC-NARIC bodies treat it as bachelor-equivalent regardless of what the relevé de notes totals.** Confidence **HIGH**. The 146 figure should not drive country selection, programme selection, or any "top-up year" strategy **once graduation is confirmed**.

Two residual risks survive, and only two:
- **Netherlands / Nuffic level downgrade** for the *licence fondamentale* (2 years WO). Real, verified, unaffected by the credit question.
- **Individual admissions officers** who read "146" off a raw transcript and reject before an evaluator ever sees it. This is a *presentation* risk, and it is cheap to defuse — see §5.

## 5. Concrete next steps — what would settle this definitively

### Tier 0 — free, do first (days)
- **Obtain the *attestation de réussite* and the *supplément au diplôme* from FSEG Sousse scolarité.** Tunisian institutions issue a **supplément au diplôme** to students who have completed a parcours and obtained its credits; it follows the UNESCO/CEPES–European Commission–Council of Europe Diploma Supplement model and describes level, context, content and status of the studies. **VERIFIED that Tunisia issues these**; whether FSEG Sousse issues it on request and in which languages is **NOT VERIFIED — ask the faculty directly.** This single document, if it states the programme total, closes the question at zero cost.
- **Request a written *attestation* from the faculty** stating the programme comprises 180 credits over 6 semesters and that the student was awarded the diploma. One paragraph on letterhead.

### Recommended sequencing
1. Get the **supplément au diplôme + attestation de réussite** from FSEG Sousse (free, fastest, usually sufficient).
2. **Check anabin** for Université de Sousse and "Licence" under Tunesien. If H+ and the degree is listed as equivalent, Germany costs €0 to prove.
3. **Only if a specific target programme demands it**, buy one formal evaluation — the **ZAB Zeugnisbewertung (~€208)** is the most portable across German-speaking Europe; the **ENIC-NARIC France attestation (~€90)** is the cheapest and best for France/Belgium/Switzerland francophone.
4. Do **not** buy multiple evaluations speculatively. With an autumn-2027 intake, there is ample time (deadlines cluster Oct 2026 – Apr 2027 for most European master's), so Tier 0 first and Tier 1 only on demand.

### Presentation tip (defuses the residual risk)
When a transcript shows 146, submit it **together with** the supplément au diplôme / attestation and a one-line cover note: *"The Tunisian licence nationale comprises 180 credits over six semesters per Décret n° 2008-3123, Art. 5; the transcript column reports capitalised credits only."* That pre-empts the admissions-officer misreading entirely.
