## 1. Bottom line

Andalucía's cheapness is **confirmed and now legally sourced**, not inferred: Decreto
98/2023 art. 3.2 (as amended by Decreto 142/2025) fixes **€13.68/credit for a máster no
habilitante** and lets universities *optionally* surcharge non-residents up to 100% of
cost — and the UMA fee annex, in the version still in force, expressly treats a student
holding **"la autorización de estancia por estudios"** as paying the standard rate. That
single phrase is what makes Andalucía different: **Cataluña's 2026/27 decree says the
opposite in terms** ("la autorización de estancia de larga duración por estudios … **no
equivale a la condición de residentes**"), Valencia doubles, Aragón doubles, Madrid bills
non-residents at the third-enrolment rate, Murcia adds 20%, Galicia's UVigo adds 25%.
Four genuinely qualifying, state-recognised, marketing-direction programmes were found in
Andalucía for **€820–€1,300 for the whole degree**, all Spanish-taught (Tier B). The
single best new find is **Universidad de Cádiz, Máster Universitario en Dirección de
Marketing Digital y Social — 60 ECTS, ≈€886 all-in, official, Direction-2 core**, whose
admission criteria are unusually favourable to this student (35% weight on professional
experience). A **potentially cheaper-than-Andalucía route exists in Galicia** (€9.85/credit
for social-science masters, +25% non-EU = €12.31) but no Galician *official* marketing
master was found — only an international-commerce one and a título propio. **Direction 3
(music marketing) remains a verified zero across all seventeen communities**, confirming 2C.

## 5. Article 18 — the access route without homologación

**Legal basis:** RD 822/2021 art. 18. Confirmed word-for-word in Andalucía's own portal:

> *"Estar en posesión de un título procedente de sistemas educativos que no formen parte
> del Espacio Europeo de Educación Superior, que equivalgan al título de Grado, **sin
> necesidad de homologación del título**"* — subject to *"comprobación por parte de la
> universidad del nivel de formación que implican"*, and provided the degree gives access
> to postgraduate study in the issuing country.
> — [Distrito Único Andaluz / SGUIT](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/economiaconocimientoempresasyuniversidad/sguit/?q=masteres&d=mo_requisitos_procedimiento.php)

### How it works in Andalucía — and why it is the cheapest version of this route in Europe
- It is **not a separate application**. It is folded into the ordinary **preinscripción**
  through the Distrito Único Andaluz, which runs **three phases, with Phase 1 reserved for
  foreign-system applicants**. One application, ranked preferences, up to all nine
  universities at once.
- **Fee: none found.** Decreto 98/2023's fee annex contains **no line item** for an
  *estudio de equivalencia* or *comprobación de nivel*, and neither the DUA portal nor the
  Universidad de Sevilla admission page quotes a charge. → **effectively €0**, marked
  `LIKELY — not positively confirmed`; the absence of a tariff in a decree that tariffs
  everything else is strong but not conclusive evidence.
- **Documents (US, Sevilla — the clearest published list):** passport; the degree
  certificate (or proof it is being issued); official transcript with **grades, credit
  hours/duration and weighted average**; a certificate from the issuing country stating
  the minimum pass mark and maximum obtainable mark; **official documentation that the
  degree gives access to postgraduate study at home**; everything **legalised (apostille)
  and translated into Spanish**.
  → [cat.us.es](https://cat.us.es/admision-general-master-para-estudiantes-extranjeros)
- **The "certificado de nota media" step is the one people miss.** Spain wants the average
  recalculated onto the Spanish 0–10 scale; the Ministry publishes an equivalence tool.
  Start it early — it is bureaucratic, not slow.

## 6. The 1 November trap — and whether it exists elsewhere

### Andalucía: real, avoidable, dated
UMA's fee annex (wording unchanged from the 2021/22 version through the 2025/26 one):

> *"Las tarifas … se aplicarán con carácter **provisional** a los estudiantes de
> nacionalidad extranjera, mayores de dieciocho años, que no tengan la condición de
> residente –temporal o de larga duración– en España **o la autorización de estancia por
> estudios** … Los estudiantes afectados deberán presentar en la Secretaría de su
> respectivo Centro, **con anterioridad al día 1 de noviembre**, el documento acreditativo
> de haber obtenido (**o resguardo de haberlo solicitado**) la mencionada condición de
> residente, o autorización de estancia por estudios. **Caso contrario se procederá a
> efectuar una liquidación complementaria que podrá alcanzar el 100% del coste real** de
> la prestación de los citados servicios."*
> — [UMA ANEXO C, másteres](https://www.uma.es/media/files/ANEXO_C_preciosMaster_kzP1x56.pdf)

Two things worth spelling out. First, **a receipt for having applied ("resguardo de
haberlo solicitado") is enough** — he does not need the permit card in hand by 1 November,
only proof he filed. Second, the underlying enabling clause is **Decreto 98/2023 art. 3.2**,
which is region-wide, so the same logic governs all nine universities; but each university
writes its own annex, so **the exact wording must be re-read on the university he picks.**

**Action item: file the study-authorisation proof at the Centre's Secretaría before 1 November of the enrolment year.** Diary it.

### Equivalents elsewhere — checked
| Community | Equivalent condition? |
|---|---|
| **Cataluña** | **Worse — no deadline to meet, because there is no exemption.** Decreto 96/2026 art. 1.5 forecloses it: the study authorisation *"no equivale a la condición de residentes"*. The surcharge simply applies |
| **Comunitat Valenciana** | No deadline mechanism; the doubling is automatic for non-residents (Decreto 101/2024) |
| **Madrid** | No deadline mechanism; third-enrolment pricing applies automatically |
| **Galicia** | No 1-November clause found, but the test is *long-term or permanent residence*, so a student permit does not qualify. UVigo's +25% applies flatly |
| **Murcia** | No deadline; +20% flat. Residents must produce a *permiso de residencia* to get the standard rate — a study permit is not one |
| **Aragón** | No deadline; parallel non-EU price column |
| **Castilla y León** | No surcharge published, therefore no deadline |

**Conclusion: the 1 November clause is not a hidden penalty — it is Andalucía's exemption
mechanism.** No other community was found to offer the same escape hatch. That, and not
the €13.68 alone, is why Andalucía wins.

## 9. Language, grants, and the practical picture

**Tier B — Spanish.** Every qualifying programme is 100% castellano. For a **native French
speaker, Spanish is the fast category**: realistically **6–9 months to a certified B2**,
against 18–24 for German. Accepted certificates: **DELE** (Instituto Cervantes) and
**SIELE**. Practical notes:
- **Instituto Cervantes has a centre in Tunis**, so DELE can be sat before departure —
  no need to enter Spain first.
- **Only UGR/UJA publishes an explicit certificate requirement** (Spanish B2 + English B1).
  **UCA, US and UMA publish none** on their access pages — `NOT VERIFIED` whether one is
  applied at enrolment. If confirmed absent, **UCA becomes reachable without any
  certificate at all**, which would be a significant simplification. Email admissions.
- Every Andalucían university runs a **Centro de Lenguas Modernas** with subsidised
  Spanish courses for its own students; UGR's is among the largest in Spain. These are
  *not* free and are **not** a visa-bearing preparatory year.
- **No integrated preparatory language year exists** at any of the four. There is no
  Spanish equivalent of the German Studienkolleg for master's entry.

**Regional grants and waivers for non-EU students — the honest answer: essentially none.**
- The **Beca General del Ministerio** requires Spanish nationality or long-term residence.
  A student on an *estancia por estudios* does **not** qualify.
- Andalucía's **99% bonificación** (§7, 2O-2) is open to him **from his second year only**,
  and only because he will by then have passed credits at an Andalusian public university.
  It is not nationality-restricted — a genuine, if delayed, benefit.
- **Asturias** is funding master's tuition for 2026-27 on an income test — `NOT VERIFIED`
  whether non-residents qualify.
- **Extremadura** offers a 99% bonificación but conditions it on prior-year credits taken
  in Extremadura — same structural exclusion in year 1.
- **AUIP** (Asociación Universitaria Iberoamericana de Postgrado) runs mobility
  scholarships at UCA and other Andalucían universities, but the programme is
  **Ibero-America-focused**; Tunisia is out of scope. → verify at becas.com/auip
- **Reciprocity lists matter and he is on none of them.** Asturias exempts Latin Americans;
  **Murcia exempts Moroccan nationals by bilateral accord — Tunisians are not covered.**
  This is worth knowing precisely because it is so nearly good news.

**Cost of living** (Andalucía is the cheapest university region in Spain): Jerez/Cádiz and
Huelva ≈**€600–750/month**; Málaga and Sevilla ≈**€750–900**; Granada ≈**€650–800**.
Compare Barcelona/Madrid at €1,100–1,400. On a €886 tuition bill, **living costs are 95%+
of the real cost of the degree**, which is the number that should drive city choice.

**Post-study rights:** Spain's *autorización de residencia para búsqueda de empleo* gives
**12 months** after graduation, and requires having *"finalizado los estudios alcanzando
como mínimo el Nivel 6 del MECES"*. **A máster universitario oficial is Nivel 7 and
qualifies. A título propio carries no MECES level and almost certainly does not.** This is
the whole reason §4 matters.
