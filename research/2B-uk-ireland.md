## 1. Bottom line

**Academically the UK is the single most open country we have looked at — and financially it is the
single most hostile.** The UK applies **no ECTS rule whatsoever**: universities convert a foreign
degree straight to a UK classification, and multiple institutions publish, in writing, that a
Tunisian **Licence at 12/20 = a UK 2:2**, which is the standard minimum for a taught master's.
His 146-ECTS transcript is therefore a **NON-ISSUE in the UK** — nobody counts the credits. That
removes the single biggest obstacle in his file. But the cost filter destroys most of the country:
typical international PGT fees are £18,000–30,000, and once you add the **£558 visa fee, £776/yr
Immigration Health Surcharge, and a £10,539 maintenance balance you must physically hold**, even a
"cheap" UK master's lands near €25,000 all-in for year one. Only a handful of post-92 universities
sit under the filter. **Ireland is the better-value half of this scope**: Munster TU charges
€13,500 with an **automatic €2,500 scholarship → net €11,000**, and Ireland gives **24 months**
post-study work against the UK's **18 months** (the Graduate Route was cut for anyone applying from
1 Jan 2027 — which is exactly his cohort). Ireland's catch is the reverse of the UK's: it *does*
care about level, asking for **NFQ Level 8 honours**, so his 3-year licence is `BORDERLINE` there
where it is `LIKELY OK` in Britain. Verdict: **the UK is viable, but only via ~3 named institutions
and only if he accepts a post-92 university** — it is not viable as a general strategy.

## 3. THE UK CREDIT QUESTION — ANSWERED DEFINITIVELY

**The UK does not use ECTS and applies no 180-credit rule to Tunisian applicants.** Admission is by
**degree classification equivalence**, decided per-country by each university's admissions team.

Verified admissions wording:

| Source | Exact wording | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| **University of Bradford**, Tunisia country page | Qualification: **Licence**. **"UK 2:1 equivalent: 14/20"**, **"UK 2:2 equivalent: 12/20"**. General rule: entry "typically equivalent to a UK Second Class Honours Second Division (2:2)." | 2026-08-17 |
| **Liverpool John Moores**, Tunisia page | **"12/20 (Assez Bien)"** = UK **2:2**; **"14/20 (Bien)"** = UK **2:1**. | 2026-08-17 |
| **York St John**, MSc Marketing | *"Minimum entry requirements: **2:2 honours degree from a UK ENIC listed institution**"* — note it references **UK ENIC's list of recognised institutions**, not a credit count. | 2026-08-17 |
| **Westminster**, MA Music Business Management | *"A minimum of an upper second class honours degree (2:1) **or a lower second class honours degree (2:2)** in a relevant degree."* | 2026-08-17 |

**146 ECTS verdict for the UK: `LIKELY OK` — in fact the credit figure is simply never asked for.**
Université de Sousse is a recognised public institution, so it is UK ENIC-listed. At **12–13/20 he
lands on/just above the 2:2 line**, which is the published minimum at the affordable universities.
He is *below* the 14/20 needed for a 2:1, so **any programme demanding a 2:1 is out** (this
excludes LIPA).

**One wording trap:** LJMU writes *"a **four year** Licence/Bachelor Degree"*. Tunisia's LMD licence
is **three** years. Bradford's page says only "Licence" with no year count. → Mark LJMU
`BORDERLINE`, Bradford/YSJ/Westminster `LIKELY OK`. Worth one email to admissions.

**UK ENIC Statement of Comparability — cost and necessity:**
- **£69.60** including the e-statement; **15 working days**. Paper copy +£12.60 UK / **+£75.60
  international**. A Fast Track exists (price not published on the cost page).
- **He almost certainly does NOT need to buy one.** Universities assess Tunisian qualifications
  in-house using their own country tables (above). Buy it only if an admissions office explicitly

| Westminster | **IELTS 6.0 (min 5.5 speaking & writing) to apply**; **IELTS 6.5 (min 6.0 writing, nothing below 5.5) to start** — a useful two-stage structure |

**The waiver question — the answer is NO, and this is important for his timeline.** The main UK
waiver is the **Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter**, which requires that his *previous degree was
taught and examined in English*. **His FSEG Sousse licence was taught in French/Arabic, so MOI does
not apply.** The secondary route — the Home Office permits a Higher Education Provider at degree
level *"to assess your level of English themselves"* via internal tests or interviews — is real and
is how "no IELTS" universities operate, **but it is discretionary, decided case-by-case, and cannot
be planned around.**

**Practical guidance:** he should plan to sit a test. Cheapest credible options are the **Duolingo
English Test** (widely accepted at post-92 universities, ~$65, at-home) or **IELTS UKVI**. Note
Westminster's structure means he can *apply* at 6.0 and reach 6.5 before enrolment — that buys
months. **Timeline: book a test by early 2027 at the latest.**
`NOT VERIFIED — check directly:` whether York St John and Wrexham accept Duolingo (their English
pages did not render).

## 8. IRELAND'S LEVEL PROBLEM — the mirror image of the UK's

Where the UK ignores credits, **Ireland asks for an NFQ Level 8 Honours Bachelor Degree**, and an
Irish L8 honours degree is commonly a **four-year** award. His **three-year** Tunisian licence is
therefore `BORDERLINE` across all Irish institutions — this is precisely why Phase 1 rated Ireland
CONDITIONAL, and my findings confirm rather than dissolve that.

**Mitigations, in order of usefulness:**
1. **ATU's published RPL route** — an explicit written alternative for applicants without the
   honours degree. Strongest single lever.
2. **NARIC Ireland (hosted by QQI)** issues **free online comparability statements** against the
   NFQ, from a database of 2,000+ qualifications from 170+ countries. **Free** — unlike
   ENIC-NARIC France's €120/4-months. If the Tunisian Licence is not in the database, *"you can
   apply for advice on the general academic recognition of your qualification."*
   `NOT VERIFIED — check directly:` I could not read the Tunisia entry; qsearch.qqi.ie is a JS
   search interface that returned no results to a fetch. **Action: run this free search early — it
   costs nothing and settles the Irish branch.**
3. The FSEG attestation (6 semesters / 3 years / 180 credits / confers access to Mastère) plus the
   *supplément au diplôme*, per the shared brief.

## 9. VERDICT — is the UK realistically viable on this budget?

**Qualified yes — but narrowly, and Ireland is the better bet.**

**In favour of the UK:** the credit problem that dogs him everywhere else simply does not exist
there; 12–13/20 is a published, sufficient 2:2; there is no ENIC requirement to fund or wait for
(vs 4–6 months in France); English-taught with no third language; and **York St John at £11,800
(~£11,210 after early-payment discount) is a genuine, verified, in-budget master's in exactly his
field** with a February start that could pull his timeline forward a year.

**Against:** the affordable set is **three institutions wide** (York St John, Wrexham, and
arguably Westminster after scholarship) — that is not a strategy, it is a shortlist with no depth.
The hidden costs are severe: **£558 + £776/yr IHS + a £10,539 balance he must actually hold**, then
**£937 + ~£1,553** again to activate the Graduate Route. And the **Graduate Route drops to 18
months for his cohort** — the UK's main selling point has been devalued by a quarter, right before
he arrives.

**Ranked recommendation for this scope:**
1. **MTU (Ireland) — net €11,000, automatic scholarship, 24-month post-study visa.** Best
   value/return combination anywhere in scope. Resolve the NFQ Level 8 question first (free).
2. **York St John MSc Strategic Digital Marketing — ~£11,210 net.** The best *academic-fit-to-price*
   ratio in the UK, and the one place where his 12/20 is unambiguously enough.
3. **ATU MSc Marketing — €14,000, with a published RPL fallback** and a mandatory industry
   placement; the safest Irish option for a borderline file.

**Music business: do not build the plan around it in the UK/Ireland.** Westminster is the only
realistic door (2:2 accepted, Sept 2027 open, net ~€15,000), and its music-industry-experience
expectation is a genuine obstacle for someone whose music work is an unportfolio'd hobby.
