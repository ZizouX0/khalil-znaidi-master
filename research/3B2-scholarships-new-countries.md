## 1. BOTTOM LINE

The single most valuable finding is **Slovakia**. The EUBA fee contradiction
resolves cleanly in his favour — **English-taught master's at EUBA cost €0/year**
for everybody, non-EU included; the €2,500 is a *penalty for exceeding the
standard 2-year duration*, not an international fee. On top of that, Slovakia
runs the **"Scholarships for Talented Students from all over the world"**
(€5,000/yr × 2 = €10,000), and **EUBA's MSc Marketing and Trade Management is on
the SK RIS3 eligible-programme list**. Critically, for foreign applicants the
**sole ranking criterion is the SAT score** (min. 1100) — *not* GPA, not a
motivation letter, not an English certificate. That converts his two biggest
weaknesses (12–13/20 and no English test) into a single solvable task: sit the
SAT. Nothing else found in this sweep comes close. Second best is **Estonia's
Harno national scholarship (€350/mo), for which Tunisia is explicitly named on
the eligible-country list**, and third is **Romania's MFA scholarship** (fully
funded, all non-EU nationals eligible, but Romanian-taught with a funded
language prep year, and a grade bar he may miss). **Norway's proof-of-funds
question resolves positively in principle** — UDI accepts "student loans, grants"
— but **no Norwegian institution in scope offers any scholarship** (three of them
say so in writing, and INN says it is *legally barred* from doing so), so there
is no grant to point at, and Lånekassen is closed to study-permit holders. The
practical route to NOK 170,368 is the Tunisian MESRS *prêt universitaire à
l'étranger* already documented in 3B-scholarships §3.5.

**Verdict: `admission.euba.sk` is right. English-taught master's at EUBA cost
€0/year for a non-EU student on the standard 2-year track. The Slovak €2,500
figure is a late-completion penalty, not an international fee.**

The Slovak internal regulation B/5/2025 (in force 1 Sept 2026) states that the
**obligation to pay annual tuition applies only to** students who study:

> *"1. Dennou formou v **nadštandardnej dĺžke** štúdia na I., II. alebo III.
> stupni štúdia; 2. Externou formou v nadštandardnej dĺžke štúdia …; 3. Externou
> formou štúdia …"*
> — i.e. **(1) full-time beyond the standard length of study, (2) part-time
> beyond the standard length, (3) part-time.**

The €2,500 line sits in a table headed:

> *"Výška určeného školného na I., II. a III. stupni štúdia **za prekročenie
> štandardnej dĺžky štúdia** v inom ako slovenskom jazyku"*
> — **"tuition for *exceeding the standard length of study* in a language other
> than Slovak"** — and lists *"2. stupeň program **marketingový a obchodný
> manažment** (v anglickom jazyku) — 2 500,00 €"*.

**Consequences:**
- A full-time student who completes the Ing. in the standard **2 years pays
  €0** — Slovakia charges **no differential non-EU fee at all**; the fee trigger
  is duration and study form, never nationality.
- **He pays €2,500 only if he needs a third year.** That is a real risk at
  12–13/20 in a foreign-language programme, and should be budgeted as a
  contingency, not an expectation.
- Fee reduction/waiver/deferral of even that penalty can be requested in writing
  from the Dean by **31 October** of the year concerned (form:
  `2026-ziadost-o-upravu-skolneho.docx`).
- **Application fee: €90.** **Application window for autumn 2027: opens
  1 January 2027, closes 28 February 2027.**
- **⚠ One thing to verify:** the Slovak admissions brochure states
  *"Cudzinci (okrem ČR) sú povinní vykonať prijímaciu skúšku zo slovenského
  jazyka"* — foreigners must sit a Slovak-language entrance exam. This almost
  certainly applies to the **Slovak-taught** programmes only, but
  `NOT VERIFIED — check directly` with prijimacie.konanie@euba.sk before relying
  on the English-taught route.

## 4. NORWAY — THE PROOF-OF-FUNDS QUESTION, ANSWERED

**The question:** Norway requires **NOK 170,368 (~€14,562)** demonstrated. Does a
scholarship or a formal study loan count toward it?

**ANSWER: YES in principle — UDI accepts grants and loans, not only cash. But in
his case there is no grant to point at, so the practical answer is a loan.**

**Primary source, UDI, verbatim (checked 2026-08-17):**

> *"You must have at least … **NOK 15 488 per month, or NOK 170 368 per year** …
> This amount of money may consist of **student loans, grants, own funds that you
> have in a Norwegian bank account or the deposit account of the educational
> institution**, or a combination of the above. If you have already been offered a
> part-time job in Norway, the income from this work may be included."*

Three things follow, and the distinctions matter:

1. **Loans and grants are named as acceptable categories in their own right.**
   The "must be in a Norwegian bank account" clause attaches **only to *own
   funds***. A grant or loan is documented by its award/approval letter. **So a
   scholarship letter does satisfy the requirement.**

2. **Tuition is a *separate* requirement on top of the NOK 170,368.** UDI:
   *"If you have to pay tuition fees, you must **also** have money to cover this
   … The money must be in a Norwegian bank account or the deposit account of the
   educational institution. **If you have an exemption from paying tuition fees
   this must be confirmed by your educational institution.**"* At Nord's
   NOK 60,000 economics rate, the real total to demonstrate is ~NOK 230,000
   (~€19,700), not €14,562.

3. **But no Norwegian scholarship exists for him** — see §2. Nord, INN and UiA
   all state in writing that they offer none; USN's exemptions are only through
   closed institutional programmes; and **there is no national scheme** since the
   Quota Scheme ended in 2016.

**And Norwegian student loans are closed to him.** Lånekassen requires a
qualifying basis that a study permit alone does not create — permanent residence,
protection/asylum, compelling humanitarian grounds, family immigration to a
Norwegian or independently-resident person, **24 months' continuous full-time
employment in Norway immediately before studies**, or **three years / 180 credits
already completed in Norway**. A newly arriving Tunisian student meets none.
(https://lanekassen.no/en-US/rules-and-regulations/tildeling/del1-kapittel2/foreign-nationals/foreign-nationals/ — checked 2026-08-17.)

**Therefore the only mechanism that actually unlocks Norway is a *foreign* study
loan** — and one already exists in his file: the **Tunisian MESRS *prêt
universitaire à l'étranger*** documented in `3B-scholarships.md` §3.5 (annual
October call). A formal loan-approval letter from the Tunisian ministry is
precisely the "student loan" category UDI names. **This is the concrete
recommendation: if Norway is to stay on the list, the MESRS loan application is
the enabling step, and its October timing must be locked into the calendar
alongside the Norwegian admission round.**

**Practical caution:** UiA and INN both operate a **Flywire "deposit for living
costs"** transfer to the university's deposit account, and INN states the
transfer is *"a requirement from … (UDI) for the residence permit application."*
So institutions in practice expect the money to **move**, not merely to be
evidenced. **`NOT VERIFIED — check directly` with the specific institution
whether it will accept a loan-approval letter in lieu of the deposit transfer.**
That is the single question that decides whether Norway is viable, and it must be
asked of the named university, in writing, before he applies.

**Norwegian tuition, 2026/27, for context (all official, checked 2026-08-17):**
- **Nord University:** **NOK 60,000/yr** for humanities, social sciences and
  economics/administrative subjects (down from NOK 141,000–280,000); NOK 85,000
  for STEM/health/media. Conditional on the pending legal amendment; current
  rates apply until the bill passes.
- **USN:** selected programmes at **NOK 50,000 / 60,000 / 90,000 per year**.
- **UiA:** rates published per programme for 2026/27; **no scholarships.**
- **INN:** publishes low rates (e.g. Norwegian Language and Culture at
  NOK 29,100/yr); **no scholarships, legally barred.**

Norway is therefore **cheaper on tuition than the brief assumed but harder on
funding**: the money must come entirely from Tunisia.

## 5. ERASMUS+ AND INTRA-EUROPEAN MOBILITY — CAN IT ADD MONEY ON TOP OF A PLACE HE ALREADY HOLDS?

**Short answer: yes, but only *after* he is enrolled, and it funds a semester
abroad rather than his degree.**

- **Erasmus+ KA131 study/traineeship mobility.** Eligibility runs on
  **enrolment, not nationality**: a student officially enrolled in a degree at a
  HEI holding a valid ECHE is eligible, and institutions state this explicitly
  (e.g. Université Paris Cité: all regularly enrolled students are eligible
  *"regardless of their nationality"*). **Once he is enrolled at EUBA, Corvinus,
  Vilnius, an Estonian HEI or a Norwegian one (Norway is a Programme-associated
  country), he can compete for an Erasmus+ semester and its grant** — typically
  a monthly mobility grant plus travel, and Erasmus+ **traineeship** grants for
  an internship abroad. Up to 12 months per study cycle.
  **Verdict: REALISTIC — but it is money for months 3–18 of his master's, not a
  way to fund entry.** It does not reduce tuition at the home institution.

- **Erasmus+ KA171 international credit mobility (the "international
  dimension").** Tunisia is a KA171 partner country and Tunisian universities
  (e.g. Université de Carthage) hold KA171 projects. **But it is nomination-based
  and requires him to be an enrolled student at the Tunisian home institution**,
  with an inter-institutional agreement in place. Once he graduates from FSEG
  Sousse he loses that status, so **KA171 cannot be used as a route into a
  European master's.** He would have needed to use it during the licence.
  **Verdict: CLOSED for his situation.** Worth one check with FSEG Sousse's
  international relations office only if he has not yet formally graduated.
  `NOT VERIFIED — check directly` whether Université de Sousse holds any KA171
  agreement covering master's-level incoming mobility.

- **Erasmus Mundus:** out of scope here — fully covered in `3A-erasmus-mundus.md`
  (SDSI and CLIDE).
