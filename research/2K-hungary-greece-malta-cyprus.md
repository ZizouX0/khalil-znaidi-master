## 1. Bottom line

**Hungary is the answer, and the Stipendium Hungaricum recommendation is now backed by
real destinations.** The single most important finding of this report: Tunisia's SH
entitlement is **"Full degree master studies: in any field of interest"** — there is
**no field restriction at all** for Tunisian applicants, so *every* SH-listed master's
is open to him, including marketing. Two SH-eligible, English-taught, 120-ECTS
marketing master's exist and both suit him: **Corvinus MSc Marketing** (which requires
**no English certificate whatsoever** — level is assessed in an internal entrance
exam) and **BGE MSc Marketing** (**IELTS 5.5 / Duolingo 95**, one of the lowest English
bars found anywhere in this project). Crucially, **Hungary's 146-ECTS risk is close to
nil**: Hungarian master's entry is governed by *subject-area* credit minimums, not by a
total-credit rule, and Corvinus MSc Marketing demands **"at least 5 ECTS credits in the
listed subject areas."** Beyond Hungary, **Cyprus University of Technology's MSc
Experiential Digital Marketing Communications (€7,500 total, public)** and the
**University of Malta's MA Digital Marketing Communication (€9,500 *total*, not
annual)** are both genuine, well-priced, English-taught options — but both gate on
**IELTS 6.5**, which he does not have. **Greece is effectively closed to him**: the two
relevant AUEB master's require a **C2 English diploma**, and every foreign degree must
clear **DOATAP** recognition, which is exactly where a 146-ECTS transcript is most
likely to be challenged. **Directions 3 and 4 (music marketing / music business)
returned absolutely nothing across all four countries.**

### 3.1 THE 146 ECTS VERDICT FOR HUNGARY — `LIKELY OK` (strongest verdict in this report)

Hungary does **not** apply a total-credit rule. Hungarian law sets **subject-area credit
minimums** for master's entry, published per programme in each university's
*"honnan-hova"* (from-where-to-where) credit-recognition table. Corvinus's official table
states, for **MSc Marketing**:

> "**To be admitted to the Master's programme, you must have at least 5 ECTS credits in
> the listed subject areas.**
> – Methodology (e.g. Mathematics, Statistics, IT),
> – Economics Foundation (e.g. Microeconomics, Macroeconomics, International Economics,
> Regional Economics, Economic History),
> – Business Foundation (e.g. Business Economics, Business Finance, **Marketing**,
> Accounting, Management and Leadership, Human Resource Management)."
> — *Minimum credit requirement: 5 ECTS. Full credit requirement: 5 ECTS.*
> <https://www.uni-corvinus.hu/downloads/aw4e.100dhye/honnan-hova-tabla-2025a-v0422en.pdf>

**A Licence in Marketing clears a 5-ECTS threshold many times over.** Whether his
transcript reads 146 or 180 is irrelevant to this test. The only gate that remains is
the generic one — that the diploma be "**legally recognised to be equivalent to a
Hungarian undergraduate degree (Act C, Recognition, 2001)**," which is a
*degree-level* question, not a credit-count question.

For the one-year **MSc Marketing Strategy and Innovation** the bar is higher — *Minimum
36 ECTS / Full 48 ECTS*, including **min. 15 ECTS Marketing Foundation** — but the same
table adds the decisive safety valve: "**Missing credits must be acquired within 2
(active) semesters during your MSc studies.**" Hungary lets you enter with a deficit and
make it up inside the degree. This is a materially more forgiving regime than Germany,
Switzerland or the Netherlands.

Pécs words it even more favourably, in years-of-access terms: "*a BA/BSc certificate …
**which entitles the applicant to enter into a master's program in the applicant's home
country***" — his Licence does exactly that.

### 5.4 Cyprus — country notes, and a blunt read

- **Post-study work:** up to **12 months** to seek employment after a master's; converts
  to a work/residence permit (1–5 years) on securing a job; PR possible after 5 years'
  continuous legal residence.
- ⚠️ **The killer detail: Cyprus applies a LABOUR MARKET TEST.** A vacancy must first be
  offered to **Cypriots and EU citizens**; only if no qualified candidate emerges may it
  go to a non-EU national. **For a generalist marketing graduate — a field with abundant
  local and EU supply — this test is very hard to pass.** It is designed to admit
  scarce skills, and digital marketing is not scarce.
- **Blunt verdict: a Cypriot master's is unlikely to convert into Cypriot employment.**
  The degree is real, EU-recognised and portable, and CUT at €7,500 is genuinely good
  value — but treat Cyprus as **a cheap route to an EU qualification, not a route to a
  job in Cyprus.** The small economy is concentrated in tourism, shipping and financial
  services; the graduate labour market is thin and youth unemployment persistently
  elevated. If the goal is study-to-work conversion, **Hungary and Malta beat Cyprus
  decisively.**
- **Cost of living:** `NOT VERIFIED` — Limassol is the most expensive Cypriot city.

## 6. GREECE — effectively closed to him

### 6.1 The two relevant programmes, and why both fail

**AUEB — MSc in Marketing & Communication, Full-Time with International Orientation (PRIMA)**
- Public university, Athens. **3 semesters.** **Taught in English.** Direction **2**.
- **Tuition €6,900 total**, in 4 instalments (€1,500 / €1,800 / €1,800 / €1,800). No
  separate non-EU rate published. <https://www.dept.aueb.gr/en/marketing/prima/content/tuition-fees>
- Entry: "Undergraduate degree from an accredited Greek or foreign university."
- ⛔ **"English language diploma (C2 level)."**
- Deadlines (2026 cycle, indicative): **1 March / 3 May / 14 June**.
- Total ECTS `NOT VERIFIED` — <https://www.dept.aueb.gr/en/marketing/prima>

**AUEB — MSc in Marketing Analytics** (Direction **1** — the best thematic fit in Greece)
- "**The first and only graduate programme of its kind in a Greek University.**"
- **3 semesters full-time. Tuition €4,800 full-time** (€5,800 part-time) — cheap.
- ⚠️ Language of instruction: "**Greek and/or English**" — **not** a fully English
  programme. `BORDERLINE — verify the actual delivery language.`
- ⛔ "**Diploma of English language (C2 for full time program, C1 for part time)**."
- Part-time additionally requires **2 years of working experience**.
- Deadlines (2026, indicative): **30 April / 30 June**. <https://www.dept.aueb.gr/en/marketinganalytics>

Other AUEB English-taught master's: **MSc Business Analytics**, **MSc Data Science**,
**MBA International** — all more quantitative than his background supports.

### 6.2 Why Greece is closed — two compounding blockers

1. **The C2 English requirement.** Not IELTS 6.5, not B2 — **C2, the top of the CEFR
   scale**, evidenced by diploma. For a student with *working English and no test at
   all*, C2 is a multi-year target, not a gap year. This alone rules Greece out for
   autumn 2027. It is also **far** stricter than Corvinus (no test), BGE (IELTS 5.5) or
   even Malta/CUT (IELTS 6.5).
2. **DOATAP.** "Applicants from foreign institutions must provide a **recognition
   certificate from the Hellenic National Academic Recognition Information Center
   (DOATAP)**." DOATAP performs a substantive equivalence assessment and is slow and
   exacting — **this is precisely the venue where a 146-ECTS transcript is most likely to
   be challenged**, and unlike Hungary there is no subject-credit safety valve and no
   "make it up during the master's" provision.
   **146 ECTS verdict for Greece: `BORDERLINE → BLOCKING`**, pending DOATAP.

### 6.3 Greece — country notes

- **Non-EU students DO pay for master's in Greece.** Free tuition applies to
  *undergraduate* study; postgraduate programmes (ΠΜΣ) charge fees at public
  universities. Fees are nonetheless low by EU standards (€4,200–€7,500 typical).
- **Post-study work:** **Law 5038/2023 (New Migration Code), Article 119** codifies a
  post-study residence permit for **job search or entrepreneurship**, typically **up to
  12 months** — but it is "**not an automatic entitlement for every graduate**" and
  conditions apply. A separate **Talent Visa (Type Z.15)** offers 12 months to recent
  master's/PhD graduates, but is restricted to graduates of **globally ranked**
  universities. `NOT VERIFIED` whether AUEB qualifies for Z.15.
- **Blunt employability read:** Greece has among the **highest youth and graduate
  unemployment in the EU**, a long-running graduate-emigration problem, and a labour
  market where Greek-language fluency is required for the great majority of marketing
  roles. **A non-EU marketing graduate with no Greek is very unlikely to convert an
  Athens master's into Greek employment.** Do not recommend Greece for this student.
