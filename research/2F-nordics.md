## 1. BOTTOM LINE — read this before anything else

**The single most important thing I found is that two of the four countries have
moved in opposite directions since this project's earlier phases, and both
movements are large enough to overturn earlier agent conclusions.**

**FINLAND HAS CLOSED. NORWAY HAS RE-OPENED.** My brief told me Finland's tuition
scholarships are "extensive — many universities waive 50–100%." That was true for
intakes 2017–2025. **It is dead for autumn 2027.** A Finnish law obliging
universities to charge *full-cost* (*täyskatteellinen*) tuition to non-EU/EEA
students takes effect **1 August 2026**, and — verified in Finnish on the OKM
sourcing — *"keväästä 2026 alkaen apurahoja ei enää myönnetä aloittaville
lukuvuosimaksuvelvollisille hakijoille"* — **from spring 2026 onward, scholarships
are no longer granted to incoming fee-liable applicants.** I confirmed this
independently at three universities' own pages: Jyväskylä ("During the 2026
admission round there are **no university level-scholarships available for the
first year** of studies"), Oulu ("There are **no scholarships for the 1st year
students**. They are only entitled to Early Bird Discount"), and Uniarts Helsinki
("The university **does not grant any scholarships** to cover the tuition fee for
students starting in August 2026 or later"). What survives in Finland is not a
scholarship system but a **discount system** — early-bird payment discounts and
second-year performance waivers — which typically cuts the sticker price by
25–35%, not 50–100%. Net Finnish cost is therefore **€7,000–€10,000/year**, not
near zero.

Meanwhile **Norway, which Agent 3F correctly excluded on cost, has reversed**.
The Norwegian government proposed repealing the cost-covering requirement
(regjeringen.no, id3109395), citing an ~80% collapse in non-EEA enrolment since
fees were introduced in 2023; the Storting was to treat it in spring 2026 with
effect from 1 August 2026. In anticipation, **UiA, Nord, USN, INN, UiT, UiS,
OsloMet and NMBU all announced lower 2026/27 fees.** I verified the resulting
rates directly: **University of Agder charges NOK 63,000/year (≈€5,400) for its
Master in International Business and Master in Management and Business
Development**, and Inland Norway lists NOK 58,200 (≈€5,000) as its lowest
category, covering *economics and administration*. **That is the cheapest verified
tuition anywhere in this agent's four countries, and it lands inside the brief's
top affordability band (under €8,000/year).** 3F's verdict was right on the
evidence available then and is now stale.

**The candid affordability verdict the orchestrator asked for:** the Nordics are
**not** uniformly unaffordable, but the affordability is **narrow and specific**,
and it is *not* where the brief expected it. Denmark and Norway are affordable at
their cheap institutions; Finland is mid-priced with no route to zero; Sweden is
affordable only in one-year 60-ECTS format, and **Tunisia is not on the Swedish
Institute's eligible-country list**, which removes the only Swedish full-funding
route. Ranked by net two-year tuition:

| Country | Best verified net tuition | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Norway** (UiA / INN / USN, business) | **≈€5,000–5,400/yr → €10,000–10,800 total** | **BEST TUITION.** Blocker is liquidity, not cost (below). |
| **Denmark** (Aalborg, Business/Social Sci.) | **€3,725/semester = €7,450/yr → €14,900 total** | **BEST OVERALL.** Plus ~15 fully-funded government scholarships/yr, automatic consideration. |
| **Finland** (JYU / Oulu / UEF / LUT / Vaasa) | **€7,000–€10,000/yr net → €14,000–€20,000 total** | Viable but no longer cheap. Best post-study permit in Europe. |
| **Sweden** (Skövde) | **SEK 110,000 ≈ €9,900 for the whole 60-ECTS year** | Affordable *only* as a 1-year master. No SI funding for Tunisians. |

**And the honest caveat that matters most:** Norway's cheap tuition is largely
neutralised by the **NOK 170,368/year (≈€14,600) proof of funds** for the study
permit (studyinnorway.no, 2026/27) — the highest liquidity bar of the four, and
roughly triple Finland's **€9,600**. For a Sousse family, Norway's problem was
never really the tuition; it is showing €14,600 in a bank account. Denmark's
DKK 90,492 (≈€12,100) sits close behind. **Finland is the cheapest country to
*prove* you can afford, Norway the cheapest to actually attend.**

**Directions 3 and 4 (music): I broke the project's zero.** Two genuine,
state-recognised music-business master's exist in the Nordics and neither has
been reported by any prior agent. Both are real; **one is unaffordable and one is
a stretch.** Details in §5. This is the only region in the project where
Direction 4 exists at university level at all.

## 2. COVERAGE STATEMENT — what I searched, and what I found nothing on

**Databases mined**

- **Opintopolku / Studyinfo.fi (Finland) — mined programmatically**, not browsed.
  I queried the `konfo-backend` search API directly

  data-driven, business analytics, service design, creative industries, music,

  university-level master's**, from which I resolved provider institutions via
  the per-programme endpoint `/konfo-backend/koulutus/{oid}`. **This is a complete
  sweep of the official Finnish register, not a sample.** The API is open, needs

  is not rediscovered a third time.
- **universityadmissions.se** (Sweden), **studyindenmark.dk** (Denmark),
  **studyinnorway.no** (Norway) — used as entry points; every figure re-verified

- **si.se** (Swedish Institute), **migri.fi**, **migrationsverket.se**,

## 5. DIRECTIONS 3 & 4 — MUSIC MARKETING / MUSIC BUSINESS

**Read this section as a corrective, not a win.** Prior agents found zero
music-business master's in Spain, Portugal, Italy, Benelux, Austria and the whole
Erasmus Mundus catalogue. **The Nordics are the exception: two exist. Neither is
affordable at list price, and one of them is the most expensive programme in this
entire project.**

### ★ Uniarts Helsinki (Sibelius Academy) — **Music Business, Master of Arts**
- Helsinki, **Finland** · **MA (Art and Design), 120 ECTS** (90 compulsory + 15
  elective + 15 optional), 2 yrs · **public specialist arts university** · **English**
- **Contents are a genuine Direction 3 + 4 hybrid with real marketing weight:**
  copyright management, genre strategies, live-event sustainability, **global
  markets, distribution strategies, branding**, music export, intercultural
  communication, with **digitalisation as a declared cross-cutting theme.** On
  *fit* alone this is the best match to the student's stated ambition found
  anywhere in this project.
- **Timing is uncannily good and then irrelevant.** The programme launched autumn
  2025 and **admits only every other year**. Uniarts states: *"The next application
  period for this programme is scheduled for 2027"* — **application January 2027,
  studies begin August 2027.** That is exactly the target intake. Admissions guide
  updates **October–November 2026**.
- Selection is **not** grade-driven — **stage 1: motivation essay (max 1,000 words)
  + CV (max 2 pages), 30%; stage 2: a 40-minute remote interview in English, 70%**,
  scored 1–5 on *"depth, clarity and quality of your answers, your communication
  skills, suitability, educability, and motivation... as well as your understanding
  of the music business."* **No entrance audition, no portfolio, no GMAT.** A
  12–13/20 average is largely irrelevant to this rubric, and his **self-built CRM
  platform** is exactly the kind of thing that scores on it. **On admissibility
  this is one of the most accessible programmes in the entire project.**
- **AND THEN: €28,000 PER YEAR. €56,000 TOTAL. NO SCHOLARSHIPS OF ANY KIND.**
  Uniarts' own page: *"The university does not grant any scholarships to cover the
  tuition fee for students starting in August 2026 or later"* and *"Uniarts
  Helsinki does not grant scholarships for living expenses."* The only carve-out is
  the **Global Music** programme, which is a performance degree, not this one.
  Source: `https://www.uniarts.fi/en/general-info/tuition-fees/`
- **VERDICT: `EXCLUDED`. Nearly four times the brief's exclusion ceiling with a
  verified zero-funding position.** This is the clearest example in my scope of the
  full-cost law's effect: an arts university's true per-student cost is enormous, so
  full-cost pricing puts it out of reach of exactly the students it would suit. **I
  am recording it in full because it is the best-fitting programme found and
  because someone will otherwise find it again and get excited.** Do not shortlist it.

| **Denmark** | 3-year job-seeking permit (replaced the Establishment Card, **abolished 1 April 2023 — do not cite it**) | **Up to 3 years** | **NOT unrestricted.** 90 hrs/month Sept–May; full-time June–Aug only. A real job needs a **separate** unlimited work permit or job-specific permit | **Looks best, isn't.** The long stay is real; the conversion is the wall. 3I verified Denmark's Pay Limit Scheme at **DKK 552,000 ≈ €74,000** — roughly double junior marketing pay. | **Sweden** | Permit to seek employment after studies | **12 months, non-extendable** | Job-seeking / business exploration; must prove self-support | Adequate. Requires ≥2 semesters completed — the Skövde 60-ECTS route only just qualifies. |
| **Norway** | Job-seeker permit | Not re-verified this run (3F's scope) | — | Norway's attraction is the **study** economics, not the exit. |

**The employability read, candidly.** For a **non-EU, non-Nordic-speaking
marketing graduate**, the Nordic labour market is **English-friendly in tech and
data and Nordic-language-gated in marketing.** 3I's Europe-wide finding holds
here: domestic marketing roles run in Finnish/Swedish/Danish/Norwegian, and the
English-working roles are engineering, data and product. **His French buys him
nothing in any of these four countries.** The practical consequence is that
**Direction 1 (analytics) converts and Direction 2 (marketing) largely does not**
— the job title on the contract decides whether he can stay. **Finland's permit is
the only one in this group generous enough to absorb a slow, difficult search**,
and it is the strongest single argument for choosing Finland despite it being
neither the cheapest tuition nor the cheapest liquidity bar.

## 9. ENGLISH — the binding constraint, restated for this region

**He has no test.** Every programme here requires one, and there is **no French
exemption anywhere in the Nordics** — French buys nothing. The verified bar:
**AAU IELTS Academic 6.5 (6.0 per section) / TOEFL iBT 85 (20 per section)**, and
**AAU accepts neither Duolingo nor PTE**, which removes the cheapest routes from
Tunisia. Skövde requires **Swedish "English 6"**, satisfied by IELTS/TOEFL.
Uniarts and UiA require English but I did not verify their exact scores.
**Tests must be under 2 years old at the deadline** (AAU) — so a test taken now
would still be valid for a January 2027 application, but **a test taken now would
expire before a January 2028 application.** Timing the test matters.

**All programmes in this report are Tier A.** No Tier B or C route is worth
opening: Finnish, Swedish, Danish and Norwegian are all long-haul languages for a
French speaker, and — per 3I — **the return on learning one is near zero unless he
intends to stay permanently.** Note the mercenary exception: **Finnish and
Swedish universities pay for language study** — Vaasa gives **€1,000** off year 2,
UEF **€2,500**, and Oulu **requires 5 ECTS of Finnish** to unlock its year-2
waiver. Take the money; do not mistake it for a career strategy.
