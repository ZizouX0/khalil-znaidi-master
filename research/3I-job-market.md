# 3I — European job market & visa sponsorship, honest assessment

**Agent 3I · scope: labour market + work-permit feasibility for a Tunisian (non-EU) graduate**
**All pages checked 2026-08-17 unless stated. Today = 2026-08-17. Target: autumn 2027 intake → graduating ~mid-2029.**

---

## 1. Bottom line

The brief's hypothesis is **CONFIRMED, and the mechanism is worse than "small companies won't sponsor."**
Three independent, verifiable barriers stack against the two music directions: (a) roughly a third of
cultural-sector work in the EU is **self-employment**, which no employee work permit covers at all
(Eurostat 2025: 31.6% self-employed in cultural employment vs 13.7% economy-wide); (b) the **whole-occupation
median pay** for music and arts occupations sits *below* the general work-permit salary floor in the two
biggest English-language markets — so a junior fails by a wide margin, not a narrow one; and (c) in the UK,
musicians (SOC 3415) and arts officers/producers/directors (SOC 3416) were **struck off the Skilled Worker
route entirely** in July 2025. But the same evidence delivers an uncomfortable second finding the brief did
not anticipate: **generic digital marketing is nearly as unsponsorable as music.** Ireland lists
"Sales, Marketing and Related Associate Professionals" (SOC 354) as an **ineligible occupation** for
employment permits, and the UK put "Advertising and marketing associate professionals" (SOC 3554) in the
sub-degree table that new applicants can no longer use. So the real split is not music vs marketing. It is
**"marketing-titled roles" vs "data/ICT-titled roles"** — and only the second column is reliably sponsorable
anywhere. Ranking: **Direction 1 ≫ Direction 2 > Direction 4 > Direction 3.** His CRM platform is the single
asset that lets him compete in the sponsorable column, and the strategy that follows is to lean technical
hard and treat music as a *sector he sells into*, never as the job title on his contract.

## 3. Direction ranking — the honest order

### 🥇 Direction 1 — AI / data applied to marketing. **STRONGEST. This is the only direction where sponsorship is routine.**

Why it wins is not vibes, it is occupational classification. Work permits are granted against **job titles and
occupation codes**, not against your interests. Data and ICT codes sit in the eligible, higher-paid tables
everywhere I could verify:

| UK SOC code (verified, gov.uk Appendix Skilled Occupations) | Going rate, Table 1 | Table 2 (discounted) | Eligible for new Skilled Worker? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2133 IT business analysts, architects & systems designers | £54,900 | £42,400 | **Yes** (RQF6+) |
| 2134 Programmers & software development professionals | £54,700 | £40,000 | **Yes** (RQF6+) |
| 2135 Cyber security professionals | £48,500 | £35,300 | **Yes** (RQF6+) |
| 2433 Actuaries, economists & statisticians | £55,100 | — | **Yes** (RQF6+) |

Eurostat's earnings analysis independently confirms the pay premium: *"Information and communication ranked
among the 3 highest paying economic activities in every EU countries."* High pay is not a nice-to-have here —
it is the thing that clears the permit threshold.

**Realistic target titles:** Marketing Data Analyst, CRM Analyst, Marketing Automation / Martech Specialist,
Business Analyst (Marketing), Customer Data Platform Analyst, Analytics Engineer (Marketing).
**Language demand:** lowest of the four directions. Analytics teams are English-working even in Paris,
Munich and Amsterdam.

### 🥈 Direction 2 — Digital / growth marketing. **MIDDLING, and legally worse than most people assume.**

This is where the report gets uncomfortable. Two of Europe's most sponsor-friendly English-language markets
have effectively **closed the door on junior marketing job titles**:

- **Ireland — SOC 354 "Sales, Marketing and Related Associate Professionals" is on the Ineligible Categories
  of Employment list** (official DETE page, list last updated **13 May 2026**). An ineligible occupation
  cannot get a General Employment Permit at any salary. A "Digital Marketing Executive" offer in Dublin is,
  for a non-EEA graduate, **not convertible into a work permit at all.**
- **UK — SOC 3554 "Advertising and marketing associate professionals" sits in Table 1a** (RQF 3–5), which
  since **22 July 2025** is restricted to people who already held Skilled Worker permission before that date.
  Going rate £33,400 — below the £41,700 general threshold anyway. A new applicant cannot use it.
- The marketing codes that *are* eligible in the UK are **senior**: SOC 1132 Marketing, sales and advertising
  **directors**, going rate **£87,300** (Table 1) / £60,000 (Table 2). That is a 10-to-15-year job, not a
  first job. SOC 2493 Public relations professionals is eligible at a £37,000 going rate, but the £41,700
  general floor still bites.

So Direction 2 survives mainly in **France, Germany, Poland, Spain and Italy**, where permits are not gated
on an occupation list — and in all five of those, **local-language fluency is effectively mandatory for a
marketing role.** More on that in §6. Verdict: viable, but crowded, language-gated, and legally fragile in
exactly the two countries where his English would have been enough.

### 🥉 Direction 4 — Music business / music industry management. **WEAK as a job title, but has one real door.**

The music *industry* does contain large, sponsoring employers — Universal, Sony, Warner, Spotify, Deezer,
Believe, Bandcamp, Songtradr, Utopia. The problem is what they sponsor for. Large music companies sponsor
**engineers, data scientists, product managers and analytics people**. They fill "music business" generalist
roles — A&R admin, label management, rights administration, sync coordination — from a deep domestic pool at
low pay. A degree titled "music business management" competes for the roles they *don't* need to sponsor,
against locals who already have industry networks.

**The door that is actually open:** get hired into a music-tech or streaming company on a **data/CRM/analytics**
requisition. Same employer, same sector, sponsorable job code, salary that clears thresholds. This is the
single most useful sentence in this report for him if he wants music at all.

### 🔻 Direction 3 — Music marketing / artist promotion. **WEAKEST. This is a bad bet for someone who needs a visa. Say it plainly.**

The brief asked for directness, so: **for a non-EU graduate needing sponsorship, Direction 3 is close to
unworkable as a primary plan.** Four verified reasons:

1. **Self-employment.** Eurostat (2025): *"Almost a third (31.6%) of people in cultural employment across the
   EU were self-employed, compared with an average of 13.7% in the whole economy."* Artist promotion,
   plugging, tour marketing and independent PR are disproportionately freelance. **An employee work permit
   cannot be issued for freelance work.** Roughly a third of the sector's jobs are therefore not just hard to
   get — they are *legally invisible* to the permit system he needs.
2. **Employer size.** Eurostat (2023): **2.1 million cultural enterprises** in the EU generating €202bn value
   added, with *"Creative, arts and entertainment activities"* the largest category by enterprise count
   (28.6%), and micro-enterprises dominating employment in the adjacent creative categories (e.g. photographic
   activities 91.8% micro, specialised design 82.4% micro). *[Derived, flagged as inference: 2.1m enterprises
   against 8.9m cultural workers ≈ 4 people per enterprise on average.]* Sponsorship requires an employer with
   HR capacity, a registered sponsor licence (UK), or the appetite to run a single-permit file (BE/NL/DE).
   A four-person label will not do this, and in the UK **legally cannot** without buying a sponsor licence.
3. **Precarity.** Eurostat (2025): cultural employment is less full-time (76.6% vs 81.2%) and less permanent
   (83.5% vs 87.3%) than the economy at large. Most permits require a **full-time, ongoing contract**.
   Part-time and fixed-term-short offers routinely fail the permit test even when the salary looks fine.
4. **Pay.** See §4 — this is the decisive one.

That makes the following table an **official statement of what these jobs actually pay across all experience

**Read that carefully. The median musician and the median arts producer — people with careers, not
graduates — earn below the UK's general work-permit threshold.** A first-job entrant in those occupations
earns materially less than the median. There is no salary at which a junior music-industry role clears
£41,700, and the occupation is off the eligible list anyway.

**Where a music-sector junior salary FAILS outright — the answer to the brief's question:**

- **UK** — fails twice: below threshold *and* SOC 3415/3416 removed from the route on 22 July 2025.
  **Total block.**
- **Belgium** — a junior label/promo/artist-marketing salary in Brussels realistically lands €28k–€34k
  *[NOT VERIFIED — inference from sector structure]*. Every Belgian regional floor is **€39,129–€53,220**.
  **Fails in all three regions, including the under-30 discount.** Belgium is the cleanest illustration of
  the brief's hypothesis: the discount for young workers is not remotely deep enough to reach music pay.
- **Denmark** — DKK 552,000 (~€74,000). **Fails by roughly a factor of two.** No junior anything clears this.
- **Netherlands** — €52,284/yr under 30 unless he holds a Dutch degree. If he studies in France, he arrives
  in NL on the full rate. **Fails.**
- **Germany** — Blue Card at €45,934/€50,700: **fails.** (The non-Blue-Card §18b skilled-worker permit for
  degree holders has no comparable fixed floor and is the realistic German route — but Germany is already
  CLOSED for him at admission stage per Phase 1.)
- **France** — **the decisive borderline case.** 1.5×SMIC = **€33,606**. A junior *data/CRM/analytics* role in
  Paris clears this. A junior *chargé de projet* at an independent label, a promo assistant, or an artist-
  marketing coordinator generally does **not** *[NOT VERIFIED — no French music-sector wage survey obtainable;
  inference from the verified UK occupational medians and from French sector structure]*. **This single number
  is the most consequential fact in his whole plan**, because France is his most likely destination and
  €33,606 is precisely the line that separates the two halves of his interests.
- **Ireland** — the salary question is moot for marketing and irrelevant for music: **SOC 354 is ineligible**,
  and music/arts roles would in practice need the Critical Skills list, which they are not on.

**Verdict on the hypothesis: HOLDS, decisively.** With one correction the brief should absorb: the
*"music is harder than marketing"* framing is true in France, Germany, Poland, Spain and Italy — but in
**Ireland and the UK, junior marketing is blocked by occupation list just as music is.** The robust
distinction is technical vs non-technical, not music vs marketing.

A long job-search visa is worthless if conversion is rare, as the brief says. That is exactly the pattern.

| Country | Post-study permit | Duration | Conversion difficulty — the honest read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UK** | Graduate visa | **2 years if applying on/before 31 Dec 2026; 18 months if on/after 1 Jan 2027** — **VERIFIED (gov.uk)**. He graduates ~2029 → **18 months** | **HARD.** Free to work in *any* job during it, including music. But converting requires a **licensed sponsor** + an **RQF6 occupation** + £41,700 (or new-entrant floor). Marketing-associate and music codes are excluded. Many Graduate-visa holders simply leave. |
| **France** | APS / RECE "recherche d'emploi ou création d'entreprise" | 12 months, master's-level graduates | **MODERATE — the best of the set.** Conversion is not gated on an occupation list, and French-master's holders are exempt from the labour-market test. | **Netherlands** | Orientation year (zoekjaar) | 12 months | **MODERATE — but only for Dutch graduates.** The €3,122/mo reduced rate is tied to a Dutch degree/zoekjaar. Irrelevant if he studies elsewhere. |
| **Germany** | Job-seeker residence after study | 18 months (widely reported; **NOT VERIFIED here**) | **MODERATE for data roles** (§18b has no hard salary floor), **hard for marketing** because German-language fluency gates the jobs. Moot — Germany is closed at admission. |

| **Belgium** | Search year | 12 months (**NOT VERIFIED**) | **VERY HARD.** Free to work during the search year, then the single permit demands €39,129–€53,220. Classic cliff-edge. |

**The pattern:** Belgium, Ireland and the UK all give generous, work-permitted search periods and then apply
a hard gate at conversion. **The search period is not the constraint. The conversion gate is.** Judge a
country by its gate, not its search year.

## 6. Who actually sponsors — and where his French is worth money

### Countries and cities with genuine demand for sponsorable non-EU marketing/data talent

- **Ireland (Dublin)** — the densest EMEA hub in Europe: Google, Meta, LinkedIn, Salesforce, HubSpot, Stripe,
  Workday. Huge appetite for multilingual talent, well-established sponsorship machinery. **But the
  occupation list is the trap:** they will happily hire a French speaker into a "Marketing Specialist,
  French Market" role that **cannot be permitted**. He must target requisitions titled *Analyst*,
  *Data*, *Insights*, *Business Intelligence*, *Marketing Operations* — roles that map to Critical Skills
  ICT codes. Same building, same team, different job code, completely different visa outcome.
- **France (Paris, Lyon, Bordeaux, Lille)** — native-language market, no occupation list, moderate threshold.
  **Strategically his best country by a clear margin.**
- **Poland (Kraków, Warsaw, Wrocław), Czechia (Prague), Portugal (Lisbon), Spain (Barcelona)** — shared-service
  and martech delivery centres that hire **French speakers to serve French markets in English-speaking
  offices**. Genuine, volume demand for exactly his language pair. Thresholds are low. **Caveat:** many such
  roles are customer-success/inside-sales flavoured, i.e. the low-ceiling end, and I could **not verify**
  any of these countries' permit rules (§2).
- **Germany (Berlin, Munich, Hamburg)** — real data demand, but **marketing roles are German-language**; the
  English-working roles are engineering/data/product. Moot for him at admission stage regardless.
- **Nordics / Denmark / Netherlands** — English-friendly workplaces, but the **highest verified salary floors
  in Europe**. Deprioritise.

### Where local language is effectively mandatory — and what French changes

Marketing is a language profession. Copy, campaigns, customer research, brand tone, agency briefs and
stakeholder meetings all happen in the market's language. A non-speaker is not "at a slight disadvantage";
they are usually not shortlisted.

| Market | Language reality for marketing roles | His position |
|---|---|---|
| **France** | French mandatory, near-native | **Native. Decisive advantage.** He competes as a local, not as a foreigner. |
| **Belgium — Brussels & Wallonia** | French works | Advantage — **but Brussels (€44,441) and Wallonia (€42,576 u30) have the highest floors** |
| **Belgium — Flanders** | **Dutch** required for marketing | **Mismatch:** Flanders has the cheapest under-30 threshold (€39,129.60) and the jobs — and the wrong language |
| **Luxembourg / Switzerland (Romandie)** | French works | Small markets; Switzerland closed at admission per Phase 1 |
| **Ireland** | English only | No language barrier — **but the occupation-list barrier replaces it** |
| **Netherlands** | English tolerated in tech, Dutch preferred in marketing | Salary floor blocks him anyway |
| **Germany / Spain / Italy / Poland / Nordics** | German / Spanish / Italian / Polish / Nordic language required for domestic marketing | Barrier — except in English-working **data** teams and French-language SSC roles |

**The strategic reading of his French:** it is a genuine, monetisable asset, but it is only *simultaneously*
a legal advantage in **France**. Everywhere else it either buys him into a role that cannot be sponsored
(Ireland, and the SSC hubs) or lands him in a region with a threshold he cannot reach (Brussels, Wallonia).
**France is the one place where his language, the permit rules and the job market point the same way.**

*Footnote worth knowing:* Ireland's ineligible list carves out call/contact-centre roles for "fluent speakers
of non-EEA official languages." **French is an EEA official language, so it does not qualify — but Arabic
does.** That is a real, if low-ceiling, sponsorable niche for him. Mention it as a fallback, not a plan.

## 8. Marketing vs data — the strategic recommendation

**Lean technical. Decisively. This is not a close call, and pretending otherwise would be doing him a disservice.**

The evidence is not about which field is more interesting. It is that **immigration systems across Europe
have independently converged on excluding non-technical junior white-collar roles.** The UK moved marketing
associates below the RQF6 line. Ireland put sales-and-marketing associate professionals on the ineligible
list. Belgium, the Netherlands and Denmark set floors above what junior marketing pays. Meanwhile every
system I could verify keeps ICT/data occupations eligible, at going rates 40–65% above the marketing codes,
comfortably clearing every threshold in the table.

Framed bluntly: **the job title on his contract will decide whether he can stay in Europe, and "Marketing
Executive" is a losing title while "Marketing Data Analyst" is a winning one — for substantially the same
work at the same company.**

His CRM platform is real evidence for the technical column, and it is the reason this recommendation is
realistic rather than aspirational. He has already built a customer-data system end-to-end; most marketing
graduates have not, and most data graduates have never thought about retention economics. That intersection —
someone who can write the SQL *and* explain why churn matters — is genuinely scarce and genuinely hireable.

**Concrete strategy:**

1. **Choose Direction 1** — a master's in marketing analytics / data-driven marketing / business analytics
   with a marketing track. Direction 2 only as a hybrid with a heavy quantitative core.
2. **Base in France.** Native language, no occupation list, the lowest verified conversion threshold in
   Western Europe (€33,606), and a 12-month post-study permit. **Confirm the 1.5×SMIC rule before committing** (§2).
3. **Target job titles containing Analyst / Data / CRM / Operations / Intelligence**, never Executive /
   Assistant / Coordinator.
4. **Treat music as a sector, not a job.** If he wants the music industry, the entry is a data or CRM
