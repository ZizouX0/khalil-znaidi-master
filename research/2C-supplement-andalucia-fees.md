# 2C supplement — the Andalucía non-EU surcharge question, resolved (orchestrator)

Agent 2C flagged this as the finding that could collapse its cheapest result:
its ≈€1,230 total for the UMA digital-marketing máster rested on the fee page
being **silent** about a non-EU surcharge, not on it saying there is none.
Checked directly on **2026-08-17** against UMA's own published fee annex.

Source: https://www.uma.es/media/files/ANEXO_C_preciosMaster_00G9p4e.pdf

## Verdict: the low fee HOLDS — but it is conditional, and the condition has a deadline

There **is** a surcharge clause. It simply does not bite for a student on a
study visa. The annex's footnote states that the standard tariffs apply
*provisionally* to:

> *"estudiantes de nacionalidad extranjera, mayores de dieciocho años, que no
> tengan la condición de residente –temporal o de larga duración- en España **o la
> autorización de estancia por estudios**"*

and that affected students must file proof of residence **or of the study
authorisation** before **1 November**, failing which:

> *"se procederá a efectuar una liquidación complementaria que podrá alcanzar el
> **100% del coste real** de la prestación de los citados servicios."*

**Read plainly:** a non-EU student who holds (or has applied for) an
*autorización de estancia por estudios* — which this student will necessarily
have, since he cannot study in Spain without one — pays the **standard public
rate**. A non-EU student who fails to file the paperwork by 1 November can be
billed **up to 100% of the real cost of the course**.

So this is not a nationality surcharge. It is an **administrative-compliance
surcharge**, and it is avoidable. But the downside is severe and the deadline is
hard, so it belongs in the action plan as a dated task, not a footnote.

## Correction to the per-credit rate

Agent 2C used **€12.62/credit**. The annex gives two first-enrolment rates:

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| *Másteres habilitantes* (professional-qualifying) | €12.62 / credit |
| ***Másteres no habilitantes*** (everything else — **including marketing**) | **€13.68 / credit** |

A marketing master's is **non-habilitante**, so the correct rate is **€13.68**.

Recomputed for a 90-ECTS máster, first enrolment:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| 90 ECTS × €13.68 | €1,231.20 |
| Apertura de expediente académico | €59.10 |
| Tarjeta de identidad / data maintenance | €5.70 |
| **Total for the whole degree** | **≈ €1,296** |

2C's headline of "≈€1,230" was right by coincidence — it matches the
*non-habilitante* arithmetic, not the €12.62 rate it cited. The conclusion
stands; the reasoning needed fixing.

**Also note the repeat-enrolment penalty:** a failed module costs **€27.35/credit**
on second enrolment, €52.13 on third, €69.51 on fourth — roughly double, quadruple
and quintuple the first-enrolment rate. Given this student passed several modules
by compensation in Tunisia, that risk is worth naming.

## The caveat that remains

**This annex is the 2023/2024 schedule** (its own title says
*Curso Académico 2023/2024*). The rate structure and the residence clause are
almost certainly stable — Andalucía has held public fees down for years — but the
exact 2027/28 figures are **`NOT VERIFIED`**. Re-check UMA's fee page in the
year of application. Treat ≈€1,300 as the right order of magnitude, not a quote.

## Why this matters beyond UMA

The residence/authorisation clause is written into the **Andalucían** public-price
framework, not a UMA quirk, so the same logic should apply across Andalucían
public universities — Granada, Jaén, Sevilla, Córdoba, Almería, Cádiz, Huelva.
That makes Andalucía as a region, not just Málaga, the cheapest verified route to
a state-recognised master's found anywhere in this project so far. Worth a
targeted second pass across those universities for all four directions.
