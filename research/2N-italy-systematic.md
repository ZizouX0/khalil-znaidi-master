## 1. BOTTOM LINE

The single most important thing this sweep found is that **2D's mental model of
Italian fees is only half right, and the half it missed is mostly good news.**
2D assumed the Bologna route — get an *ISEE parificato*, fall under the no-tax
threshold, pay €157 — is how Italy works. In fact Italian public universities
split into **two distinct regimes**, and for a Tunisian student the second one is
usually *better*:

- **Regime A — ISEE/ISEE parificato.** Bologna, Unimore, Parma, Salerno, Bari,
  Genova, Trento, Verona, Padova. Very cheap (€157–€200/yr) **but conditional on
  a stack of Tunisian income documents, legalised by the Italian Embassy in
  Tunis, on a hard autumn deadline.** Miss it and you land in the top band.
- **Regime B — automatic flat rate by country of residence.** Pavia, Milano-Bicocca,
  Torino, Pisa, Ca' Foscari, Sassari, Firenze, Calabria, Milano Statale.
  **No ISEE, no Tunisian paperwork, no deadline risk** — the university simply
  assigns a band from the World Bank / GDP-per-capita classification of Tunisia.

Because Tunisia sits in the **lower-middle-income / lowest band** of almost every
one of these tables, Regime B produces figures like **Pavia €390/yr all-in,
Milano-Bicocca €346/yr all-in, Torino €656/yr, Pisa €736/yr, Sassari ~€156 in
year 1**. These are *close to Bologna's €157 but with none of the documentary
risk*, which in practice makes them **safer, not worse**.

The one place Regime B bites hard is **Milano Statale, where Tunisia is in Group
C (the most expensive) and the regulation explicitly forbids an ISEE parificato
for tuition — ~€3,394/yr with no escape.** Ca' Foscari also bars ISEE parificato
for fee reduction (flat €1,300/yr). Politecnico di Milano charges non-EU the
*maximum* amount unless scholarship-backed. **Do not assume "Italian public =
€157".**

Second finding, equally valuable: **ER-GO's €7,171.11 is not an Emilia-Romagna
premium — it is the national MUR minimum for 2026/27.** Every region must pay at
least that to a *fuori sede* winner. And **EDISU Piemonte beats it: €8,096.78**,
with **€6 million ring-fenced for non-EU international students in hardship** and
**15% of first-year residence places reserved for extra-EU students whose family
lives in a non-EU country.** Turin therefore combines the second-cheapest tuition
regime with the richest grant found in Italy.

On programmes: **~24 qualifying *lauree magistrali*** across directions 1 and 2.
**Directions 3 and 4 remain a confirmed near-zero — I independently re-tested 2D's
negative and it holds.**

## 3. ⚠️ THE TWO FEE REGIMES — read before the table

**Regime A (ISEE / ISEE parificato).** Tuition is a function of the household
ISEE. A student whose family lives abroad cannot get an ordinary ISEE (DPCM
159/2013 art. 8) and must get an **ISEE parificato** from a *CAF convenzionato*,
supported by consular documentation. Falls below the no-tax threshold → pays only
regional tax + stamp (€140–€190 + €16).

**Regime B (flat rate by country).** The university assigns a fixed *contributo*
from a country table and **does not accept an ISEE parificato for tuition at all**
(Milano Statale, Ca' Foscari, and from 2026/27 Torino), **or accepts it only as an
opt-out** (Pavia, Pisa, Calabria). Regime B is *automatic* — the single biggest
practical advantage for a student in Sousse whose documents must cross an embassy.

> **Practical rule for this student: a €390 flat rate with zero paperwork is worth
> more than a €157 rate that depends on legalised Tunisian income certificates
> arriving before an October deadline.**

## 5. REGIONAL DSU GRANTS — the biggest money in Italian scope

### 5.1 The national floor (this corrects 2D)

The MUR minimum grant amounts for **a.a. 2026/2027** are:

| Category | Minimum amount |
|---|---|
| *Fuori sede* (living away from home — a Tunisian student always is) | **€7,171.11** |
| *Pendolare* | €4,190.71 |
| *In sede* | €2,890.16 |
| Max ISEE (ISEE Universitario / Parificato) | **€26,306.25** |
| Max ISPE | **€57,187.53** |

**These are floors that bind every region.** ER-GO's €7,171.11 (2D's headline
figure) is therefore *exactly the national minimum*, not an Emilia-Romagna
advantage. Any region can be at least as good. Source: DM 1320 as applied in the
EDISU Piemonte bando 2026/27; cross-checked against the ER-GO table in 2D.

### 5.2 ★ EDISU Piemonte (Torino, Piemonte Orientale) — the best found

Verified in full from the official bando PDF, 2026/27:

| ISEE band | *Fuori sede*, no residence place | *Fuori sede* in an EDISU residence |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ €13,153.12 | **€8,096.78** | €5,396.78 **+ free bed for 11 months (valued €2,700)** |
| €13,153.13 – €17,537.50 | €7,021.11 | €4,321.11 + bed |
| €17,537.51 – €23,626.00 | €5,586.89 | €2,886.89 + bed |
| €23,626.01 – €26,306.25 | €3,800.00 | €1,100.00 + bed |

Three things make this the strongest DSU finding in Italy:
1. **€8,096.78 > ER-GO's €7,171.11.**
2. **A dedicated non-EU fund:** *"CUP J61I26000230009 con stanziamento di 6
   milioni: saranno destinati agli studenti internazionali Extra-UE in situazione
   di svantaggio (con ISEE fino a metà del limite massimo)"* — €6m ring-fenced for
   non-EU students with ISEE ≤ €13,153.12. A Sousse family will be in that band.
3. **A reserved quota on housing:** of the 15% of beds allocated to first-year
   students, *"riservando il 15% agli studenti con cittadinanza Extra-UE con
   nucleo familiare residente in un Paese Extra-UE"*.

Non-EU applicants apply under art. 31 with **consular documentation** and get an
**ISEE parificato calculated by EDISU itself** — note the irony: Torino will
compute an ISEE parificato for the *grant* while refusing to use one for
*tuition*. Deadline (2026/27 cycle, indicative for 2027/28): **22 July –
4 September 2026, 12:00**. Rankings 9 Nov and 16 Dec 2026.
Source: [EDISU bando 2026-27 PDF](https://www.edisu.piemonte.it/sites/default/files/documentazione/bandi-di-concorso/Bando_borsa_di_studio_servizio_abitativo_premio_di_laurea_contributo_studenti_con_disabilit%C3%A0_dal_46_p.c._iscritti_al_collocamento_mirato_a.a._2026-27.pdf) (read 2026-08-17)

## 7. RESERVED PLACES FOR NON-EU APPLICANTS — a systematic feature

Every Italian degree course must publish, in the annual MUR circular on
international student access, a number of **"posti riservati a studenti non-UE
residenti all'estero"**. These are a *separate competition* from the domestic
pool. For an applicant with a 12–13/20 average this is a genuine structural
advantage — he competes against other non-EU applicants, not against Italian
graduates. **It applies to essentially every programme in this report**, not just
GIOCA. The per-course numbers are published each spring on each university's
"Studenti internazionali" page and on Universitaly; **the 2027/28 numbers are not
yet published (checked 2026-08-17)**.

Bicocca goes further and runs **a separate, earlier selection round reserved to
non-EU visa applicants**, explicitly so they have time for the visa — a real
scheduling advantage.

## 10. WHAT THIS CHANGES ABOUT ITALY'S RANKING

1. **Italy's cheap-route depth is much greater than 2D showed.** Not one €157
   university but roughly ten universities at or under €750/yr, several of them
   requiring **no Tunisian documentation at all**.
2. **Turin is arguably now the single best city in this whole project**: €656/yr
   tuition, an €8,096.78 grant with a €6m non-EU ring-fence and reserved housing,
   and an LM-77 marketing degree with open "titolo equipollente conseguito
   all'estero" entry wording. Its weakness is that the degree is Italian-taught
   (Tier B) and that Torino abolished the ISEE-parificato tuition reduction.
3. **Milano-Bicocca is the best Tier-A (English) option**: €346/yr, "Bachelor
   diploma (or higher)" entry, analytics inside a marketing degree, and a separate
   earlier admission round for non-EU visa applicants.
4. **Bologna is no longer uniquely cheap** — Unimore beats its threshold
   (€30,000 vs €27,000) inside the same ER-GO grant region.
5. **Directions 3 and 4 in Italy are closed.** Two LM-76 cultural-industries
   degrees, and nothing else. This is now confirmed twice.
