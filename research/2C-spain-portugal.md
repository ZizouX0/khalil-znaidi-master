## 1. Bottom line

**Spain is the single most affordable serious option found in this whole scope, and
Portugal is the most affordable *English-taught* one.** Three structural facts drive
this. (a) Spain has an explicit legal route — RD 822/2021 Art. 18 — letting a
university admit a non-EHEA graduate to a *máster oficial* **without homologación**,
via a paid "comprobación del nivel de formación" costing roughly **€47–€211** and
decided by the university itself; this is far cheaper and faster than the €120 /
4–6-month ENIC-NARIC France route, and it is a *level* check, which suits the 146-ECTS
transcript problem well. (b) Fees are set per autonomous community and vary by an
order of magnitude: **Andalucía charges €12.62/credit with no non-EU surcharge**
(a 60-ECTS máster oficial ≈ **€820 total**), whereas Catalonia (UAB €75/cr) and
Valencia (UA: double price for non-resident non-EU) land at €4,000–4,500/year — all
still inside filter tier 1–2. (c) Spain's post-study permit is **24 months,
non-extendable** (RD 1155/2024) — much longer than the 12 months widely reported —
but it **does not itself authorise work**; you must convert it once you have a job.
Portugal's equivalent (AIMA, art. 122/1(p)) is up to **1 year**. On language: for a
native French speaker **Spanish and Portuguese are firmly Tier B, not Tier C** —
Spanish B2 in ~6–9 months of serious study, European Portuguese B2 in ~9–12; but
Portugal also offers genuinely **Tier A** English-taught masters at public
institutions (NOVA IMS €7,500 total; Iscte €9,800 total; U.Porto FLUP €4,000/yr),
which Spain does not at these prices. The sharpest warning in this scope: **almost
every Spanish "máster en industria musical" is a *título propio* / *máster de
formación permanente*, not a state master's** — verified for UC3M, IL3-UB, UAM, SAE.
Do not treat any of them as a master's degree for visa or CV purposes.

## 3. The *máster oficial* vs *título propio* distinction — applied rigorously

This is the highest-value filter in Spain and it is applied to every entry below.

| | **Máster Universitario (oficial)** | **Título propio / Máster de Formación Permanente** |
|---|---|---|
| Legal status | State degree, verified by ANECA, published in BOE, entered in RUCT | The university's own certificate; no state verification |
| Credits | ECTS, EQF level 7 | "créditos" that are usually *not* ECTS |
| Doctorate access | Yes | No |
| Recognised abroad / EU Diploma Supplement | Yes | No |
| Student visa | Yes (long-stay study authorisation) | Institution must be an authorised higher-education centre; **the qualification itself does not make you a master's holder** |
| **Post-study job-search permit** | **Yes — meets the "Nivel 6 EQF minimum" requirement** | **Very likely NO** — the permit requires having *finalizado los estudios alcanzando como mínimo el Nivel 6 del MEC, correspondiente a la acreditación de grado universitario*. A título propio is not a state qualification and does not carry an EQF level. |

That last row is the decisive one for this student. **A título propio can cost €5,900
and leave you with no post-study permit.** Everything below is labelled.

## 4. Access without homologación — how it actually works (Spain) — **VERIFIED**

**Legal basis:** Real Decreto 822/2021, **Art. 18**. Holders of a qualification from
a **non-EHEA** system, equivalent in level to a Spanish *Grado*, may access an
official Spanish máster **without homologating the degree**, subject to
*comprobación por la universidad del nivel de formación que implican*, **provided
that in the issuing country the degree gives access to master's-level study**.
https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2021-15781

**What the university actually asks for** (verified in full on Universidad de Burgos,
which publishes the clearest procedure):
1. Application for level verification (*solicitud de comprobación de nivel*)
2. Passport copy
3. Official academic certification stating **duration of studies, subjects,
   credits/hours** ← *this is where the 146 vs 180 issue lives*
4. The degree certificate
5. **Proof that the degree gives access to postgraduate study in the issuing
   country** ← the FSEG Sousse attestation ("confère l'accès au Mastère") is exactly
   this document
6. Ministry-calculated academic average (*nota media*)
7. Legalisation (apostille) + sworn translation into Spanish

Exact wording quoted by UBU: *"La comprobación del nivel de formación de los estudios
extranjeros cursados es imprescindible para el acceso."* The university issues a
**written resolution**; it is valid at that university.

**Cost (verified, varies by university — the range is wide, so this is a real lever):**
| University | Fee for the equivalency/level study |
|---|---|
| Universitat de les Illes Balears | **€46.76** |
| Universidad Miguel Hernández (Elche) | **€139.70** |
| Universidad de Burgos | **€210.97** |
| Ministry route (Declaración de equivalencia, *not required here*) | €166.50 |

**Timing:** not published as a fixed SLA; it runs inside the normal preinscripción
window, i.e. weeks not months. **Compare with ENIC-NARIC France at €120 and 4–6
months** (Phase 1) — Spain's route is cheaper at some universities and far faster.

**146-ECTS verdict for Spain generally: `LIKELY OK`.** The check is of *nivel de
formación* and of whether the home country grants master's access — both of which the
Tunisian *licence* satisfies under Décret 2008-3123. Spain does **not** impose a
literal 180-ECTS-on-the-transcript rule at the legal level. Risk is at the individual
university's discretion, not the law. Attach the FSEG attestation and the *supplément
au diplôme* to pre-empt it.

**Portugal equivalent:** Portuguese universities admit foreign bachelor holders under
the *Estatuto do Estudante Internacional* + institutional recognition of the foreign
degree (DL 66/2018 regime); no homologation of the degree is required for access to a
mestrado, only recognition/verification by the receiving institution. The DGES pages
for this returned 404 on both URL patterns tried — `NOT VERIFIED — check directly` at
https://www.dges.gov.pt (Reconhecimento de Graus Estrangeiros).

## 9. Country notes

### Spain

**Study visa (Tunisian national):** long-stay study authorisation (*estancia por
estudios*), applied for at the Spanish consulate in Tunis. Requires the university's
letter of admission, proof of means, medical insurance, apostilled + sworn-translated
documents. Students on a study *stay* may work part-time under the current rules —
but note UAB's warning that this stay **does not confer resident status** for fee
purposes.

**Post-study work — VERIFIED, and better than commonly reported:**
*Autorización de residencia para búsqueda de empleo o emprender un proyecto empresarial*
- Legal basis: **RD 1155/2024** (new Reglamento de Extranjería, in force 20 May 2025);
  DA 17ª Ley 14/2013; Directive (EU) 2016/801; LOSU DF 5ª. Page last updated Sept 2025.
- Eligibility: held a long-duration study stay authorisation **and finished studies at
  an authorised Spanish higher-education institution reaching at least **EQF Level 6**
  (bachelor-degree accreditation)** — a máster oficial clears this; **a título propio
  almost certainly does not.**
- **Duration: *"un período de veinticuatro meses improrrogable"* — 24 months,
  non-extendable.**
- **Critical caveat, quoted:** *"Durante la vigencia de la autorización de residencia
  para la búsqueda de empleo o para emprender un proyecto empresarial, no se está
  autorizado a trabajar."* **You may not work on this permit** — it is a *search*
  permit. On finding a suitable job you must convert to a work authorisation.
- Application window: within the **60 days before** the study stay expires, or up to
  **90 days after** (with sanction risk).
- Means test: **100% of IPREM per month** (50% if accommodation is pre-paid).
- Resolution: **20 days**, positive silence thereafter. Fee: modelo 790 código 052.
- Useful downstream: the *situación nacional de empleo* is disapplied once the person
  has been legally resident in Spain for at least one year.
- Source: https://www.inclusion.gob.es/documents/410169/2260168/20.+Autorizaci%C3%B3n+de+residencia+para+b%C3%BAsqueda+de+empleo+o+emprender+un+proyecto+empresarial.pdf

**Language — Spanish for a native French speaker: TIER B, high confidence.**
- Level required: **B2** is the norm (UGR states it explicitly: *"se requiere un
  certificado oficial de nivel B2"*; URJC states B2 for international students).
- Accepted certificates: **DELE** (Instituto Cervantes, session-based, ~€200 at B2) and
  **SIELE** (on-demand, multi-level, results in ~3 weeks, cheaper and much faster to
  schedule — **SIELE is the pragmatic choice** for a fixed application deadline).
- **Realistic time from zero for a native French speaker: ~6–9 months** of serious
  study (roughly 400–500 contact-equivalent hours). French→Spanish is the easiest
  major-language transfer available to him: shared Romance morphology, ~75–85% lexical
  similarity, same tense/mood architecture including the subjunctive. This is *not*
  comparable to German or a Nordic language. If he starts now, B2 by mid-2027 is
  comfortable for an autumn-2027 intake.
- **Watch out:** UGR *also* requires **English B1 certified** — a second, separate
  certificate. He has working English but **no test taken**; budget for an
  Aptis/Linguaskill/Cambridge B1 or equivalent.
- **Free/subsidised courses:** Instituto Cervantes Tunis operates in Tunisia (paid but
  affordable); Spanish universities generally run their own *Centro de Lenguas Modernas*
  Spanish courses for international students at a fee (UGR's CLM is one of the largest
  in Spain). **No integrated preparatory-language route admitting below-level students
  onto a máster oficial was found** — Spanish universities expect the B2 at admission.
  `NOT VERIFIED` whether any of these specific programmes offer conditional admission
  pending language — worth asking each directly.
- **French exemption:** not applicable — Spanish programmes require Spanish, and none
  of the Spanish public programmes found teach in French.

**Cost of living — the second big lever.** The regional spread mirrors the fee spread.
Indicative monthly student budgets (rent in shared flat + food + transport):
Madrid/Barcelona **€900–1,300**; Valencia/Alicante **€700–900**; **Granada/Málaga
€600–800** — Granada is consistently among the cheapest university cities in Western
Europe. **These are indicative ranges, `NOT VERIFIED` against a primary official
source in this pass** — the official figure that *is* binding is the visa means test,
100% of IPREM/month. Combined with Andalucía's €820 fees, **Granada or Málaga is the
lowest total-cost pathway in this entire scope.**

### Portugal

**Study visa (Tunisian national):** national visa for study, then residence permit
under **art. 91.º** (higher-education students) via AIMA.

**Post-study work — AIMA, art. 122.º/1 al. p):** a student who held a residence permit
for 2nd- or 3rd-cycle study (i.e. **mestrado** or doctorate) and completed the studies
may change the permit type in order to work in Portugal. The job-search allowance is
**up to one year** to *"procurar emprego ou criar uma empresa em território nacional
compatível com as suas qualificações"*. Reported nuance worth verifying: those who
completed a **full** degree cycle in Portugal face fewer time limits than those who
completed only part. **This is 1 year against Spain's 24 months — Spain is materially
better on this axis.** Application is via the AIMA portal; note that AIMA has a
well-documented backlog.
Source: https://aima.gov.pt/pt/trabalhar/tendo-beneficiado-de-ar-para-estudantes-do-2-o-ou-3-o-ciclos-do-ensino-superior-ou-de-ar-para-investigacao-e-concluido-os-estudo
(fetched via search summary; direct fetch returned 503/SSL errors — `re-verify`)

**Language — Portuguese for a native French speaker: TIER B.**
- Level typically required for Portuguese-taught mestrados: **B2**; certificate
  **CAPLE** (Centro de Avaliação e Certificação de Português Língua Estrangeira) —
  the B2 exam is **DIPLE**; also **CIPLE** (A2), **DEPLE** (B1), **DAPLE** (C1).
- **Realistic time from zero for a native French speaker: ~9–12 months.** Grammar and
  vocabulary transfer from French is very strong (comparable to Spanish); the extra
  cost is **European Portuguese phonology** — heavy unstressed-vowel reduction and
  consonant clusters make listening comprehension the bottleneck, which is why it runs
  a few months behind Spanish rather than level with it. Still firmly Tier B.
- **Crucially, Portugal has a Tier A escape hatch Spain lacks:** NOVA IMS, Iscte and
  UCP Porto all teach **entirely in English** at prices inside filter. He could enrol
  with **no Portuguese at all**, then learn it on the ground. That materially changes
  Portugal's ranking.
- Free/subsidised: most Portuguese public universities run Portuguese-for-foreigners
  courses; several are free or low-cost for enrolled students. `NOT VERIFIED` per
  institution.

**Cost of living:** Lisbon **€850–1,200/month** (housing pressure is acute and has
worsened); **Porto €700–950**; Aveiro/Braga/Coimbra/Leiria **€550–800**.
Indicative, `NOT VERIFIED` against a primary source. **Porto is the sweet spot** —
both strong Direction-4 programmes (FLUP, UCP) are there.
