## Correction: the cap went UP, not down. The *grounds* narrowed.

Source: https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/fr/faq-droits-d-inscription-differencies-pour-les-etudiants-extra-communautaires-103043

2A wrote that the decree "caps exonérations at 30% in 2026-27, 25% in 2027-28",
implying a tightening of the quota. The ministry's own text says the opposite
about the quota:

> *"sous le régime antérieur, ces étudiants partageaient avec les étudiants
> extracommunautaires un plafond global d'exonérations individuelles de **10 %**...
> Avec le nouveau régime, chaque catégorie dispose désormais de **son propre
> plafond de 20 %**, porté transitoirement à **30 % en 2026-2027 et 25 % en
> 2027-2028**."*

So the individual-exoneration quota moved from **10% shared** to **20% per
category**, transitionally 30% then 25%. Numerically that is an increase.

What actually tightened is the **grounds**. The decree exists because:

> *"Cela a conduit à un phénomène d'**exonération massive** qui a dénaturé les
> objectifs de la réforme de 2019."*

and it now *"recentre les exonérations sur les cas suivants: **situation
personnelle de l'étudiant, bénéficiaires de bourses du gouvernement français,
accords de coopération avec réciprocité**."*

**2A's practical conclusion is right — do not budget on being exonerated — but for
the right reason.** It is not that the quota shrank; it is that blanket
institutional exoneration has been abolished and replaced with case-by-case
assessment on defined grounds.

## The opportunity this opens — which 2A missed

Two of the three retained grounds are things this student can actively pursue:

1. **"Bénéficiaires de bourses du gouvernement français"** are exonerated
   **de plein droit** (R. 719-49) — not subject to any quota at all. A French
   government scholarship therefore carries a **double benefit**: the stipend,
   plus automatic removal of the €3,950 differentiated fee. This makes the
   *bourses du gouvernement français* available through the French Embassy /
   Institut français in Tunisia materially more valuable than their headline
   amount suggests. **Agent 3B should treat this as a priority.**
2. **"Situation personnelle... notamment au regard de leurs ressources"** —
   individual exoneration on **means** grounds is explicitly retained. This suits
   a self-funding Tunisian student far better than merit-based schemes do. It is
   applied for at the institution, after admission.

Also relevant: **"Le fait d'avoir obtenu un bac français ne crée pas, en
lui-même, de droit particulier à l'exonération."** If he attended a French-curriculum
lycée, that alone earns nothing — worth knowing before he counts on it.

And a smaller trap: students enrolled **only** for a *diplôme d'établissement* or
*diplôme universitaire* fall outside the differentiated-fee quota base entirely.
These are **not national master's degrees** and are excluded under §4 of the brief
— do not let a cheap "DU" look like a bargain master's.
