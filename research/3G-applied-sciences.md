# 3G — Universities of Applied Sciences: cross-country sweep

**Agent 3G · scope: applied-sciences sector only (hogescholen, hautes écoles, Fachhochschulen, UAS, TUs, politécnicos, professionshøjskoler, HES) · all four directions · checked 2026-08-17**

---

## 1. Bottom line

The "applied sciences are more flexible" claim is **true in exactly one place in this sweep, and it is not where anyone expects it: Austria.** The Austrian *Fachhochschul-Gesetz* §4(4), reproduced verbatim on every FH programme's admission fact sheet, defines master's entry as *"the completion of a subject-relevant university of applied sciences bachelor degree program **or the completion of an equivalent degree program at a recognized domestic or foreign post-secondary educational institution**"* — with an explicit clause letting the programme director admit a candidate whose equivalence is only partial and settle the gap through *"examinations to be taken **during** the master degree program."* **There is no 180-ECTS count anywhere in that text.** The only credit number FH Kufstein publishes is *"at least 30 ECTS"* in marketing/communication/management/business subjects — which a Licence in Marketing clears several times over. Combined with a €726.72/year tuition that is identical for third-country nationals, 100%-English teaching, a **B2** English bar that accepts a plain medium-of-instruction letter instead of IELTS, and a fixed 3 March deadline for non-EU applicants, **FH Kufstein Tirol's MA Digital Marketing is the single best applied-sciences result in this entire sweep** and probably the cheapest genuinely-open master's this student will find anywhere in Europe. Everywhere else the myth fails, and in one case spectacularly: the Belgian *haute école* ICHEC — a practice-oriented institution — demands a **Bac+4 with a minimum 13/20 average**, which is *stricter* than any German Fachhochschule we examined. Directions 3 and 4 (music) are close to empty in the applied sector; the honest answer is that the applied-sciences route serves directions 1 and 2 well and directions 3 and 4 barely at all.

## 5. Country-level notes on the applied sector

**Austria — the one genuine flexibility finding.** §4(4) FHG is a *national statute*, so its equivalence-based wording applies at **every** Austrian Fachhochschule, not just Kufstein. Where an Austrian FH runs an English-taught, full-time master in a relevant field, the same reasoning transfers. FH tuition of €363.36/semester is the standard national rate and Kufstein applies it to third-country nationals unchanged. **Recommend a wider Austrian FH sweep as follow-up work** — FH Wien der WKW, IMC Krems, FH JOANNEUM, FH St. Pölten and FH Salzburg were not reached (site-access failures / no search quota), and any of them could yield a second Kufstein-class result.

**Ireland — worth the attention the brief predicted, but not cheap.** The Technological Universities are the applied sector and they are *not* meaningfully cheaper than traditional Irish universities: non-EU postgraduate taught fees run **€11,250 (SETU Carlow) → €13,500 (TU Dublin) → €14,000 (ATU on-campus) → €14,500–21,500 (TUS)**. The compensations are real: **24 months post-study work** (the best in this sweep), **mandatory placements** (TU Dublin's 12 weeks), **English-only so Tier A throughout**, and entry rules stated in *level/class* terms (2.1 or 2.2 honours, Level 8) rather than credit totals — so the 146-ECTS artefact is rarely the thing that fails. What fails is the **grade**: TU Dublin's 2.1 is above his reach and only the discretionary clause saves it; SETU states **no minimum class at all** and is therefore the most accessible Irish door.

**Netherlands.** HBO master's are the right level for a *Licence Appliquée* and the wrong level for nothing else — the AUAS programme's own wording never mentions credits. **The whole Dutch question reduces to the Fondamentale/Appliquée branch.** Resolve it.

**Belgium.** Flanders is structurally empty at master's level in the professional sector. French-speaking hautes écoles *do* award master's — and ICHEC shows they can be **harder** than a Fachhochschule, with a Bac+4 plus a numeric GPA floor. Do not assume a haute école is a soft option.

**Finland — the myth's clearest failure, confirmed.** Finnish UAS master's are explicitly designed as post-experience degrees. Metropolia states it plainly: *"Our students come with the minimum of two years of work experience; in many programs 10+ years."* The requirement flows from the Universities of Applied Sciences Act and applies sector-wide. **A marketing internship is not two years of relevant post-degree employment. Finland's UAS sector is closed to this student — say so plainly and stop looking there.** Finnish *universities* (not UAS) are a different question and outside my scope.

**Germany.** Fachhochschulen apply the 6-semester/180-ECTS rule as Phase 1 established, and where they do not print a credit number (Popakademie) other filters bind instead. Nothing in the German applied sector survived this sweep for any of the four directions.

**Portugal.** Politécnicos are the quiet winner on *price*: **€3,000/year international, halvable to €1,500 by a phase-1 merit award**, with access defined by possession of the *grau de licenciado* — a degree-status test that the 146-ECTS artefact cannot touch. The cost is language: teaching is in Portuguese. For a native French speaker that is the **fast** category, not the slow one, and it is the best value-for-effort trade in the report after Austria.

**Norway.** Public applied institutions (INN) charge ~**NOK 58,200/year (~€4,900)** to non-EU students — half what the private applied høyskoler charge. Norway's years-based assessment tradition (Phase 1) shows up in INN's *Digital Communication and Culture* wording (80 ECTS specialisation, no 180 total) but **not** in its *Business Analytics* wording, which prints 180 ECTS explicitly. **Test per programme, never per country.** Watch the gatherings-based delivery format — it may not support a study permit.

**Denmark, Sweden, Switzerland.** Denmark: structurally no full-time English master's in the professionshøjskole sector — a real negative result. Sweden and Switzerland: **not resolved this round** — declared as coverage gaps above rather than guessed at.

## 6. Where the "applied sciences are more flexible" myth holds and where it fails

**Holds — with the admissions wording as evidence:**

| System | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Austria (all FHs, by statute)** | §4(4) FHG: *"…or the completion of an **equivalent degree program** at a recognized domestic or foreign post-secondary educational institution"* + conditional admission via *"examinations to be taken **during** the master degree program."* No credit total. Only threshold: **≥30 ECTS** in the subject core |
| **Portugal (politécnicos)** | *"Titulares do grau de licenciado ou equivalente legal"* — degree-status test, no ECTS figure |
| **Norway, INN Digital Communication & Culture** | *"A completed bachelor's degree, or a degree recognized as equivalent, with a specialisation of at least 80 ECTS…"* — 80, not 180 |
| **Ireland (TU sector, partially)** | Requirements stated as NFQ **Level 8 / honours class**, not credit totals; SETU states no minimum class; TUS and ATU both publish **Recognition of Prior Learning** portfolio routes |

**Fails — and Phase 1 was right:**

| System | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Finland (UAS)** | *"Our students come with the minimum of two years of work experience"* (Metropolia). Sector-wide. **He is disqualified.** |
| **Belgium (hautes écoles)** | ICHEC: non-EU need **Bac+4** in economics/management, **minimum 13/20**, degree ≤3 years old. Stricter than any German FH checked |
| **Germany (FH)** | 180-ECTS rule holds; Popakademie is credit-silent but blocks on C1 German + 6 months creative-sector experience + entrance exam |
| **Norway (INN Business Analytics)** | *"a Bachelor's degree (**180 ECTS credits**)"* + 30 ECTS of statistics **and** mathematics + C average |
| **Denmark** | No master's level in the professional-college sector at all |
| **Switzerland** | Phase 1's 180-ECTS-plus-experience finding stands; **not re-verified by me** (site unreachable) |

**One-line rule for the final report:** *test flexibility per institution, per programme, and read the actual admission fact sheet — the sector label predicts nothing.*
