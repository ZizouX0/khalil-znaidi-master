## 1. BOTTOM LINE

This sweep produced **one genuinely outstanding find and one very good one**, plus a
long tail of €4,500–6,500/yr programmes that are competent but do not beat what is
already on the shortlist. The outstanding find is **Bratislava University of Economics
and Business (EUBA)**: two English-taught, AACSB-accredited marketing master's at
**€0 tuition**, with **no written entrance exam, no published English-certificate
requirement**, a €90 application fee, and Bratislava living costs of €260–718/month.
It is the only programme found anywhere in my scope that is simultaneously free,
on-direction, accredited, and open to an applicant with no language test. The second
find is **ASE Bucharest's MSc Customer Relationship Management** — an almost exact
match to his final-year CRM project — at €4,500/yr, but into a weak labour market.
Estonia is the strongest *country* (best English-medium ecosystem, real digital-economy
jobs, 9-month job search) but **the University of Tartu abolished non-EU tuition
waivers from 2026/27**, so it now costs €4,800–5,000/yr — good value, not cheap.
**Directions 3 and 4 (music marketing, music business) returned ZERO across all eight
countries.** The single adjacent result is the EAMT/EBS *Cultural Management* MA in
Tallinn. **Bulgaria and Latvia yielded nothing worth his time.**

### Language tiers

**Every programme in this file is Tier A (English-taught).** No Tier B or Tier C
programme is recommended: Estonian, Latvian, Lithuanian, Slovene, Croatian, Slovak,
Romanian and Bulgarian are all **Tier C for a French/Arabic speaker** — none is in the
Romance family except Romanian, and even Romanian would be a 12-month-plus commitment for
academic use. **Romanian is the one exception worth flagging**: as a Romance language it
is markedly faster for a French speaker than any of the others, and ASE offers
Romanian-taught master's at lower fees, plus **master's programmes taught in French** —
which I did not enumerate and which may deserve a follow-up given his native French.

Several institutions offer **free or subsidised host-language courses** to enrolled
students (standard across Estonian and Lithuanian universities); not verified per
institution.
