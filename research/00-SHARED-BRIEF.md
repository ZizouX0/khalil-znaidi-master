## 2. Phase 1 findings — ALREADY ESTABLISHED, do not re-research

**The 146 ECTS is almost certainly a transcript artefact, not a shortfall
(confidence ~85–90%).** Tunisian law (Décret 2008-3123, Art. 5) fixes the
licence at **180 credits over six semesters**. A UE is *capitalised* only at
≥10/20 (Art. 34), but students progress by **annual compensation** at a ≥10/20
average (Art. 35) — so a transcript printing only "crédits capitalisés" drops
every compensated UE. 180 − 146 = 34 credits ≈ 5–8 UEs, exactly the profile of a
12–13/20 student.

**Practical consequence:** treat him as holding a 3-year, 180-credit bachelor's
equivalent. Where a university's wording is about *years of study* he is fine.
Where it demands a literal 180 ECTS on the transcript, mark `BORDERLINE` and note
that a free FSEG attestation (6 semesters / 3 years / 180 credits / confers
access to Mastère in Tunisia) plus the *supplément au diplôme* normally settles it.

**UNRESOLVED — carry both branches.** We do not know whether he holds a *Licence
Fondamentale* or a *Licence Appliquée*. Nuffic rates **Fondamentale = 2 years of
WO** (below a Dutch bachelor) and **Appliquée = 3 years of HBO** (fine). This
only bites in the Netherlands. If your scope includes NL, cover both branches.

**Verified cost/timing for a formal equivalency statement (primary source,
checked 2026-08-17):** ENIC-NARIC France charges **€20 + €100 = €120**, takes
**4 months average, up to 6**, and covers **one qualification per file**.
