## 1. BOTTOM LINE

**Three of 2F's four declared gaps are now closed, and all three closed
favourably.** (a) **The Storting has voted.** The Norwegian fee repeal is not
"probable-not-certain" — it is law. Kunnskapsdepartementet's press release of
**7 July 2026** states in Nynorsk: *"kravet om at studieavgifta minst må dekke
kostnadene for studieplassen er no fjerna"* — the requirement that tuition must
at least cover the cost of the study place **is now removed** — *"Endringane vil
gjelde frå 1. august 2026."* (b) **Aalborg's non-EU deadline is 15 January, not
1 March**, and the residence-permit restriction 2F worried about applies **only**
to the second-round "available study places", not the main September round —
exactly as 2F suspected but could not confirm. (c) **INN and USN both verified**,
and USN turned out to be the better lead of the two.

**Norway is now, by a wide margin, the cheapest verified destination in this
entire project.** The new business/economics/administration band has converged
across the sector at **NOK 58,200–63,300 (€4,975–€5,410) per year**. Nine
institutions sit in it. The single best-matched programme is **USN's Master in
Digital Transformation at NOK 60,000/yr (€5,128), taught in English, 120 ECTS**
— a genuine Direction-2 hit at a price no other country in this project
approaches. Close behind: **INN's MSc Business Administration – Business
Analytics at NOK 58,200 (€4,975)**, the cheapest verified tuition anywhere in the
project, and a Direction-1 hit; and **NTNU's Master in International Business and
Marketing at NOK 61,705 (€5,274)**, which NTNU has carved out as a *separate,
discounted* rate against its own standard business band of NOK 176,300.

**But the liquidity bar is confirmed and it is the real obstacle.** Verified on
studyinnorway.no: **NOK 15,488/month = NOK 170,368/year (2026-2027) ≈ €14,562**,
which must be shown *in addition to* tuition. Norway asks a Sousse family to show
roughly **€14,600 in cash plus ~€5,100 tuition ≈ €19,700 for year one**. The
tuition is trivial; the proof-of-funds is not. This is the correct way to frame
Norway to the student — not "cheap", but "cheap to attend, expensive to qualify
for".

**Sweden does not survive.** One line, as instructed: **fees SEK 100,000–190,000
(€9,000–€17,100)/yr, the Swedish Institute scholarship is closed to Tunisians,
and every university-level award I could verify is a *partial* (25–50%),

**Finland survives but only as a considered trade.** Net €7,000–€10,000/yr is

entire goal is an international career — but only if he can actually convert it,

thin.** My honest read: Norway at €5,100/yr with a 1-year job-seeker permit beats
Finland at €8,500/yr with a 2-year one, *for this student*.

**Portugal is now properly swept and it is the cheapest Tier-A/B country after
Norway.** Best new finds: **U.Porto FEP Mestrado em Marketing** (top-15
Eduniversal Western Europe, propinas €1,500–€5,000/yr for 2026/27) and
**Universidade de Aveiro's Mestrado em Marketing e Comunicação Digital**
(€4,150/yr international) — the latter is the single best *named* match to

**Music (Directions 3–4): I found nothing new that beats UiA.** Denmark and

## 6. FINLAND — what actually remains, and whether the permit justifies it

2F's finding stands: the full-cost tuition law took effect **1 Aug 2026** and
**scholarships are no longer granted to incoming fee-liable applicants**. What
survives is a **discount system**. Verified mechanics:

| Institution | Sticker | Early-bird (yr 1) | Yr 2 waiver | **Net 2-yr total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UEF (Eastern Finland)** | €10,000–12,000 | **−30% (€3,000–3,600)**, pay within 21 days | **−30%**, needs ≥55 ECTS in yr 1 | **€14,000–€16,800** |
| **Vaasa** | €14,000 | **−€6,000**, pay within 21 days → €8,000 | **−€5,000**, needs ≥60 ECTS → €9,000 | **€17,000** |
| **LUT** | €15,000 | **−€5,000** → €10,000 | **−€5,000**, needs ≥60 ECTS → €10,000 | **€20,000** |

Notes: UEF adds that **50% waivers in Business School programmes go to "only a
very limited number"** of admitted students — do not plan on it. Vaasa's figures
are the 2025 rates and are the ones to re-check. **Vaasa's Marketing Management
master's is the best Finnish direction match** and lands at **€8,000/yr in year
one** — inside band 3. LUT at €20,000 total is **over the filter**; exclude.

**Weighing the 2-year permit explicitly, as instructed.** Finland's **2-year,
any-sector post-study residence permit is the best in Europe** — it is
unconditional on field, which matters enormously for a marketing graduate who
cannot rely on a shortage-occupation route. Against that: **Finland costs him
roughly €9,000–€12,000 more in tuition than Norway over two years**, and Finnish
graduate marketing hiring in English is genuinely thin outside Helsinki tech,
with Finnish expected for most client-facing commercial roles. **My verdict:
Finland's permit does not justify the premium for this student.** Norway gives
him a 1-year job-seeker permit at **€5,100/yr** with a **years-based credential
assessment that clears his 146-ECTS problem outright**. He should treat Finland
as a **backup**, not a target — and if he does apply, apply to **UEF or Vaasa**,
never Aalto or Helsinki (selectivity filter). **Finnish UAS remain closed**
(2 years' work experience required) — confirmed, unchanged.
