## 1. Bottom line

Poland is the single best **affordability-plus-accessibility** match found in this scope: English-taught master's at genuinely public universities run **€2,500–5,300/year**, admission is largely non-selective (several are explicitly first-come-first-served or a low-bar entry test rather than GPA-ranked), the entry rule is written as *"the diploma gives access to the next level in the country of issue"* — i.e. **years-of-study logic, not a literal 180-ECTS count** — and cost of living in Kraków/Łódź/Katowice is €550–800/month. Czechia is a solid second: English-taught fees are somewhat higher (€2,700–6,000/year), admission at the economics faculties involves a real entrance exam, but the **zero-tuition-in-Czech rule is real and legally guaranteed to all nationalities** (Higher Education Act; confirmed on studyin.cz). **However, I do not recommend the Czech-language route**: the only credible bridge, Charles University ÚJOP's one-year foundation year, costs **€5,150–7,253 plus a year of his life plus ~€7,000 living costs** — roughly €12,000–14,000 — to save €3,000–5,000/year on a two-year master's. The arithmetic does not work, and it delays graduation by a year. Directions 1 and 2 (AI/analytics for marketing; digital marketing/growth) are **very well served** in both countries. Directions 3 and 4 (music marketing; music business) are **almost entirely absent in English** — I found exactly one English-taught creative-industries master (Katowice film school) and the genuine music-business master's that do exist (JAMU Brno, HAMU Prague, VŠE Arts Management, Akademia Muzyczna Łódź) are **all taught in Czech or Polish and all require a portfolio/project/talent entrance exam**, which he cannot pass with a self-taught hobby and no portfolio. I say this plainly rather than padding.

## 5. Country-level notes

### 5.1 The Czech zero-tuition rule — VERIFIED, and my honest assessment

**Verified on the official government portal studyin.cz** (checked 2026-08-17): *"By law, higher education at public and state institutions is free of charge for citizens of all nationalities."* This applies to **programmes taught in Czech** at public and state institutions, for **all nationalities including non-EU** — there is no EU-citizenship condition. English-taught programmes at the same institutions are paid, ranging **0–22,350 USD/year** nationally; in practice €2,700–6,000/year for the business programmes in this report.

**So the lever is real. Here is why I still advise against pulling it.**

The only credible bridge is a Czech-language foundation year. **Charles University's ÚJOP** (Institute for Language and Preparatory Studies) runs one-year foundation programmes, **three trimesters**, September intakes confirmed for **2026 and 2027**, in six subject streams including **Economics**:
- **COMPLEX** (35 hours/week, 36 weeks, Czech from zero to **B2** plus entrance-exam and university-study preparation): **€7,167–7,253**
- **PROGRESS** (25 hours/week): **€5,150–5,236**
- **Mandatory health insurance via ÚJOP: €611/year**
- ÚJOP claims a **90%+ university acceptance rate** for its completers.
- URLs: https://ujop.cuni.cz/ujopen-69.html · https://ujop.cuni.cz/UJOPEN-465.html

**The arithmetic, stated plainly.** A preparatory year costs roughly **€5,800–7,900 in fees and insurance, plus about €6,000–9,000 in living costs** — call it **€12,000–17,000 all-in** — and it costs **one full year**, pushing graduation from 2029 to 2030. What it buys is the avoidance of **€5,400–9,600** of English-taught tuition over a two-year master's. **The preparatory year costs roughly twice what it saves, and the saving is entirely notional because the cheaper English-taught options (UW Digital Business at €5,000/yr, UTB Zlín at €2,700/yr, Łódź at €2,500/yr) already sit below the threshold that would justify it.**

Two further honesty points: **(a)** ÚJOP's foundation programmes are documented as preparation for university study generally, and the page does not state that they suit **master's**-level applicants specifically — `verify before relying on it`; and **(b)** even after B2, sitting a Czech-language economics entrance exam at VŠE (≥100 points, ≥50% per subject) against native speakers is a materially different proposition from studying in English.

**Recommendation: classify the Czech-language route Tier C and do not pursue it.** The saving does not justify the year. Czech should be learned *after* arrival, for employability (§5.4), not before it, for tuition.

**Note the asymmetry with Poland:** Poland has **no** equivalent rule. Under Art. 324 of the Law on Higher Education and Science (20 July 2018), Polish-taught full-time studies are free only for the categories listed in **Art. 324(2)** — Karta Polaka holders, permanent residents, NAWA scholarship holders and similar. A Tunisian national with no such status **pays full fees even for Polish-taught programmes**, though a **rector may grant a discretionary waiver by administrative decision**. So *"learn Polish and study free"* is **not** available to him the way *"learn Czech and study free"* technically is.

### 5.2 Language tiers, summarised

- **Tier A (ready now):** every English-taught programme in §3 — the great majority of this report. **No French-taught master's was found in either country**, so French confers no exemption here; he will need an English certificate for most programmes.
- **Important English-requirement finds, in ascending order of pain:** **University of Warsaw Digital Business** accepts **its own free online B2 test** — no external exam at all. **WSB Merito** will confirm B2 **via a Skype conversation**. **SGH** accepts **IELTS 5.5 / TOEFL iBT 72** — remarkably low. **Kozminski** is the outlier at **IELTS 6.5 / TOEFL 87**. Given "working English, no test taken", the first three are the pragmatic targets.
- **Tier C (Czech or Polish):** VŠE Arts Management, JAMU, HAMU, Akademia Muzyczna Łódź, Jagiellonian's Polish-taught *Zarządzanie kulturą i mediami* and *Zarządzanie mediami i reklamą* (JU confirms these are Polish-only and *"knowledge of Polish… will be verified upon application"*). **Both Czech and Polish are West/Slavic languages with complex case systems and no Romance cognate base — for a French/Arabic speaker these fall firmly in the "harder than German", not the "faster than Spanish/Italian" category.** Realistic time to certified B2: **18–24+ months**, not 6–12.
- **Free/subsidised language provision:** Polish and Czech universities commonly include free or cheap Polish/Czech-for-foreigners modules *for enrolled students*. That is the sensible way in — enrol on an English-taught degree, take the free language module for two years, and arrive in the job market with a working local language. `Specific per-institution offerings NOT VERIFIED.`

### 5.3 Entry / recognition stance — POLAND

**Confirmed on the official portal study.gov.pl** (checked 2026-08-17), exact wording:

> *"If a diploma confirming completion of studies abroad gives access to the next level of higher education or entitles its holder to apply for doctoral degree in the country of issue, also in Poland it gives the right to access education at the corresponding level (second-cycle studies, doctoral school)."*

**This is the Lisbon Recognition Convention access principle in operation, and it is exactly the right rule for this student:** the test is *does the diploma open the next level at home*, not *does the transcript print 180*. A Tunisian *licence* confers access to the *Mastère* in Tunisia; that is the fact to evidence.

Three concrete corrections and additions to the Phase-1 picture:

1. **Poland does NOT mention the Lisbon Convention on the education-recognition page** — the portal invokes it only in the context of *scientific degrees and habilitation*. The access principle above is nonetheless stated in Polish law in its own right. **Do not cite "Lisbon" at a Polish admissions office; cite the access-to-next-level rule.**
2. **NAWA confirms nostrification is NOT required for master's entry:** *"Nostrification is not required in order to commence education on second cycle studies."* And **NAWA recognition statements on higher-education diplomas are issued FREE OF CHARGE.** This compares extremely favourably with **ENIC-NARIC France at €120 and 4–6 months** (Phase 1).
3. **But there is a country-list catch.** The University of Warsaw's rule: *"Candidates whose higher education diploma was issued **outside** the education system of a EU, EFTA or OECD member state… are required to obtain the **NAWA's Director individual recognition statement**."* **Tunisia is not in the EU, EFTA or the OECD.** So at UW — and probably at other large public universities — he needs the free NAWA statement **in addition to** the access document: *"a document (information) stating that the certificate / diploma entitles to undertake or continue university-level study in country in which the diploma was issued."*
   - **Practical consequence: start the NAWA recognition statement early — it is free, but it is a queue.** Apply well before the application deadline of whichever programme he targets.
   - **`NOT VERIFIED`: whether a Poland–Tunisia bilateral education agreement exists.** The portal does not publish the country list and directs enquiries to NAWA. **Check NAWA's KWALIFIKATOR system (https://nawa.gov.pl/en/recognition/system-kwalifikator) for the Tunisian *licence* specifically — if an agreement exists, the NAWA statement is unnecessary.** This is a concrete, cheap next action and I could not complete it within budget.
   - Note the smaller institutions (WSB Merito, UE Katowice, Łódź, UTB Zlín) publish **no such requirement** — they ask for a legalised/apostilled diploma and supplement plus translations. The NAWA burden appears to be a large-public-university practice, not a national universal.

**Documents to prepare either way:** legalised or **apostilled** bachelor's diploma; **diploma supplement / *supplément au diplôme***; **sworn translation into English or Polish**; and the **FSEG attestation** (6 semesters / 3 years / 180 credits / confers access to Mastère) to pre-empt the 146 question. UE Katowice explicitly demands *"legalized/apostilled"* diploma **and** supplement with sworn translation.

**146 ECTS verdict across Poland: LIKELY OK almost everywhere.** Not one Polish programme in this report publishes a numeric ECTS threshold except MERCURI. The wording is uniformly level-based ("a Bachelor's diploma in any field", "Bachelor's or Master's degree holders may apply", "a first degree (Bachelor's or equivalent)").

### 5.4 Entry / recognition stance — CZECHIA

**Confirmed on studyin.cz:** full **nostrification is NOT required for admission**. Universities holding **institutional accreditation** may recognise foreign prior education **as part of their own admission process**; that recognition is *"valid only for the particular university and faculty"*. Standalone recognition through a Rector's Office is a separate, optional procedure: **30 days by law, extendable to 60** in complex cases.

- **UTB Zlín publishes an explicit price for this: CZK 990 (~€39) "recognition fee for foreign education"**, alongside the CZK 740 application fee. That is the whole cost — a fraction of France's €120/4–6 months.
- **Cheapest and fastest recognition regime found in this entire scope.** Czechia's admissions-integrated recognition is a genuine, underrated advantage.
- **146 ECTS verdict across Czechia: LIKELY OK**, with one caveat — **VŠE Prague explicitly requires the "diploma supplement or final transcript"**, so the 146 figure will be visible to an assessor. Bring the FSEG attestation. Masaryk and UTB Zlín both use purely level-based or subject-based wording.
- Note the direct-use exception (Slovakia, Poland, Hungary, Slovenia) does **not** cover Tunisia.

### 5.5 Post-study work rights — and a candid read

**POLAND — temporary residence permit for a graduate seeking work or planning to start a business**
- **Duration: 9 months.** **Once only, and only immediately after graduating** from a Polish university.
- Application is **electronic only, via the MOS portal**, no later than the last day of legal stay. Requires diploma, health insurance, proof of accommodation, and funds for 9 months plus a return trip. **Legal changes take effect 27 April 2026** — re-check the rules nearer the time.
- **Critical wording to understand:** *"This permit does not allow work in Poland"* — the permit itself is not a work authorisation. **But separately:** *"If you are a graduate of full-time studies at a Polish university, you may work without a work permit."* So a **full-time** Polish graduate can work; **the full-time mode of study matters and should be treated as a hard requirement** when choosing between full-time and part-time/online variants (relevant to Kozminski and Lazarski).
- Sources: https://www.gov.pl/web/udsc-en/permit-for-temporary-residence--graduate · https://mos.cudzoziemcy.gov.pl/en/informacje/absolwent_EN/zlozenie_EN

**CZECHIA — long-term residence permit for the purpose of seeking employment or starting a business**
- **Up to 9 months, with NO possibility of extension.**
- **Materially better in one respect:** holders who completed university studies have *"free access to the labour market and you can work, do business, seek employment or start a business without limitations."* Unlike Poland's, the Czech permit **is itself a work authorisation**.
- Apply **in person** at a Ministry of the Interior office, **before** the study permit expires. **Fee CZK 2,500. Processing 60 days.** Then transition to an **Employee Card**, which is the route to long-term residence.
- Source: https://ipc.gov.cz/en/visa-and-residence-permit-types/third-country-nationals/long-term-residence-permits/long-term-residence-permit-for-the-purpose-of-seeking-employment-or-starting-a-business/

**The candid part.** Nine months is short, and in both countries **the graduate marketing job market runs on the local language.** Warsaw, Kraków and Prague have large English-speaking employment pools — but they are concentrated in **shared-service centres, BPO/SSC, IT outsourcing and multinational back offices**, not in the client-facing brand and agency roles he is aiming at. A **digital marketing, CRM or marketing-analytics role in an international SSC is realistically attainable in English**; a **brand-management or agency role at a Polish or Czech company is not, without Polish or Czech.** His **French is a genuine and underrated asset here** — Warsaw, Kraków, Brno and Prague all host French-language customer-service and shared-service operations that recruit specifically for French speakers, and that is a plausible first-job wedge. **Recommendation: take the free university language course from day one and treat B1 local language as a graduation requirement he sets himself.** His analytics/CRM/data skills are the part of his profile that travels without language; the pure brand-marketing ambition does not.
