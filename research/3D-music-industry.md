# 3D — Music Marketing / Artist Promotion (Direction 3) & Music Business / Music Industry Management (Direction 4)
### Europe-wide. Agent 3D. All checks performed **2026-08-17** unless stated.

---

## 1. Bottom line

The affordable, visa-viable music-industry master's in Europe is **almost entirely a French-language, French-public-university phenomenon**, plus one outstanding English-taught Italian option. Every well-known specialist music school this brief named — Popakademie, BIMM, Catalyst Berlin, SAE, ICMP, Berklee Valencia, Saint Louis Rome, UB/IL3 Barcelona, UC3M Madrid — **fails at least one hard filter**, and most fail on accreditation, price, or both: several award a *título propio* / *Master di I livello* / UK-validated diploma rather than a state-recognised master's of the host country, and the UK ones sit at £16,000–£31,900. Of everything I verified, exactly **three programmes clear all filters**: **Bologna GIOCA** (English, 120 ECTS *laurea magistrale*, ~€157–4,080/yr, explicitly accepts "a Bachelor's… **in any discipline**… of minimum three-year duration" — the single best 146-ECTS-proof wording I found anywhere), **ULB Master en gestion culturelle** (French, 120 ECTS, €5,369/yr all-in for non-EU), and the **UVSQ MOCA** management route in France (French, ~€250–3,879/yr, and its official *licences conseillées* list literally includes "Gestion"). My candid verdict on the career question is in §7 and it is **negative**: this direction is materially harder to convert into a work visa than the data/marketing directions, and I would not advise it as the primary track.

## 6. ⚠ Two France-specific traps the country agents will miss

**(a) The *alternance* trap.** The most prestigious and most practical French music/culture master's are heavily or wholly apprenticeship-based:
- Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne M2 Économie de la culture et numérique: **"100% des étudiants en alternance"**, described as *"une formation professionnalisante en alternance"*.
- Paris 8 M2 Industrie de la musique: **"between half and three-quarters of the student body"** on apprenticeship or professionalisation contracts.
An *alternance* place requires the student to **personally secure an employment contract with a French company before or at enrolment**. A Tunisian applicant abroad, with no French network, no portfolio and no music-industry contacts, is at a severe structural disadvantage — and failing to find a contract can mean losing the place. **Any French music master's advertised as alternance-heavy should be treated as high-risk for him.**

**(b) The application-platform trap.** Every French programme page quotes **Mon Master** (M1) or **eCandidat** (M2) dates. **A Tunisian resident does not use these.** He must go through **Campus France Tunisie / "Études en France"** (offices in Tunis, Sousse — his city — and Sfax), which runs a *much earlier* campaign with a compulsory interview and a file fee. Campus France Tunisie's own site references a *"Calendrier des procédures"* page and confirms Mon Master applies only to *"candidats concernés"*. **Anyone reading Mon Master's February–March window as his deadline will miss the real one by months.** `NOT VERIFIED — check directly`: https://www.tunisie.campusfrance.org/

## 8. CANDID VERDICT — is music a visa-viable career path for him?

**No — not as the primary track. I would advise against it, and here is the evidence rather than the intuition.**

**1. He fails the sector's entry currency before he even applies.** Across the programmes I verified, the recurring admission demands are: *"some experience of working in the music industry"* (Westminster), *"at least six months of practical experience"* proven by certificates or project reports (Popakademie), *"une pratique ou une culture musicale manifeste"* (Sorbonne Nouvelle), *80 credits in performing music/musicology* (Agder), a conservatory diploma (Saint Louis). **He is a self-taught hobbyist with no portfolio.** Only GIOCA, York and the general French management routes are indifferent to this — and York costs £31,900. The sector's admissions gate is *demonstrated involvement*, and he cannot demonstrate any.

**2. The employer structure cannot sponsor him.** Music-industry employers in Europe are overwhelmingly **micro-enterprises**: independent labels, artist-management companies, booking agencies, festivals, promoters — typically single-digit headcount. Visa sponsorship requires an employer with the administrative capacity, legal appetite and continuity to file for a third-country national. Micro-firms in a low-margin creative sector are the **worst possible** sponsor profile. Contrast the destination roles the French programmes themselves advertise — *chargé de production, chargé de diffusion, relations presse, chargé de développement culturel* — these are junior, often project-funded, frequently on French *CDD* or *intermittent* contracts. **A fixed-term contract at a five-person label is not a foundation for a residence permit.**

**3. A concrete, verified number that makes the point.** France's **"passeport talent — salarié qualifié"**, the standard graduate-to-worker route for a master's holder, requires an employment contract with a **minimum gross salary of €39,582** (service-public.gouv.fr, checked 2026-08-17). Entry-level cultural-production salaries in France are nowhere near that figure. There is an ordinary *changement de statut* route that does not carry that threshold and that exempts French-master's graduates from the *opposabilité de la situation de l'emploi* — so the €39,582 is not an absolute bar — **but it does establish that the premium, low-friction immigration channel is closed to music-sector salaries, and the fallback channel still requires a small employer to file paperwork for him.** In data/marketing, that same €39,582 is a reachable number at a mid-size company with an HR department that has done this before.

**4. The market prices the music label itself.** The cleanest evidence I found is internal to one institution: **University of Agder charges NOK 135,000/yr for the Popular Music: Music Business master's and NOK 63,000/yr for its International Business master's.** Same country, same university, same degree level, same English medium — **the music-branded version costs 2.1× more**, and UiA offers no scholarships. He would pay a large premium for a credential that narrows, rather than widens, his employer pool.

**5. What I recommend instead.** His CRM platform — a built, working system for retention, tracking and automated reminders — is a **data/marketing asset, and it is worthless as a music credential.** The rational structure is:
- **Primary track: Directions 1 and 2** (AI/data-applied marketing, digital/growth marketing), where the CRM project is the centrepiece of his file and where employers are large enough to sponsor.
- **Music as sector, not as degree.** If he wants the music industry, the realistic route is a *general* marketing/data master's followed by applying his skills to music companies — labels and streaming platforms hire CRM, growth and audience-data people, and those are exactly the roles that a music-business MA does *not* train for.
- **The one exception worth pursuing: GIOCA at Bologna.** It is essentially free, it is a real state degree from a management department, it accepts any three-year bachelor in any discipline, it needs no portfolio, and it reserves 25 places for non-EU applicants. It is a *cultural and creative industries management* degree, not a music degree — which is precisely why it keeps his options open. **If he wants one foot in this direction at acceptable risk, GIOCA is the only programme I found that I would put on a shortlist without a caveat.**
- **Prerequisite for that: sit IELTS or TOEFL.** It is the single binding constraint on his best option in this direction.
