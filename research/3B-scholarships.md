## 1. BOTTOM LINE

Funding for this student is **narrower than it looks, and the highest-value schemes are Tunisian, not European.** The single best instrument by a wide margin is the **Tunisian MESRS "bourse spécifique" for mastère abroad** (≈€800/month in the EU — €600 grant + €200 soft loan — *plus* tuition, social cover, health insurance, study-permit fees and the flight), because it is the only scheme that covers cost of living *and* tuition *and* is open to a fresh licence graduate with no work experience; its published academic floor is exactly 12/20, which he meets, though it is awarded by national competition among "lauréats". Second-best in reliability is **Stipendium Hungaricum** (Tunisia holds a 250-place national quota, Economic Science is an eligible field, tuition-free + free dorm), whose weakness is a stipend of only ~€110/month. Third, and the biggest single-shot prize, is the **Eiffel scholarship** (€1,200/month + travel + insurance + *de plein droit* tuition exemption, worth ≈€3,950/yr on top) — but it is nominated **by the French institution, never by the student**, so it must be *requested from French universities between September and November 2026*; on a 12–13/20 profile it is a long-shot. Two widely-assumed options are **verified closed**: the **Islamic Development Bank** master's scholarship is restricted to its 20 *Least Developed* Member Countries and Tunisia is not one, and the **Aga Khan Foundation ISP** does not operate in Tunisia. Most critically for the project: **Austria, now the top-ranked country, has effectively no national scholarship for a Tunisian doing a full master's** — the Ernst Mach FH grant explicitly refuses to fund a full degree, and the one programme that did (Erhard Busek, Tunisia eligible, €1,300/month) has been suspended with "no further call planned". Finally, a **correction to an earlier finding: Poland *does* have a Tunisia-eligible scheme** — NAWA Banach, tuition exemption + stipend, no field restriction.

### 3.7 — France Excellence **Eiffel** ⭐ **BIGGEST SINGLE PRIZE** — **institutions deposit by ~8 January 2027; campaign opens ~1 October 2026**
> **He must act in September–November 2026, months before the deadline that matters.**

- **Awarding body:** Ministère de l'Europe et des Affaires étrangères, operated by Campus France.
- **WHO APPLIES — verified, and this is the whole trap:** **the French institution, never the student.** From the official *Guide de candidatures Eiffel* (§1.5): *"Seules les candidatures transmises par les établissements français d'enseignement supérieur et de recherche sont recevables… Les candidatures transmises par d'autres voies ne sont pas recevables."* And from the official Eiffel page: *"Les candidatures envoyées directement par les étudiants ou transmises par des établissements étrangers seront déclarées inéligibles."* Campus France's own instruction to students is a two-step:
  1. contact **Campus France Tunisie / the SCAC of the French Embassy in Tunis** for orientation;
  2. **contact the *service des Relations internationales* of each French institution he is applying to, by email or phone, to ask their internal Eiffel procedure and their internal deadline** — every school sets its own, typically **October–November**, i.e. *earlier* than the Campus France date.
  Then the institution pre-selects him and deposits the file on `campusfrance.smapply.io`. **A candidate presented by more than one institution is rejected outright** — so he must pick one champion school, not scattergun.
- **Tunisian eligible?** Yes — open to any foreign national; **Franco-Tunisian dual nationals are excluded**.
- **Field eligible?** **Yes — "Economie Gestion" is one of the seven eligible domains** (the SHS field comprises: Histoire/langue/civilisation française; Droit et science politique; **Economie Gestion**). Marketing sits inside Economie Gestion.
- **Other hard conditions:**
  - **Age: 29 or under** at selection (for the 2026 round: born after 31 March 1996). Fine for him.
  - **Must be residing abroad** — candidates already studying in France are ineligible at master's level.
  - Only **state-accredited** French qualifications conferring the *grade de master*. For an **MSc**, only **CGE-labelled** MScs qualify, and the accreditation number must be attached — this bites on private business schools.
  - **Distance/online and délocalisé programmes are excluded**; at least 50% of the course must be in France.
  - **A rejected Eiffel candidate can never re-apply at the same level, even via a different school or field.** One shot only. Do not burn it on a weak nomination.
  - Holding **any other French government scholarship at the time of application makes him ineligible**.
- **Covers:** **€1,200/month** at master's level (rate from January 2026), **plus direct payment of** international transport, national transport, insurance, housing search, cultural activities. **Duration: up to 24 months for an M1 entry**, 12 months for M2 entry.
  **Tuition is NOT paid by Eiffel — but as a *boursier du gouvernement français* he is exempt from the differentiated fees *de plein droit*, outside any quota**, per the established finding. Net effect ≈ **€1,200 × 24 months + ≈€3,950/yr of waived fees.**
- **Language:** the institution must certify his level matches the course. As a native French speaker on a French-taught master's, no test issue — this is one of the few schemes where his no-English-test status is irrelevant.
- **Documents:** compiled by the institution — the online Master form, plus mandatory annexes (transcripts, degree, CV, the school's statement of the *criteria by which he was pre-selected among all admitted foreign students*).
- **Verdict: LONG-SHOT on merit, but MANDATORY to attempt.** It is described in its own guide as *"un programme d'excellence très sélectif"*, and each school must justify why it picked him over every other admitted international student — a 12–13/20 average is a weak card. His **CRM platform final-year project** is the only asset likely to move a selection committee and must be the centre of the pitch. Because it costs him nothing but emails, and because it is worth ~€32,800 over two years, expected value is high even at low probability.
- **URLs:** https://www.campusfrance.org/fr/la-bourse-france-excellence-eiffel · guide: https://www.campusfrance.org/system/files/medias/documents/2025-09/guide_depot_candidatures_eiffel_2026.pdf · FAQ: https://www.campusfrance.org/fr/faq-appel-a-candidature-a-la-bourse-eiffel · contact: candidatures.eiffel@campusfrance.org · checked 2026-08-17
- **Date note:** the **2026** campaign ran **1 Oct 2025 → 8 Jan 2026**. Expect **1 Oct 2026 → ~8 Jan 2027** for the autumn-2027 intake. `2027 dates NOT YET PUBLISHED — the 2026 dates are given as an indication.`

### 3.8 — **Stipendium Hungaricum** (Hungary) ⭐ **MOST WINNABLE** — **~15 January 2027, 14:00 CET (2026/27 deadline was 15 Jan 2026, 14:00)**
- **Awarding body:** Hungarian Government / **Tempus Public Foundation (TPF)**, under a bilateral education agreement with the Tunisian ministry.
- **Tunisian eligible? — CONFIRMED, in the official 2026/27 Call PDF.** *"Tunisian Republic"* appears on **both** lists: full-degree (full-time) programmes **and** partial/exchange programmes. Business/management/marketing master's sit inside Economic Science. *Which specific Hungarian programmes are open to Tunisia in 2027/28 is set by the annual call* — `check the Study Finder when the 2027/28 call opens`.

- **Verdict: REALISTIC — the single most winnable full scheme on this list, conditional on an English test.** 250 places for Tunisia, a field he qualifies for, no work-experience requirement, no age problem, and selection that weighs the sending partner's nomination heavily. The cost is a thin stipend.

### 3.11 — **Erhard Busek Grant (OeAD, Austria)** — **SUSPENDED — MONITOR** — Tunisia explicitly eligible
> This is Austria's *only* full-degree master's scholarship for his profile, and it is currently switched off.

- **Awarding body:** OeAD, funded by BMFWF.
- **Purpose:** *"to enable highly qualified students from non-European developing countries to complete a **full two-year Master's programme** in Austria"*, with emphasis on STEM **or English-taught** master's programmes and on integrating graduates into the Austrian labour market.
- **Tunisian eligible? — YES, explicitly.** Countries of origin: Afghanistan; Algeria; Bangladesh; Cabo Verde; Egypt; Côte d'Ivoire; Gambia; Ghana; India; Indonesia; Kenya; Kyrgyzstan; Libya; Mauritania; Morocco; Nepal; Nigeria; Pakistan; Philippines; Rwanda; Senegal; Somalia; Sri Lanka; Sudan; South Sudan; Chad; **Tunisia**; Uganda; Uzbekistan; Vietnam.
- **Covers:** **€1,300/month for 24 months** + one-off travel allowance up to €1,200. Tuition is not paid by the grant, **but Busek holders are exempted from tuition by the admitting institution.** Age limit 35.

## 5. STACKING AND STRATEGY NOTES

1. **The English test is the real gatekeeper, not the GPA.** Hungary, Poland and Italy's English track all require a certificate at the host institution's minimum (typically B2). Nothing else on this list is blocked by a 12–13/20 average as decisively as everything is blocked by having no English certificate. **Booking an IELTS/TOEFL for late 2026 unlocks three of the four realistic schemes.**
2. **France is the exception where his native French is a competitive asset** — Eiffel is judged on the file, not a language test, and a French-taught master's needs no certificate. That is an argument for a French-focused Eiffel push in parallel with the Hungarian/Polish applications.
3. **Do not stack French government scholarships.** The Eiffel guide is explicit: holding **any other French government scholarship at the moment of application makes an Eiffel candidate ineligible** — so he cannot apply for IsDB-France and Eiffel in a way that has him holding both.
4. **The Tunisian bourse and the Tunisian loan are cumulative with each other**, and the loan is means-tested rather than merit-tested, so it is the sensible backstop if the bourse competition goes against him.
5. **The Hungarian package is thin on cash.** €110 stipend + €100 accommodation ≈ €210/month against Hungarian living costs. If he wins Stipendium Hungaricum he should still pursue the **Tunisian prêt universitaire** to top it up — but check the SH Call's §5.3.3 *"Other Scholarships and Double Financing"* rules first, which restrict combining SH with other scholarships.
6. **Sequencing, if he is willing to wait:** Chevening (UK) and DAAD EPOS (Germany) both become genuinely available after **two years of full-time work in Tunisian marketing**. Both are fully funded and neither weighs a 12–13/20 licence average heavily. A 2029 intake with two years of CRM/digital-marketing experience behind him is a **materially stronger funded proposition** than a 2027 intake. This should be presented as a real alternative, not a consolation.
