**Agent 3E · checked 2026-08-17 · target intake autumn 2027 (with autumn 2028 fallback)**

---

## 1. BOTTOM LINE

Tier B is **real but slower than the brief hopes**; Tier C is **mostly fantasy for a 2027 intake**.
Only three genuine pathway mechanisms survive verification: (a) **Germany's statutory
conditional-admission route** — §16b(1) sent. 3 no. 1 AufenthG puts a study-preparatory
language course *inside* the student residence permit when a university has admitted the
student on condition he takes it (this is the single best-designed integrated route in
Europe, but Phase 1 already rates Germany closed on GPA/180 ECTS, so it is a route with no
door); (b) **Czechia's ÚJOP year** — a 10-month, 25 h/week Czech course at Charles
University, €4,807, explicitly visa-qualifying, feeding free Czech-taught master's — but
Czech is the slowest language on the list and the degree's international marketing value is
low; (c) **Italy's and Spain's language-course study visas**, both confirmed to exist but
both *standalone* — no progression guarantee, no work rights in Spain, and the Italian one
is barred from free/public courses by rule. **Poland's preparatory-course route is
effectively closed to him** (entry visa restricted by Council of Ministers regulation to
Belarusian citizens) and Poland's 2025 Visa Act now *obliges* universities to verify B2 in
the language of instruction — which converts Poland's English-taught programmes from "no
test needed" to "certificate required." On timing: from a French base, **Spanish or Italian
to certified B2 takes ~9–11 months part-time or ~5 months full-time; German ~20–24 months
part-time; Czech/Polish 2.5–3 years part-time.** My recommendation is **Spanish, started
now, aimed at autumn 2028 — not 2027** — with the 2027 cycle run entirely on Tier A
(French/English), and an **IELTS booking in Sousse as the single highest-return action he
can take this month.**

## 3. THE PATHWAY ROUTES, RANKED BY REAL VIABILITY

### ROUTE 1 — GERMANY: conditional admission with an integrated study-preparatory language course
**Status: legally the best-designed route in Europe. Practically blocked for THIS student.**

Verbatim from **§16b(1) AufenthG** (gesetze-im-internet.de, checked 2026-08-17):

> "Der Aufenthaltszweck des Studiums umfasst auch studienvorbereitende Maßnahmen […]
> Studienvorbereitende Maßnahmen sind 1. der Besuch eines studienvorbereitenden
> Sprachkurses, **wenn der Ausländer zu einem Vollzeitstudium zugelassen worden ist und die
> Zulassung an den Besuch eines studienvorbereitenden Sprachkurses gebunden ist**…"

**What this means, precisely:** if a German university issues a *conditional* master's
admission whose condition is attendance at a study-preparatory German course, the student
gets a **full student residence permit (2 years standard, §16b(2))** covering the language
phase — 140 work-days/year permitted (§16b(3)) — and no separate language visa is needed.
The same paragraph adds that language proof is only demanded "wenn diese Sprachkenntnisse
weder bei der Zulassungsentscheidung geprüft worden sind noch **durch die
studienvorbereitende Maßnahme erworben werden sollen**" — i.e. the requirement is waived
at visa stage precisely because the course will deliver it.

**Fallback within Germany — §16b(5) no. 2:** a permit *may* be granted ("kann") to someone
accepted onto a study-preparatory language course **with no university admission at all**.
This is discretionary, not a right, and is the weaker branch.

**§16f AufenthG (general language courses, NOT study-preparatory):** discretionary permit;
notable that it now allows employment up to 20 h/week. But it is explicitly for courses
"die nicht der Studienvorbereitung dienen" — the wrong instrument here.

**Studienkolleg / Propädeutikum:** Phase 1's finding stands and is reinforced by the statute
— §16b(1) sent. 3 no. 2 treats Studienkolleg as a separate preparatory measure, and Phase 1
confirmed via DAAD/uni-assist that it is bachelor-entry only. **A Propädeutikum carries no
ECTS and cannot repair an ECTS deficit — it is language/subject revision only.** Do not
propose either.

**Verdict:** `ROUTE EXISTS AND IS STRONG — BUT DOOR IS SHUT.` Phase 1 rates Germany closed
on the 180-ECTS + ~2.5 GPA thresholds. The route is only usable if a German university
issues a conditional admission, and a 12–13/20 applicant is unlikely to get one. **Record it,
do not build on it.** German is also the slowest of the "reachable" languages (§4).

### ROUTE 2 — CZECHIA: ÚJOP language year → free Czech-taught master's
**Status: fully verified, genuinely viable, but poor return on 12+ months of effort.**

Charles University **Institute for Language and Preparatory Studies (ÚJOP)**
(ujop.cuni.cz/UJOPEN-43.html, checked 2026-08-17):

| Course | Duration | Intensity | Price | Visa-qualifying? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-semester Czech | 10 months (Sept start) | 25 h/week, Mon–Fri 09:00–13:25 | **CZK 112,000 ≈ €4,807** | **✓ Yes** (visa deadline 31 Mar) |
| One-semester Czech | 5 months (Sept or Jan) | 25 h/week | CZK 58,000 ≈ €2,489 | ✓ Yes (visa deadline 31 Aug) |
| 8-week intensive | 8 weeks | 25 h/week | CZK 28,200 ≈ €1,210 | (short-stay) |
| Evening courses | 3–5 months | 4–6 lessons/week | CZK 12,500 ≈ €536 | **✗ NOT acceptable for visa** |

**Foundation Programmes in Czech** (ujop.cuni.cz/ujopen-69.html): three trimesters,
25 h/week ("PROGRESS") or 35 h/week ("COMPLEX"), **CZK 120,000–169,000 ≈ €5,150–7,253**,
entry "Czech from 0" or "Czech from B1", claimed **>90% university-acceptance rate**. The
page does **not** state whether it serves master's applicants or only bachelor entry, and
does **not** guarantee admission — `BORDERLINE, email studujop@ujop.cuni.cz`.

**Visa status — VERIFIED:** Czechia issues a **long-term visa for the purpose of studies**
covering accredited language/preparatory courses, max validity 1 year including extensions
(ipc.gov.cz; mzv.gov.cz). Proof of funds ≈ **CZK 124,500 for the year**. ÚJOP explicitly
marks which of its courses qualify. **This is one of the few countries where a
language-only year unambiguously grants a student visa.**

**Progression:** *facilitated, not guaranteed.* ÚJOP is Charles University's own institute
and its exams are the recognised Czech-language proof (only ALTE-certified Czech exam
provider), but admission to any master's is a separate competitive process.

**The prize:** Czech-taught study at a Czech **public** university is **tuition-free**
regardless of nationality. So the arithmetic is €4,807 + one year → potentially €0/year
master's.

**The problem:** Czech is a Category-III language (§4) — 1,100+ guided hours to B2. ÚJOP's
10-month course delivers ~900 hours, so B2 in one year is *possible* but demanding, and a
Czech-taught marketing master's has weak international currency for a career in digital
marketing/CRM. **Verdict: `VIABLE BUT LOW ROI`. Keep as a cost-of-last-resort option.**

### ROUTE 3 — ITALY: language-course study visa → very low public tuition
**Status: visa verified; no progression guarantee; a rule that bites.**

**Visa — VERIFIED** (Italian consular network, consrecife.esteri.it, checked 2026-08-17).
Italy issues a **visto per studio** for an Italian language course, subject to:
- the course must be **"di tipo superiore e di almeno 20 ore settimanali"** (≥20 h/week);
- **"Il corso non può essere gratuito o fornito da una scuola pubblica in Italia"** —
  **free courses and Italian public-school courses are EXCLUDED.** This is the rule that
  bites: he cannot use a free university CLA course as his visa basis; he must pay a
  private/recognised school.
- acceptance letter with fixed start/end dates, proof of means (Tabella A), insurance,
  return flight reservation, all documents **in hard copy**.

**Universitaly** — the official MUR pre-enrolment portal — carries a dedicated
*"Corsi di Lingua Italiana per Studenti"* section, and states that the fixed visa deadlines
apply only to *corsi ordinamentali* (bachelor/master/single-cycle); **"For all other course
types (Masters, Doctorates, Erasmus, individual courses, and foundation courses) there are
no time limits."** Practical effect: a language-course pre-enrolment is not locked to the
30 Sept / 30 Nov calendar.

**Degree-side requirement:** B2 Italian, evidenced through Universitaly by a recognised
certificate. Universities may also run their own Italian test — **exact per-university
policy `NOT VERIFIED`, and it belongs to Agents 3A–3D's programme sheets.**

**Why Italy is still attractive:** public-university fees are set by **ISEE / ISEE
parificato**, so a low-income non-EU family frequently pays only the regional tax
(~€140–160) plus a small contribution; and regional **DSU** grants (worth several thousand
euro plus free tuition and canteen) are genuinely won by non-EU students. **Figures are
`NOT VERIFIED` here — they are 3A–3D scope — but the mechanism is what makes Italy the
cheapest Tier B destination in Europe.**

**Verdict: `VIABLE`. Second-best Tier B route. Language cost ≈ same as Spanish (§4).**

### ROUTE 4 — SPAIN: *estancia por estudios* covering a language course
**Status: exists under the new Reglamento, but with two real restrictions.**

Under **RD 1155/2024** (in force **20 May 2025**, replacing RD 557/2011), the authorisation
was renamed *"estancia para estudios, movilidad de alumnos, prácticas o voluntariado"* and
is developed by **Instrucciones SEM 3/2025**. Secondary specialist analysis
(expertoextranjeria.es, checked 2026-08-17) states:

- Language courses **do qualify**, but only at **"CENTROS OFICIALES reconocidos por el
  Ministerio de Educación o por las Consejerías de Educación"** — e.g. Instituto Cervantes
  or a recognised centre. A private academy is not automatically sufficient.
- The programme must be **full-time** (exact weekly-hour threshold not stated —
  `NOT VERIFIED`).
- Means: **€600 per month** of study.
- **CRITICAL: language courses are NOT in the list of study types that carry work rights.**
  Work permission attaches only to higher education, official professional training and
  technical-certification programmes. **A Spanish language year is a year of pure outgoing
  cost with no earning permitted.**

I retrieved the official SEM 3/2025 PDF but its substantive text was not machine-extractable;
the above is therefore **`PARTIALLY VERIFIED — confirm against SEM 3/2025 and RD 1155/2024
Título V before relying on it`** (inclusion.gob.es; boe.es BOE-A-2024-24099).

**Verdict: `VIABLE BUT EXPENSIVE`.** For Spain the smarter play is **not** to spend a year
in Spain learning Spanish — it is to reach DELE/SIELE B2 **in Tunisia** (cheap, §5.1) and
apply straight into the master's. That is the plan I recommend in §7.

### ROUTE 5 — POLAND: preparatory course. **EFFECTIVELY CLOSED TO A TUNISIAN.**
**Status: this is the most important negative finding in my scope.**

- **Art. 144(2) of the Foreigners Act** does provide a temporary residence permit for a
  foreigner taking a **preparatory course to study in Polish**, and migrant.info.pl (the
  state-funded information service) confirms it.
- **BUT** the entry instrument is restricted. UDSC's own guidance states a national visa
  with the "student" annotation, and the corresponding TRC, may be issued for a preparatory
  course **only to citizens of countries specified in a regulation of the Council of
  Ministers**, and that **at present this is limited to citizens of the Republic of
  Belarus.** A Tunisian therefore **cannot enter Poland for a preparatory year.**
  `VERIFIED via udsc.gov.pl guidance text as returned by search; the live UDSC/MOS pages
  returned 502/503 on direct fetch — RE-CONFIRM at mos.cudzoziemcy.gov.pl before discarding
  Poland entirely.`
- **Second, larger Poland finding — affects Tier A, not just Tier B.** The **Visa Act
  enacted 4 April 2025** inserted **arts. 5e and 5f**: *"Uczelnia dokonuje weryfikacji […]
  na podstawie dokumentu poświadczającego znajomość języka – na poziomie biegłości nie
  niższym niż B2."* Every Polish university is now **legally obliged** to verify B2 in the
  language of instruction, documented by certificate, with document types set by
  ministerial regulation. Non-EU applicants with non-automatically-recognised secondary
  documents additionally need **written NAWA confirmation** of eligibility to commence
  studies. A **50% cap** on foreign students per institution was also introduced.
  `SOURCE IS A LANGUAGE SCHOOL'S SUMMARY OF THE ACT — treat as PARTIALLY VERIFIED and
  confirm against the consolidated Prawo o szkolnictwie wyższym.`
  **Consequence for this student: Poland's English-taught programmes are no longer a
  "no test needed" Tier A. He would need a B2 English certificate (IELTS ~6.0–6.5).**
  Flag this to whichever agent covers Poland.

## 4. REALISTIC TIMELINES FROM A FRENCH/ARABIC BASE

### 4.1 The baseline data
Two independent, recognised benchmarks:

**(a) FSI (US Foreign Service Institute) guided-hours categories** — calibrated for *English*
natives, so they are the wrong baseline for him, but they correctly rank *difficulty*:
- Category I (Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, French, Romanian, Dutch, Norwegian, Swedish,
  Danish): ~600–750 hours to professional working proficiency (≈B2/C1)
- Category II (German): ~750–900 hours
- Category III (Czech, Polish, Greek, Turkish, Hebrew): ~1,100 hours
- Category IV (Finnish, Hungarian, Estonian): ~1,100+ hours

**(b) Publisher-set curricula, taken from the actual Tunis institutes** — this is the more
useful number because it is what he would actually sit through:

| Language | Provider (in Tunisia) | Hours to B2 from zero | Cost to B2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | Instituto Cervantes Tunis | A1 60h + A2 120h + B1 120h + B2 180h = **480 hours** | `NOT VERIFIED` (fees page 404) |
| German | Goethe-Institut Tunis | A1 140 UE + A2 140 UE + B1 160 UE + B2 200 UE = **640 UE = 480 clock hours** | 1,240+1,240+1,400+1,840 = **5,720 TND ≈ €1,700** |
| Italian | IIC Tunis | A1–C2 offered, **hours NOT PUBLISHED** | `NOT VERIFIED` |

Note the trap: **Cervantes and Goethe both nominally reach B2 in ~480 class hours, but those
curricula are written for a generic learner.** They are not comparable for *this* learner.

### 4.2 The correction for a French/Arabic base
- **Spanish, Italian, Portuguese**: French is a Romance language. Lexical similarity
  French–Italian ≈ 0.89, French–Spanish ≈ 0.75, French–Portuguese ≈ 0.75. Grammar
  (gendered nouns, three verb conjugation groups, subjunctive, compound past, clitic
  pronouns, agreement) transfers almost wholesale. He should expect to be **placed at A2
  after ~6–8 weeks** and to need roughly **55–70% of the published hours**.
- **German, Dutch**: no Romance transfer. His working English gives him a *small* Germanic
  bridge (word order, some vocabulary), but German's case system, verb-final subordinate
  clauses and separable verbs are all genuinely new. Expect **100–120% of published hours**,
  plus a heavier self-study ratio.
- **Czech, Polish**: seven cases, aspectual verb pairs, consonant clusters, no cognate
  vocabulary. **Expect 1,100+ hours and a much steeper plateau at B1.** His Arabic helps
  slightly with tolerance for morphological complexity — nothing more.
- **Nordic**: Germanic; grammatically simpler than German but phonologically hard and with
  almost no French cognates. ~700–900 hours.

### 4.3 Calendar conversion — the actual answer

Assume **1 hour of class ⇒ 1 hour of self-study** (standard, and required at B1→B2).

| Language | Effective guided hours to B2 (French base) | Total hours incl. self-study | **Part-time (10–12 h/wk total, alongside work)** | **Full-time (25 h/wk class + 10 self, immersion)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Spanish** | 320–380 | 640–760 | **~13–15 months** | **~4.5–5.5 months** |
| **Italian** | 320–400 | 640–800 | **~13–16 months** | **~5–6 months** |
| **Portuguese** | 350–420 | 700–840 | ~14–17 months | ~5–6 months |
| **German** | 550–700 | 1,100–1,400 | **~22–28 months** | **~9–11 months** |
| **Dutch** | 500–650 | 1,000–1,300 | ~20–26 months | ~8–10 months |
| **Norwegian/Swedish/Danish** | 550–700 | 1,100–1,400 | ~22–28 months | ~9–11 months |
| **Czech / Polish** | 900–1,100 | 1,800–2,200 | **~3–3.5 years** | **~14–18 months** (ÚJOP's 10-month, 900-hour course gets a strong student to a shaky B2; most land at B1+) |

**Two honest caveats the brief deserves:**
1. **B2 *certified* ≠ B2 *reached*.** Add **6–10 weeks** for exam preparation to exam
   technique, plus **6–8 weeks** for DELE/CILS results, or ~3 weeks for SIELE. Build this
   into every deadline.
2. **Part-time alongside work at 10 h/week is an assumption that usually fails.** The
   realistic median is 6–8 h/week with holiday gaps. If he cannot commit ~10 h/week
   consistently, add 40–50% to every part-time figure above and the 2027 intake is
   unreachable in any language.

  TOEFL iBT 79–90
- Selective / UK Russell Group: IELTS 6.5–7.0 with 6.0–6.5 in each band
- French *grandes écoles* English-track: often **TOEFL 90 / IELTS 6.5**, some ask TOEIC 850+

### 6.3 Waiver routes — be honest about which are real
**REAL for him:**
- **French-test exemption.** This is the exemption that actually applies. French-medium
  prior education normally exempts a Tunisian applicant from TCF/TEF/DELF at French,
  Belgian, Luxembourgish and Swiss francophone universities, and Campus France Tunisie
  treats French-medium *bac* holders accordingly in practice. **Confirm per institution** —
  a minority still demand a certificate. This is worth several hundred euro and months of
  effort saved, and it is the single strongest language asset he already owns.
- **University-internal English test.** Many continental universities (particularly Italian
  and Spanish public universities, and French business schools' AST route) run their own
  English interview or online test instead of demanding IELTS. Where offered this is free
  and immediate. **Per-programme — belongs on 3A–3D's data sheets.**

**NOT REAL — do not let him believe these:**
- ❌ There is **no general francophone waiver from English testing.** A French-medium
  bachelor's does not exempt anyone from IELTS for an English-taught programme.
- ❌ **Medium-of-Instruction (MOI) letters do not help him** — MOI waivers require the prior
  degree to have been *taught in English*. His was taught in French/Arabic. FSEG Sousse
  cannot issue a usable MOI letter.
- ❌ Poland is no longer a waiver country — see §3 Route 5: B2 verification is now statutory.

### 6.4 Recommendation on English — act this month
**Book IELTS Academic in Sousse now.** It costs ~700 TND, requires no new learning (he has
working English), takes one morning, is valid two years, and it is the **only** thing
standing between him and the entire Tier A English-taught field for autumn 2027. Every
month he delays narrows the 2027 application window. If a diagnostic suggests he is below
6.0, six to eight weeks of focused preparation will normally close that gap — this is a
test-technique problem, not a language-acquisition problem.

## 7. THE DECISIVE JUDGEMENT — ONE LANGUAGE, WHICH ONE, AND WHEN

### 7.1 Recommendation: **SPANISH.**

**The shortlist reduces to Spanish vs Italian.** Everything else is eliminated on evidence:
- *German* — 22–28 months part-time, and Phase 1 already closes Germany on GPA/180 ECTS.
  Spending two years on a language to enter a country that has already rejected the profile
  is the single worst allocation available.
- *Czech/Polish* — 3 years part-time; Poland's prep route is closed to Tunisians and its new
  B2 statute raises the bar; a Czech-taught marketing degree has little international
  currency.
- *Dutch* — the Netherlands is closed (Phase 1).
- *Nordic languages* — Nordic master's are already in English, so the language buys nothing
  at admission, and the countries now charge non-EEA tuition.
- *Portuguese* — near-identical learning cost to Spanish, but a smaller programme field, a
  weaker graduate job market, Portugal only CONDITIONAL in Phase 1, and no confirmed exam
  centre in Tunisia.

**Spanish beats Italian — not on speed (they are within a month of each other) but on the
three things that matter after he is admitted:**

1. **Job-market access.** Spanish is a genuinely global business language — Spain plus all
   of Latin America. Italian is a single-market language. For someone whose stated ambition
   is an *international* career in digital marketing / CRM / brand, **French + Arabic +
   English + Spanish** is a rare and directly monetisable combination (Maghreb ↔ Europe ↔
   LatAm), whereas French + Arabic + English + Italian largely confines him to Italy, where
   the graduate marketing market is weak and salaries are low.
2. **Sector fit for his music direction.** Madrid and Barcelona are the European hub for
   the Latin music market — the fastest-growing recorded-music region globally — with
   major-label regional operations and the Primavera Sound / Sónar ecosystem. Italy has no
   equivalent. His music-marketing direction is *only* served by Spanish.
3. **Testing logistics from Tunisia.** DELE is sittable in Tunis for **260 TND (≈€78)** with
   a **published 2026 calendar** and a November sitting; SIELE offers a ~3-week-turnaround
   fallback. Italian's IIC Tunis page still shows **2024 dates** and publishes neither fees
   nor course hours — a materially worse operational picture for someone planning to a
   deadline.

**Italy's one genuine counter-argument is money** — ISEE-based public tuition can approach
zero and DSU grants are real. If Agents 3A–3D surface an outstanding, affordable
**Italian-taught** hybrid (music business + digital marketing) that has no Spanish
equivalent, that would justify overturning this. Absent such a find, Spanish wins.

**Note the asymmetry that protects him:** a French speaker at Spanish B2 can reach Italian
B1–B2 in a further ~4–6 months of part-time study, because the two are mutually reinforcing.
Choosing Spanish therefore does not permanently close Italy. **This is exactly why the brief
is right that he must pick one — the transfer effect only works if he reaches real B2 in one
Romance language first. Splitting his hours across Spanish and Italian simultaneously would
produce two shaky B1s, and B1 opens no master's anywhere.**

### 7.2 When must he start — and the honest verdict on 2027

**Start date: NOW — September 2026.** But be clear about what that does and does not buy.

**Scenario A — part-time alongside work (realistic), 10–12 h/week from Sept 2026:**
- B2 competence reached ≈ **Oct–Dec 2027**
- Certified (SIELE, ~3-week results) ≈ **Nov 2027 – Jan 2028**
- **This MISSES autumn 2027 entirely. It comfortably makes autumn 2028.**

**Scenario B — full-time intensive, 25 h/week class from Sept 2026:**
- ~550 hours by **Feb–Mar 2027** → B2
- SIELE in **March 2027**, result by **early April 2027**
- Spanish *preinscripción* for public *máster universitario* typically opens
  **Jan–Mar 2027** with main resolution phases **Apr–Jun 2027**
  (`exact 2027 calendars NOT YET PUBLISHED — 2026 dates are the indication`)
- **This DOES make autumn 2027** — but requires him not to work for six months, and the
  Instituto Cervantes Tunis intensive-course price is `NOT VERIFIED`.

**Therefore the strategy I recommend, and it is a two-track one:**

| Track | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Track 1 — 2027 intake, Tier A only** | Book **IELTS Academic, Sousse** (~700 TND). Apply exclusively to **French-taught** (France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland — where his native French also exempts him from any French test) and **English-taught** programmes. | IELTS **Sept–Oct 2026**; applications **Oct 2026 – Mar 2027** |
| **Track 2 — Spanish, for 2028 and as leverage** | Begin Spanish at Instituto Cervantes Tunis **September 2026** at 10–12 h/week. Target **DELE B2, November 2027** (or SIELE earlier). | Start **Sept 2026**; certified **Nov 2027 – Jan 2028** |

**The hard truth to hand back:** **no new language can reliably unlock autumn 2027 for him.**
Anyone who tells him otherwise is assuming a full-time immersion year he has not said he can
afford. Spanish is worth starting **immediately** — but it is a **2028 instrument, and a
career instrument**, not a 2027 admissions fix. **The 2027 cycle must be won on French and
English.** If he is willing to defer to 2028, Spanish converts from a nice-to-have into a
genuinely decisive advantage: it opens the Spanish public system at €1,000–3,500/year,
roughly a tenth of what the same programme costs him in the UK or at a French private school.
