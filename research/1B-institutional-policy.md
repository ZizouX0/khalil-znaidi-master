# Agent 1B — Institutional Policy Landscape: the 180 ECTS Entry Requirement

**Research date: 2026-08-17.** All pages checked 2026-08-17 unless otherwise stated.
**Student profile:** Tunisian national; Licence in Marketing, FSEG Sousse; GPA ~12–13/20; transcript shows **146 ECTS**; native French, working English; needs a student visa in every country listed. Target intake: **autumn 2027**.

---

## 0. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT FINDING — READ THIS FIRST

**The "146 ECTS" number is probably not the real barrier, and may not be a real number at all.**

The Tunisian LMD *Licence* is **nationally defined** as 6 semesters / 3 years / **180 credits**. This is the national framework, not an institutional choice. (Tunisian Ministry LMD reform, integrated across all HEIs from 2009.)

Nearly every European system, when it meets a **foreign** degree, does **not** re-add the applicant's transcript line by line. It asks a **level question**: *"is this qualification, as defined in its own national system, equivalent to our bachelor's, and does it give access to master's study in its home country?"* That is the operative test in France, Spain, Italy, Poland, Norway, Denmark, the UK and Ireland. Norway's HK-dir states the rule explicitly: *"one year of standard study time in the country of education is equivalent to one year of standard study time (60 ECTS) in Norway."*

Therefore the highest-leverage action available to this student, worth more than any country choice:

> **Obtain from FSEG Sousse / Université de Sousse an official attestation stating that the Licence corresponds to 6 semesters / 3 years / 180 credits in the Tunisian LMD framework, and that it confers access to Mastère study in Tunisia.**

That single document neutralises the 180 ECTS objection in most of the OPEN countries below. It costs essentially nothing and is the cheapest workaround by an enormous margin.

**Two caveats that keep this from being a clean fix:**

1. **`BORDERLINE` — the 146 figure needs explaining before it can be dismissed.** Possibilities: (a) the transcript reports Tunisian local *coefficients*/credits rather than ECTS; (b) some modules are recorded as validated by compensation and not credited; (c) the degree is genuinely incomplete. These have very different consequences. **This must be resolved with the FSEG scolarité before any application is filed.** If (c) is true, most of this report's OPEN verdicts collapse.
2. **The Netherlands has a completely separate and worse problem** that the attestation does *not* fix — see §NL below. This is the one country where the brief's assumption of strictness is not merely correct but understated.

**Which Licence is it?** `NOT VERIFIED — check the diploma`. Tunisia has two: **Licence Fondamentale** (academic) and **Licence Appliquée** (professional). FSEG faculties offer both in gestion/marketing. Which one this is **changes the Dutch verdict completely** and affects Ireland and Finland. Confirm from the diploma wording.

## 2. PER-COUNTRY DETAIL

### 🇬🇧 UNITED KINGDOM — **OPEN** (most permissive in the study)

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **No.** The UK does not operate the ECTS system domestically. UK degrees are measured in **CATS credits** (a taught master's = 180 CATS = 90 ECTS). Entry to a taught master's is assessed on **possession of a recognised bachelor's degree plus its classification** (typically 2:1, often 2:2), not on a credit total. The brief's constraint effectively **does not apply here**.
2. **Years of study?** Yes — "a bachelor's degree from a recognised university equivalent to UK undergraduate standard"; 3- and 4-year degrees both normal.
3. **Recognition body:** **UK ENIC**. Issues a *Statement of Comparability*, **£49.50 + VAT (£59.40)** plus delivery. Note ENIC's own caveat: the statement "reflects **national standards** rather than individual achievement" — i.e. it compares the *Tunisian Licence as a national qualification*, which further insulates the student from the transcript-count problem. Turnaround typically ~15 working days standard.
4. **Institution types:** No FH/university split. In practice **post-1992 universities** and those with large international recruitment operations are markedly more flexible on entry than Russell Group. Many run **pre-master's / Graduate Diploma** routes.
5. **Verdict: OPEN.** The credit constraint is a non-issue. The binding constraints in the UK are instead **cost** (fees typically £16–25k, plus IHS surcharge and maintenance funds) and the **GPA ~12–13/20 → likely a 2:2-equivalent**, which restricts the tier of institution. `BORDERLINE` on classification mapping — UK ENIC or the individual university will decide whether 12–13/20 reads as 2:1 or 2:2.

### 🇫🇷 FRANCE — **OPEN**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** Stated as the norm ("open to students who have validated 180 ECTS credits, equivalent to **Bac+3**"), but this is **institutional discretion via a commission pédagogique**, not a rigid national bar for foreign applicants. Explicitly, diplomas "considered equivalent include **foreign diplomas equivalent to the French diplomas** required for master's admission."
2. **Years of study?** **Yes — decisively.** French practice is framed in **"Bac+3"** (three years past baccalaureate), a *duration* concept, not a credit count. This is the single most favourable framing for this student, and Tunisia's system is a direct descendant of the French one.
3. **Recognition body:** France has **no compulsory credential-conversion step** for university admission. **ENIC-NARIC France** issues an *attestation de comparabilité* (optional, ~€70) but universities are not bound by it. The operative gate is instead **Campus France Tunisie / "Études en France"** — mandatory for Tunisian residents. Fee **220 DT**, includes a compulsory interview on motivation and project coherence. Up to **7 choices**.
4. **Institution types:** **This is where France is strongest.** Beyond public universities:
   - **Écoles de commerce** via **admissions parallèles / AST2** (Tremplin 2, Passerelle) — explicitly open to "students holding a **licence**, a recognised bachelor, or a first year of master's." Admission is *sur dossier* + exam/interview. Marketing is core territory for these schools. Schools reachable via Tremplin 2 include EM Strasbourg, KEDGE, Montpellier BS, NEOMA, Rennes SB.
   - `NOT VERIFIED — check directly`: whether individual écoles apply a hard 180 ECTS filter on the dossier. Their published criteria say "**selon le niveau académique validé (ECTS)**", so a school *could* raise it. Needs an email per school. Check at e.g. https://www.bsb-education.com/fr/conseils/admission-parallele-ecole-commerce
5. **Verdict: OPEN.** Best combination of linguistic fit (native French), cultural/administrative familiarity with Tunisian diplomas, year-based framing, and a large private sector with genuinely flexible entry. **Caveat:** French *public* universities via "Mon Master" are highly competitive and a 12–13/20 is mid-range; the écoles de commerce route is the more realistic one but costs €12–20k/yr.
   - ⚠️ **Campus France publishes a list of "admissions non recevables"** — admission types it will not process. `NOT VERIFIED — must check` at https://www.tunisie.campusfrance.org/4-types-d-admissions-non-recevables-par-campus-france-tunisie before committing to any private school. This could silently invalidate an otherwise good offer.

### 🇵🇱 POLAND — **OPEN**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** Institutional, and **overridden by a stronger legal principle**. NAWA: *"If your degree gives access to the next level of higher education... in the country of issue, in Poland it also gives the right to access education at the corresponding level (second-cycle studies)."* The Tunisian Licence **does** give access to Mastère in Tunisia. This is the cleanest legal argument available anywhere in this report.
2. **Years of study?** Yes, via the access-rights test rather than a duration test.
3. **Recognition body:** **NAWA**. Key condition: the issuing institution must have been an **accredited HEI** at the date of issue — FSEG Sousse, as a public university faculty, satisfies this. Contact: dyplom@nawa.gov.pl.
4. **Institution types:** Large private/vocational sector (Kozminski, Lazarski, WSB group) actively recruiting internationally with flexible entry.
5. **Verdict: OPEN.** Also among the cheapest (fees €2–5k, low living costs). Main downsides: English-taught master's quality varies widely; language barrier outside class; less prestigious for a marketing career.

### 🇪🇸 SPAIN — **OPEN**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** The formal rule states "minimum duration of **3 complete academic years and 180 ECTS or 1800 teaching hours**." **But** there is a dedicated statutory route: holders of non-EHEA degrees may access a máster **without homologación**, where **the university itself verifies the level of training**, provided the degree grants access to postgraduate study in the issuing country.
2. **Years of study?** **Yes** — "3 complete academic years" is the primary phrasing, with the ECTS figure as a gloss.
3. **Recognition body:** Ministerio de Ciencia, Innovación y Universidades. Two distinct things — **homologación** (slow, months–years, not needed here) vs **declaración de equivalencia** vs **acceso sin homologación** (the fast route, decided by the university itself). Requires a certificate from the Tunisian university/ministry confirming the degree grants access to master's study — **the same attestation recommended in §0**. Documents need apostille (Tunisia is a Hague Convention party).
4. **Institution types:** Public universities apply the statutory route; **private universities** (Universidad Europea, ESIC, Nebrija, IE, EAE) are notably more flexible and market explicitly to non-homologated foreign degree holders.
5. **Verdict: OPEN.** Strong marketing-master's market. Moderate fees at public universities (€1.5–4k). Language: many programmes in Spanish (learnable from French), some in English.

### 🇮🇹 ITALY — **OPEN**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** Stated as 180 CFU, but access to a *laurea magistrale* requires "possession of a three-year Laurea **or another foreign study title recognised as suitable**" — an explicit suitability judgement, made by the university.
2. **Years of study?** **Yes — "almeno tre anni"** is the standard phrasing.
3. **Recognition body:** **CIMEA** (Italian ENIC-NARIC). *Statement of Comparability* **€150**; *Statement of Verification* **€65**. Some universities still demand the older **Dichiarazione di Valore** from the Italian consulate in Tunis (slower, free-ish, consulate-dependent). Increasingly CIMEA is accepted in its place.
4. **Institution types:** Moderate flexibility difference. Private (Bocconi, LUISS, IULM) are more selective, not more flexible.
5. **Verdict: OPEN.** Very low public fees (often €0–3k with income-based *ISEE* reductions, and Tunisia-issued income documentation is accepted). Regional **DSU scholarships** are generous. Good marketing/fashion-marketing programmes. Main friction: bureaucratic slowness, and **pre-enrolment via Universitaly with consulate involvement** adds months.

### 🇳🇴 NORWAY — **OPEN / CONDITIONAL** (brief's "Nordics are strict" assumption is **WRONG** here)

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** Nominally yes (Norwegian bachelor = 180 ECTS), **but the conversion rule is explicitly year-based and generous.**
2. **Years of study?** **YES — the most explicit year-equivalence rule found anywhere.** HK-dir guidelines: *"one year of standard study time in the country of education is equivalent to one year of standard study time (60 ECTS) in Norway."* Recognition is given "in whole and half academic years," **not** by recounting individual course credits. Requirements for bachelor-equivalence: (a) minimum **3 years** recognised as higher education, (b) a **completed degree**, (c) "should normally **give access to second cycle studies** in the country of origin." The Tunisian Licence meets all three on its face.
3. **Recognition body:** **HK-dir** (Directorate for Higher Education and Skills; absorbed NOKUT's foreign-education functions). Also maintains the **GSU list** — but note GSU governs *bachelor* entry, not master's entry, so it is largely irrelevant here.
4. **Institution types:** Universities and *høgskoler*; both operate under the same national recognition framework.
5. **Verdict: OPEN on the credit question — CONDITIONAL overall.** The 180 ECTS problem is genuinely solved by Norwegian rules. **The binding constraints are elsewhere:** (a) **Norway introduced tuition fees for non-EEA students in 2023** (roughly NOK 130k–400k/yr depending on programme) — the "free Norway" assumption is obsolete; (b) very high living costs and a demanding financial-proof requirement for the visa; (c) few marketing master's taught in English. `NOT VERIFIED — confirm current fee levels and any Tunisia-specific GSU note` at https://hkdir.no/en/foreign-education

### 🇮🇪 IRELAND — **CONDITIONAL**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** No explicit ECTS bar, but the **NFQ level** matters: master's (Level 9) entry normally wants a **Level 8 Honours Bachelor's**. Ireland maps Level 7 ≈ ordinary/180 ECTS and Level 8 ≈ honours/240 ECTS — so a 3-year 180 ECTS degree can land at **Level 7**, one level short.
2. **Years of study?** Yes, but combined with an honours/classification test. UCD requires "Honours Bachelor's Degree, NFQ Level 8 or equivalent, with at least **Second Class Honours Grade 1 (2H1)**."
3. **Recognition body:** **NARIC Ireland**, hosted by **QQI**. Free online comparability database (2,000+ qualifications, 170+ countries), plus formal comparability statements. `NOT VERIFIED — search the Tunisian Licence directly` at https://qsearch.qqi.ie/webpart/search?searchtype=recognitions — **this is a free, fast, high-value check the student should do immediately.**
4. **Institution types:** **Yes, a real difference.** The **Technological Universities** (TU Dublin, ATU, MTU, SETU) and **DCU / Maynooth** are materially more flexible on 3-year international degrees than **Trinity / UCD**. Several run explicit pathways for 3-year bachelor holders.
5. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** Admissible at the TU/DCU/Maynooth tier; likely closed at TCD/UCD given both the Level 7/8 gap **and** the GPA. English-speaking is an advantage; fees €13–20k; strong post-study work visa (2 years at Level 9).

### 🇵🇹 PORTUGAL — **CONDITIONAL**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** Not as a hard national bar. Access to *mestrado* is "carried out through **competitions organised by each Higher Education Institution**," which set their own requirement for prior training to be recognised.
2. **Years of study?** Yes — recognition turns on whether "level, goals and nature are identical to the Portuguese degree of *licenciado*."
3. **Recognition body:** **DGES**, under Decree-Law 66/2018 and Order 33/2019. Three recognition types (automatic / level / specific). ⚠️ **Practical blocker: the DGES RecOn platform has been inoperative since 19 February 2026 and recognition certificates cannot currently be issued.** For an autumn 2027 intake this may well be resolved, but it must be re-checked. `NOT VERIFIED for 2027` — recheck https://www.dges.gov.pt/pt/pagina/reconhecimento
4. **Institution types:** **Politécnicos** are more applied and generally more flexible than the classical universities (Lisboa, Porto, Coimbra, Nova).
5. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL** — good on cost and lifestyle, but gated on a recognition system that is currently broken. Also relatively few English-taught marketing master's outside Nova/Católica (which are expensive and selective).

### 🇨🇿 CZECHIA — **CONDITIONAL**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** Not a credit rule — the gate is **nostrification** (formal recognition of the foreign diploma). Non-EU applicants must generally hold a nostrificated bachelor diploma before enrolling in a master's.
2. **Years of study?** Yes, assessed as programme comparability during nostrification.
3. **Recognition body:** Nostrification is done by a **public university rector's office** that runs a comparable programme (not a central agency). Statutory processing period **30 days** (in practice longer). **Alternative:** universities holding **institutional accreditation** may recognise the qualification **inside the admission procedure** — faster, but valid only at that one university. This alternative is the practical route.
4. **Institution types:** Substantial private sector (Metropolitan University Prague, VŠEM, Anglo-American University) that handles nostrification routinely and is more flexible.
5. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** Very affordable; English-taught programmes plentiful. Risk is administrative: nostrification can be refused or can demand supplementary exams, and the timeline is unpredictable.

### 🇦🇹 AUSTRIA — **CONDITIONAL**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **Yes** — admission requires "a professionally relevant Bachelor's degree with at least **180 ECTS**, or another professionally relevant degree of at least the same academic level from an accredited domestic **or foreign** post-secondary institution."
2. **Years of study?** Partly — equivalence ("Gleichwertigkeit") is the test, which considers both duration and content.
3. **Recognition body:** **ENIC NARIC Austria**. Universities make the admission decision themselves.
4. **Institution types:** **FH (Fachhochschulen)** are somewhat more flexible in practice and more oriented to applied business/marketing, but apply the same statutory 180 ECTS baseline.
5. **Workaround built into the law:** **Ergänzungsprüfungen** — supplementary examinations of **up to 30 ECTS** to compensate for substantial subject-specific differences. Can be completed **during** the first year (by end of second semester) rather than before entry. **If the deficit is judged >30 ECTS the application is rejected outright.** A 146-vs-180 gap is 34 ECTS — **dangerously just over the line.** This makes resolving the §0 attestation question critical for Austria specifically.
6. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL, and finely balanced.** Low fees (~€750/semester for non-EU at public universities). German-language requirement for many programmes is a real obstacle.

### 🇧🇪 BELGIUM — **CONDITIONAL**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **Yes, and it is written explicitly into access rules — this is one of the more literal jurisdictions.** French-speaking Belgium: you must hold "a Bachelor's degree (**1st cycle of 180 ECTS credits**) in an area of study that is similar or identical." ULiège's non-EU master access page is even more explicit: a first-cycle degree from a recognised institution "**(at the end of 3 years and with at least 180 credits)**". Both a duration test *and* a credit test.
2. **Years of study?** Both are applied together, so the years test alone does not rescue the applicant.
3. **Recognition body:**
   - **Flanders: NARIC-Vlaanderen.** Offers **level recognition** ("completion of a higher education level... without recognising a specific diploma"). Fees: **€180** for bachelor/master-level recognition, **€90** for a "higher education corresponding level statement". Assessors "compare requirements defined by Belgian law including **minimum year, course and credit burdens**" — so the credit count *is* explicitly in scope here.
   - **French Community:** recognition handled via the Ministry / ARES; for study purposes the institution decides.
   - Note for Flanders: where a degree receives only **partial (level) recognition**, "an additional permission of the concerned faculty is mandatory" — so a level statement alone is not sufficient.
4. **Institution types:** **Hautes écoles** (FR) / **hogescholen** (NL) are more flexible than universities, and the Flemish system has formal **schakelprogramma / voorbereidingsprogramma** bridging routes (see §3.1).
5. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** Genuinely attractive for a francophone (Brussels, Liège, Louvain; low fees ~€835/yr for the "droit d'inscription", though non-EU "droits majorés" can reach €4k+). But the 180 ECTS language is unusually literal, so **this country's outcome depends more than most on the §0 attestation.** ⚠️ Also note the separate hurdle of **non-EU quota restrictions** and the *"attestation d'équivalence"* requirements. `BORDERLINE — email admissions at ULB/ULiège/UCLouvain with the transcript before applying.`

### 🇩🇰 DENMARK — **CONDITIONAL** (brief's assumption broadly correct)

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **Yes** — "a Bachelor's degree relevant to your chosen subject matter and worth the same number of credits as a Danish degree (**180 ECTS**)."
2. **Years of study?** Partly — the national assessment states level and, where possible, field; institutions then judge academic content.
3. **Recognition body:** **Danish Agency for Higher Education and Science (Uddannelses- og Forskningsstyrelsen)**. Assessment is **free of charge**, takes **1–2 months**. **Crucially: "the level of education indicated by the assessment is BINDING on the educational institution when it decides whether you satisfy the general entry requirements."** This is a double-edged sword — a favourable assessment forces the university's hand; an unfavourable one is fatal and cannot be argued around. Contact: vur@ufm.dk.
4. **Institution types:** **Professionshøjskoler** and business academies are more applied and more flexible than the universities (KU, AU, CBS, DTU).
5. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** **Recommended action: request the free Danish assessment early** — it is free, fast, and its binding nature makes it a decisive, low-cost probe of whether the Tunisian Licence reads as a full bachelor. Even if the student doesn't study in Denmark, the result is diagnostically valuable. Downsides: non-EU fees €10–17k/yr, very high living costs, CBS extremely competitive.

### 🇸🇪 SWEDEN — **CONDITIONAL** (brief's assumption broadly correct)

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **Yes** — general entry requires "a Bachelor's degree equivalent to a Swedish **kandidatexamen (180 ECTS)** from an internationally recognised university," or "a professional degree of at least 180 credits **or an equivalent foreign degree**."
2. **Years of study?** Partly — "an equivalent foreign degree" leaves room, and a three-year foreign bachelor meeting 180 ECTS is "generally accepted."
3. **Recognition body:** Assessment is **centralised inside the application process at universityadmissions.se** rather than requiring a separate credential body (UHR handles it). One application, one assessment, up to 4 programme choices. This centralisation is a **risk**: a single adverse assessment closes the entire country at once, with no institution-by-institution appeal.
4. **Institution types:** Sweden has **no separate university-of-applied-sciences sector**, so there is no more-flexible tier to fall back on. This makes Sweden structurally less forgiving than Germany or the Netherlands despite similar nominal rules.
5. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** Non-EU fees SEK 90k–160k/yr. Application fee SEK 900. Deadline for autumn 2027 intake will be **mid-January 2027** — the earliest hard deadline of any country here, so this needs to be decided early.

### 🇫🇮 FINLAND — **CONDITIONAL** (with a counter-intuitive twist)

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **Yes** — "a recognised Bachelor's degree of **at least 180 ECTS** in a relevant field."
2. **Years of study?** Partly.
3. **Recognition body:** Finnish National Agency for Education (**EDUFI/OPH**); universities assess eligibility directly for admission purposes.
4. **Institution types — VERIFIED, AND THE ASSUMPTION IS REVERSED:** **Finnish UAS master's are HARDER to enter, not easier.** UAS (*ammattikorkeakoulu*) master's degrees require **at least 2 years of relevant post-degree work experience** (reduced from 3 years in 2019) — a requirement research universities do **not** impose. Additionally Finnish UAS *bachelor's* degrees are 210–270 ECTS, so a UAS master's is built on a longer undergraduate base, further disadvantaging a 180-ECTS-or-less applicant.
   > **This directly contradicts the brief's hypothesis that applied-sciences institutions are more flexible. In Finland the opposite is true.**
5. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL**, and research universities (Aalto, Turku, Tampere, Jyväskylä) are the better target, not the UAS sector. Non-EU fees €8–18k/yr, though scholarships covering 50–100% are common and genuinely accessible.

### 🇱🇺 LUXEMBOURG — **CONDITIONAL**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **Yes** — "a bachelor's degree or equivalent of **at least 180 ECTS** in a related field."
2. **Years of study?** No — ECTS-framed.
3. **Recognition body:** Non-EU/EEA/Swiss degree holders must apply to have the diploma **registered in the *register of qualifications* at the Ministry of Research and Higher Education**. Helpfully, this "is **not required when submitting your application**... it can only be requested **after you have been accepted**." So the university's academic decision comes first and the ministerial step second — a favourable sequencing that lets the student test admissibility before incurring the bureaucratic cost.
4. **Institution types:** Only one university; no applied-sciences alternative.
5. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** Trilingual (FR/DE/EN) environment suits a francophone well, and fees are low (€400–800/semester for most master's). But the institution is small with a narrow programme range — `NOT VERIFIED` whether a suitable marketing master's exists at all. Very high living costs. Check https://www.uni.lu/en/admissions/admission-criteria/

### 🇩🇪 GERMANY — **CONDITIONAL (hard)** — brief's assumption **CONFIRMED**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **Yes, and it is the most literal regime of the major destinations.** The standard formulation requires a completed undergraduate degree "comprising **at least 6 semesters of theoretical study**... and **180 ECTS credits**." This is a **KMK-derived national framework**, applied via uni-assist, not merely institutional discretion — although each university sets its own additional requirements on top.
2. **Years of study?** **Partly, and this is the useful crack.** The "at least **6 semesters**" limb is a *duration* test that the Tunisian Licence satisfies unambiguously. The problem is that German rules require **both** limbs, joined by "and", not "or".
3. **Recognition body:** **anabin** (KMK database) + **ZAB** (Zentralstelle für ausländisches Bildungswesen). Mechanism:
   - Institutions are graded **H+** (equivalent to a German Hochschule), **H−** (not equivalent), **H+/−** (case-by-case).
   - `NOT VERIFIED — must check` whether **Université de Sousse / FSEG Sousse is listed H+** at https://anabin.kmk.org — **this is a free check and a hard gate. If the institution is H− the entire country closes regardless of credits.** Do this first.
   - If unlisted or H+/−: apply for a **ZAB *Zeugnisbewertung* (Statement of Comparability)** — **€208**, standard processing **3 months** from receipt of full documents and fee (faster tracks exist but are for Blue Card / skilled-worker routes, not students). A reissued copy is €104.
   - **uni-assist** performs the per-application academic evaluation, charging ~€75 for the first application and ~€30 for each additional one. A **VPD** (Vorprüfungsdokumentation) is required by some universities.
4. **Institution types — VERIFIED, AND THE ASSUMPTION IS WRONG:** **Fachhochschulen are NOT more flexible on this rule.** HM Hochschule München (a large, internationally-oriented FH) requires exactly the same: "a completed degree... with a minimum of **6 theoretical semesters**, at least with the converted grade **'good'**, and **180 ECTS points**." Several FH additionally require **210 ECTS**, making them *stricter* than universities, because German FH bachelor's are often 7 semesters. Where FH do differ is in accepting **work experience** as a partial substitute and in offering paid bridging modules — not in relaxing the 180 threshold.
   > **The brief's "applied sciences are more flexible" hypothesis fails in Germany as it does in Finland.**
5. **Workarounds that do exist in Germany:**
   - **Auflagen (conditional enrolment):** if **up to 30 ECTS** of study components are missing, enrolment under conditions is possible, to be completed during the first year. **Above 30 ECTS this is explicitly not possible** because the degree "differs substantially." A 34-ECTS gap sits just outside — again making §0 decisive.
   - **Brückenkurse** (bridge courses) offered by some institutions before/at the start of the master's. At some private institutions (e.g. Euro-FH) a bridge course can substitute for additional credits or work experience.
   - **Studienkolleg does NOT apply.** **VERIFIED and the brief's suspicion is correct:** DAAD and uni-assist both state Studienkolleg is for **bachelor** entry only — for students whose *school-leaving* qualification doesn't permit direct entry. For master's applicants the analogue is a **Propädeutikum**, which is optional preparatory/language work and **does not confer credits or fix an ECTS deficit.**
   - A further note: the "grade at least **2.5 / 'good'**" requirement appears widely. **A Tunisian 12–13/20 typically converts (Bavarian formula) to roughly 2.7–3.1 — i.e. likely BELOW the 2.5 cut-off at selective programmes.** For this student the **GPA may be a harder blocker in Germany than the credits.**
6. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL (hard).** Germany combines (a) a literal dual duration+credit rule, (b) an FH sector that is no more forgiving, (c) a grade threshold this student may miss, and (d) a €208 + 3-month ZAB step. **Attractive on cost (no/low tuition) but the entry gate is the tightest of the large countries.** Not a first-choice target unless the §0 attestation and an H+ anabin listing both come back clean.

### 🇨🇭 SWITZERLAND — **CLOSED (practically)**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** **Yes, and stated in the most explicit terms found in this study.** HES-SO: applicants must hold a degree "obtained in a university with a programme recognised by the HES-SO at the end of a course lasting **at least 3 years (at least 180 ECTS credits)**." Both limbs, explicitly. Swiss cantonal universities (UZH, UNIGE, UNIL) are notoriously restrictive with non-European qualifications and frequently refuse them outright regardless of credits.
2. **Years of study?** Applied jointly with the credit test, so it does not rescue the applicant.
3. **Recognition body:** **swissuniversities** issues recommendations, but **each university decides autonomously** and there is no binding national mechanism compelling acceptance. Several Swiss universities publish blanket lists of countries whose bachelor's they do not accept for direct master's entry.
4. **Institution types — assumption WRONG again:** HES-SO (the applied-sciences sector) is **not** more flexible. It applies the same 180 ECTS rule **and adds a requirement**: "the type of Bachelor's degree is examined to define whether it is vocational or university training. In the latter case, **additional professional experience will be required**." So an academic *Licence Fondamentale* holder would face a work-experience requirement on top.
5. **Verdict: CLOSED (practically).** Even setting the credit rule aside, Switzerland imposes among the highest financial-proof thresholds for a student visa (typically CHF 21,000+/yr proven), high tuition for non-EU at some institutions, and cantonal quotas. **Recommend deprioritising entirely** — the effort-to-probability ratio is the worst of the eighteen.

### 🇳🇱 NETHERLANDS — **CLOSED** (if Licence Fondamentale) — brief's assumption **CONFIRMED AND UNDERSTATED**

**This is the most important negative finding in the report, and it is not about credits at all.**

1. **Strict 180 ECTS?** The 180 ECTS framing is **not the operative barrier**. The barrier is that **Nuffic rates the Tunisian Licence Fondamentale as equivalent to only 2 YEARS OF WO** — i.e. **below** a completed Dutch bachelor's, by a full year, as a matter of published national benchmark.

   **Nuffic's official Tunisia diploma comparison table (checked 2026-08-17, https://www.nuffic.nl/en/education-systems/tunisia):**

   | Tunisian Diploma | Dutch Equivalent |
   |---|---|
   | Brevet de Technicien Professionnel | MBO diploma level 3 or 4 |
   | Diplôme du Baccalauréat | At least a HAVO diploma |
   | Brevet de Technicien Supérieur | Associate degree or 2 years of HBO |
   | **Licence Fondamentale** | **2 years of WO** ❌ |
   | **Licence Appliquée** | **3 years of HBO** ✅ |
   | Diplôme National d'Ingénieur | 1-year WO master's degree |
   | Mastère Professionnel | HBO master's degree |
   | Mastère de Recherche | 1-year WO master's degree |

   > **Everything for the Netherlands turns on which Licence this is.**
   > - **Licence Appliquée → "3 years of HBO"** = a full HBO bachelor equivalent. This makes the student **admissible to an HBO (hogeschool) master's directly**, and eligible for a WO pre-master's. Verdict would upgrade to **CONDITIONAL**.
   > - **Licence Fondamentale → "2 years of WO"** = a one-year shortfall against *any* Dutch bachelor. No pre-master fixes this, because Dutch rules cap a pre-master at **60 EC**, and *"if you have a greater deficiency, you are required to follow a second Bachelor's programme."* A one-year level shortfall plus a subject/level gap will generally exceed that cap. Verdict: **CLOSED.**

2. **Years of study?** Yes — Nuffic works entirely in years-of-Dutch-education equivalence, not credits. Unfortunately here that framing works *against* the student.
3. **Recognition body:** **Nuffic** (for study purposes) / **IDW** (for employment). Nuffic's own note: "you can only use the MBO comparison if you want to check whether a diploma can give access to Dutch higher education." Institutions may deviate from Nuffic in principle but in practice follow it closely.
4. **Institution types:** **Hogescholen (HBO) are genuinely more flexible** and are the correct target *if* the degree is a Licence Appliquée. But see the visa trap below, which affects both sectors.
5. **⚠️ THE PRE-MASTER VISA TRAP — a decisive practical finding:**
   - **UvA (verified, quoted):** *"The UvA is **not allowed to apply for a residence permit on your behalf solely for a pre-master's programme**."* A permit is possible **only** if the pre-master "grants **direct admission** to the accompanying master's programme **and** if you start the master's immediately after." So the pre-master must be pre-linked to a guaranteed master's place — not a general bridging year.
   - **Utrecht University (verified):** pre-master's is *"only available for students with a **Dutch educational background** who do not need housing, and **who do not have or require a residence permit for study purposes**."* → **Utrecht's pre-master is entirely closed to this student.**
   - **Erasmus/EUR and Radboud (verified):** more permissive — the residence permit "will cover the duration of **both your pre-master's as well as your master's**." So the rule is **institution-specific, not national.**
   - **Conclusion:** the Dutch pre-master, widely recommended online as *the* fix for credit gaps, is **partly or wholly unavailable to visa-needing students depending on the institution.** Any Dutch plan must verify this per university before applying. Also note: pre-master's fees are **non-refundable in full** if abandoned, and pre-master's generally do **not** attract Dutch student finance.
6. **Verdict: CLOSED** on the base case (Licence Fondamentale). **CONDITIONAL** if the diploma turns out to be a Licence Appliquée, in which case target **HBO master's** at institutions that sponsor pre-master residence permits (EUR, Radboud — **not** Utrecht).

### 3.3 UK — does the ECTS constraint apply? **VERIFIED: essentially NO**

- The UK does not use ECTS domestically; it uses **CATS credits** (bachelor ≈ 360 CATS; taught master's = 180 CATS = 90 ECTS).
- Entry is assessed on **holding a recognised bachelor's degree + its classification** (2:1 or 2:2), not on a credit total.
- **UK ENIC explicitly benchmarks national standards, not individual achievement** — so the assessment addresses "what is a Tunisian Licence worth", not "how many credits does this transcript show".
- **Verdict: the 180 ECTS constraint does not bite in the UK.** The real UK constraints are **cost** (fees £16–25k + IHS + maintenance proof) and **classification** (12–13/20 likely maps to 2:2, restricting institution tier).
- **This makes the UK the single most open jurisdiction on the specific question this research was commissioned to answer** — though possibly not the best on cost.

### 3.4 Private business schools & applied-sciences institutions — **the assumption is only half right**

**Where applied-sciences institutions ARE more flexible:** Ireland (TUs vs TCD/UCD), Czechia (private HEIs), Poland (private HEIs), Spain (private universities), Portugal (politécnicos), Belgium (hautes écoles/hogescholen), Netherlands (HBO — if Licence Appliquée), UK (post-92 universities).

**Where they are NOT — verified counter-examples:**
- 🇩🇪 **Germany:** FH apply the identical 6-semester + 180 ECTS rule; many require **210 ECTS**, making them *stricter*.
- 🇫🇮 **Finland:** UAS master's require **2 years' work experience** that research universities don't; UAS bachelor's are 210–270 ECTS.
- 🇨🇭 **Switzerland:** HES-SO applies the same 180 ECTS rule **and adds** a professional-experience requirement for academically-trained applicants.

**The genuinely flexible private route — French écoles de commerce:** *admissions parallèles / AST2* are explicitly designed for **Bac+3** entrants holding "a licence, a recognised bachelor, or a first year of master's", admit **sur dossier** plus exam/interview, and place entrants directly into the **master cycle** of the Programme Grande École. Marketing is their core subject. **For a francophone marketing graduate this is the best-fitting flexible route in Europe.** Cost €12–20k/yr. ⚠️ But verify Campus France Tunisie will process the admission type (§France).

### 3.5 Completing missing credits in Tunisia first

**Options, roughly in order of merit:**

1. **Obtain the attestation instead (§0).** ✅ **Do this first, always.** If the Licence is already nationally 180 credits, there is nothing to complete and every other option in this section is wasted money and a wasted year.
2. **Enrol in a Tunisian *Mastère* (M1) and use it as evidence.** Completing even one year of Tunisian master's study proves definitively that the Licence grants access to second-cycle study at home — which is exactly the test used by Poland, Norway, Spain and the Lisbon-principle countries. It also adds credits. **Cost: very low (public Tunisian fees). Time: 1 year. Realism: HIGH.** This is the strongest "repair in Tunisia" play, because it converts the weakness into positive evidence rather than merely patching a number.
3. **Re-enrol at FSEG to validate specific outstanding modules.** Only sensible if the 146 figure reflects genuinely unvalidated modules (case (c) in §0). Cost low; time 1 semester–1 year; **but administratively awkward and only worth it if the deficit is real.**
4. **A European "top-up" final year.** Available mainly in the **UK** and at some Dutch/Belgian applied institutions (e.g. direct entry to a final bachelor phase for applicants with up to ~160 European credits). Yields a *European* bachelor, which eliminates the problem permanently and definitively. **Cost: €10–18k + a year. Realism: medium** — expensive, but it converts a contested credential into an uncontested EU one, which also helps for later employment and visa purposes.
5. **A second full bachelor's.** ❌ Not worth it — 3 years and full cost to solve a problem that an attestation may solve for free.

**Bottom line on this workaround:** the expected value is dominated by option 1, with option 2 as the strong fallback. **Do not spend money completing credits until the §0 ambiguity is resolved** — it may be a phantom deficit.
