# CONTRADICTION RESOLVED — Ireland does NOT bar marketing roles from work permits

**Agents in conflict:** 2B (UK/Ireland) recommended Irish marketing master's as
the best-value finds in its scope. 3I (job market) then claimed those degrees
lead to an unsponsorable job, calling it "one correction the brief did not
anticipate."

**3I's claim, verbatim:**
> *"Ireland's official Ineligible Categories list (updated 13 May 2026) bars
> **SOC 354 'Sales, Marketing and Related Associate Professionals'** — a Dublin
> digital-marketing offer cannot become a permit at any salary."*

**Verdict: 3I is WRONG. 2B stands.** Per the brief's anti-miss rule, flagged
rather than averaged — and here the flag resolves cleanly on the primary source.

## What the official list actually says

Source, checked **2026-08-17**:
https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/employment-permit-eligibility/ineligible-categories-of-employment/

The page states: *"This list is effective from 13 May 2026, when SI 213 of 2026
came into effect."* — so 3I had the right document and the right date.

But the list is structured as **SOC-3 category headers** with the **specific
SOC-4 occupations** that are actually ineligible listed against them. Under
category 354 only **two** occupations are barred:

| SOC-4 | Ineligible occupation |
|---|---|
| 3544 | Estate agents and auctioneers |
| 3546 | Conference and exhibition managers and organisers |

**Marketing associate professionals are not on the list.** 3I read the category
header as if the whole category were excluded. It is not — the header names the
group, the SOC-4 entries name the exclusions.

## A detail that actively helps this student

Under SOC 7211 (call and contact centre occupations), which *is* ineligible, the
list carves out an exception for:

> *"employment of a person fluent in the official language, apart from English,
> of a state which is not a Member State of the EEA, where the employment is
> supported by an enterprise development agency and is in — ... **a specialist
> online digital marketing and sales role**..."*

A fluent **Arabic** speaker (and French, though France is in the EEA) in a
specialist online digital-marketing role is precisely the profile this carve-out
describes. Ireland's large multilingual shared-services sector in Dublin and Cork
recruits exactly this way. This is a **positive** finding for Ireland, and the
opposite of what 3I concluded.

## What survives from 3I

3I's **central thesis is unharmed** and remains the most valuable finding in the
project: technical job titles clear salary thresholds and occupation filters that
non-technical ones do not, and the music sector is structurally hard to sponsor
(31.6% self-employment; ~4 employees per cultural enterprise; UK median musician
£37,500 against a £41,700 floor). That analysis stands on Eurostat and ONS data.

What must be struck is the specific Ireland claim, and with it the inference that
"generic digital marketing is nearly as unsponsorable as music" **in Ireland**.
The UK half of that claim (SOC 3554 in Table 1a, closed to new applicants since
22 July 2025) was separately sourced and is **not** disturbed by this check —
but it should not be generalised to Ireland, and 3I generalised it.

## Consequences for the synthesis

1. **Ireland moves back up.** 2B's finds — MTU Cork (net €11,000 after an
   automatic €2,500 award) and ATU Sligo — remain live, and Ireland's Stamp 1G
   at **24 months** beats the UK's post-2027 **18 months**.
2. **Do not tell the student Irish marketing roles are unsponsorable.** That
   would have been a materially damaging error in the final report.
3. 3I's "lean technical" recommendation still holds on its own merits — it is
   just no longer supported by the Ireland argument.
